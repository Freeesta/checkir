## molecule

5-BPDi, 1-(2,3-dihydro-1H-inden-5-yl)-2-pyrrolidin-1-ylhexan-1-one (indanyl-α-PHP). Base: C19H27NO, 285.4 g/mol; no CAS number in PubChem, stereochemistry at C2 not specified. Identity checked against PubChem. It is a cathinone, not a cannabinoid. [f:pubchem]

## category

## history

A purchased off-white powder entered the Slovenian National Forensic Laboratory in 2015 and was identified as 5-BPDi by NMR; the compound was structurally characterised in a 2019 paper on synthetic cathinones. [f:nfl-si-5-bpdi] [f:blazewicz2019]

## origin

A synthetic substance, an α-pyrrolidinophenone (pyrovalerone-type cathinone) in which the aromatic ring is an indane. [f:blazewicz2019]

## effects

Synthetic cathinones have stimulant and, in part, hallucinogenic properties. [f:machacek2026]

They amplify catecholamine release, like cocaine and amphetamine-type stimulants. [f:gresnigt2026]

## risks

Synthetic cathinones can cause arrhythmia, ischaemia, myocardial injury and sudden cardiac death. [f:gresnigt2026]

They are associated with acute symptomatic seizures, often with lethal outcomes: in 42 reported cases death occurred in nearly half. [f:machacek2026]

## interactions

Cocaine, amphetamine-type stimulants and synthetic cathinones have sympathomimetic effects through catecholamine excess. [f:gresnigt2026]
