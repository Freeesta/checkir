## category

## history

Brorphine appeared after the DEA announced, in mid-2020, its intent to schedule isotonitazene: isotonitazene declined and brorphine replaced it as a new opioid on the market. [f:krotulski2021b]

Most of the first cases came from Midwestern states of the United States. [f:krotulski2021b]

## origin

A synthetic substance, an opioid with a piperidine benzimidazolone scaffold; at the time of the study it was the only new synthetic opioid with this scaffold. [f:vandeputte2024-brorphine]

## metabolism

In human liver microsomes and in authentic specimens the metabolism of brorphine included N-dealkylation and hydroxylation. [f:krotulski2021b]

## effects

Brorphine is a potent μ-opioid receptor agonist; preclinical studies show strong antinociceptive activity, respiratory depression and abuse potential. [f:marchei2026]

In vitro brorphine was among the most active of five related benzimidazolones at the μ-opioid receptor, and in mice it produced some of the highest levels of antinociception. [f:vandeputte2024-brorphine]

## risks

Brorphine was confirmed in 20 forensic cases; blood concentrations averaged 2.5 ± 3.1 ng/mL (median 1.1, range 0.1-10). [f:krotulski2021b]

Since 2019 brorphine has been implicated in several non-fatal and fatal intoxications, often together with fentanyl or benzodiazepines. [f:marchei2026]

## interactions

In the 20 cases brorphine was found with fentanyl in 100 % and with flualprazolam in 80 %. [f:krotulski2021b]
