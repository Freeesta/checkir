## molecule

Alprazolam, 8-chloro-1-methyl-6-phenyl-4H-[1,2,4]triazolo[4,3-a][1,4]benzodiazepine. Formula C17H13ClN4, 308.8 g/mol. Identity checked on PubChem. [f:pubchem]

Free base: CAS 28981-97-7, melting point 224-230 °C. [f:swgdrug-alprazolam]

Bands labelled in the ATR (diamond) spectrum of the SWGDRUG monograph: 1612, 1539, 1487, 1323, 1298, 1165, 850, 744 and 698 cm-1; values read from the plot labels (±2 cm-1), without assignment to groups. [f:swgdrug-alprazolam]

## category

Chemical class: benzodiazepines. Expected effect: central nervous system depressant (sedative). Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. It belongs to the triazolobenzodiazepines. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

In 1991 alprazolam and diazepam were the two most prescribed benzodiazepine anxiolytics in the United States. [f:juergens1991]

## origin

Synthetic substance: the triazolo ring is incorporated into the benzodiazepine structure. [f:huybrechts1991]

## metabolism

Metabolism is mainly hepatic microsomal oxidation; the main metabolites are alpha-hydroxy- and 4-hydroxy-alprazolam, with lower benzodiazepine receptor affinity and plasma concentrations below 10% of those of the parent drug. [f:greenblatt1993]

After 1 mg by mouth the peak plasma concentration occurs at 0.7-1.8 h and the elimination half-life is 9-16 h. [f:greenblatt1993]

The cytochrome P450 IIIA (CYP3A) subfamily appears to mediate alprazolam metabolism in humans. [f:greenblatt1993b]

## effects

The anxiolytic effect is similar to that of other benzodiazepines; the most common side effect, mild sedation, occurs early in treatment. [f:huybrechts1991]

Drowsiness and sedation are consistent with agonist action at the benzodiazepine receptor and become more frequent as the steady-state plasma concentration rises. [f:greenblatt1993]

## risks

Alprazolam and diazepam are among the most reinforcing benzodiazepines in experimental conditions; alprazolam withdrawal may be more severe and may occur after a shorter period of use. [f:juergens1991]

Physiological dependence on benzodiazepines comes with a withdrawal syndrome of sleep disturbance, irritability, anxiety, tremor, sweating and palpitations; seizures and psychotic reactions are also reported at high doses. Withdrawal appears more severe after high doses or after short-acting benzodiazepines. [f:petursson1994]

## interactions

Concurrent use of an opioid and a benzodiazepine is associated with a higher risk of an emergency room visit or hospital admission for opioid overdose (adjusted odds ratio 2.14, 95% confidence interval 2.05-2.24; privately insured US patients 2001-2013, retrospective study: an association, not proof of causation). [f:sun2017]

Cimetidine, fluoxetine, fluvoxamine and propoxyphene significantly impair alprazolam clearance; propranolol, metronidazole, disulfiram, oral contraceptives and ethanol do not alter it (a pharmacokinetic finding: it does not rule out an additive effect on the central nervous system). [f:greenblatt1993]

## counselling

Ask whether the person also takes opioids or heroin: the UBC guidelines say benzodiazepines can depress breathing and that the risk of respiratory arrest rises with opiates; the combination with opioids is linked to more overdose visits. [f:sun2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask how long and how regularly the person has used it: alprazolam has a short half-life and withdrawal that can be severe; if the person wants to stop, abrupt discontinuation after prolonged use exposes them to withdrawal. [f:juergens1991] [f:petursson1994]
