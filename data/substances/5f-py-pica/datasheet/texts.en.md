## molecule

5F-PY-PICA, [1-(5-fluoropentyl)indol-3-yl]-pyrrolidin-1-ylmethanone. Base: C18H23FN2O, 302.4 g/mol, CAS 2166085-89-6. Identity checked against PubChem; achiral. It is the indole analogue of 5F-PY-PINACA (sheet 5f-py-pinaca), which has an indazole in place of the indole. [f:pubchem] [f:banister2019]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

5F-PY-PICA and 5F-PY-PINACA are pyrrolidinyl 1-(5-fluoropentyl)ind(az)ole-3-carboxamides identified in 2015 as putative synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist new psychoactive substances. [f:banister2019]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a putative synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist (indole-3-carboxamide with a pyrrolidine ring and a fluoropentyl chain). [f:banister2019] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In human liver microsomes, human hepatocytes and rat hepatocytes the main metabolites of 5F-PY-PICA were the ω-carboxylic acid (M4, main in human hepatocytes), the defluorinated ω-hydroxy metabolite (M3, main in microsomes) and the carboxylic acid on the pyrrolidine (M6, main in rat hepatocytes). [f:mardal2018]

M6 is proposed as one of the main urinary targets; the parent compound, M4 and the hydroxy-indole glucuronide (M22) are proposed as targets for bile analysis. [f:mardal2018]

## effects

In receptor assays all 5F-PY-PICA, 5F-PY-PINACA and analogues tested showed minimal affinity for CB1 (pKi below 5), and none produced an effect above 50% of CP55,940 at CB1. [f:banister2019]

5F-PY-PICA and 5F-PY-PINACA did not produce cannabimimetic effects (hypothermia, bradycardia) in mice at doses up to 10 mg/kg; no data on effects in humans were found in the sources used. [f:banister2019]

## doses

In mice no cannabimimetic effect was seen up to 10 mg/kg and no human potency data were found in the sources used, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:banister2019]

## risks

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis (43.45 vs 22.91 days). [f:ricci2026]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
