## molecule

Methoxyacetylfentanyl, 2-methoxy-N-phenyl-N-[1-(2-phenylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]acetamide. Base: C22H28N2O2, 352.5 g/mol, CAS 101345-67-9. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

The US Drug Enforcement Administration reported methoxyacetylfentanyl in seized drug evidence once in the second quarter of 2017 and seven times in the third quarter; in 11 postmortem cases with a quantified result the blood concentration ranged from 0.21 to 39.9 ng/mL. [f:fogarty2018]

In a series of eleven postmortem cases (aged 27 to 41) methoxyacetylfentanyl was found in femoral blood, and it was the cause of death alone or with other drugs in all but one. [f:kronstrand2021]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a fentanyl analogue. [f:armenian2018]

## metabolism

In the urine of those cases 27 metabolites were found, eight of them glucuronides; the main biotransformations were O-demethylation, dealkylation to the nor-metabolite, and mono- and dihydroxylation of the phenethyl moiety. [f:kronstrand2021]

The most abundant metabolites in hydrolysed urine included O-desmethyl-, O-desmethyl-phenethyl-hydroxy-, O-desmethyl-phenethyl-hydroxymethoxy- and nor-methoxyacetylfentanyl. [f:kronstrand2021]

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

Existing literature suggests that methoxyacetylfentanyl is about 3- to 5-fold less potent than fentanyl. [f:kronstrand2021]

## doses

Methoxyacetylfentanyl is reported to be about 3- to 5-fold less potent than fentanyl, but no dose is safe to suggest. [f:kronstrand2021]

## risks

In poisonings with fentanyl analogues larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

As soon as a compound is regulated new ones appear, which makes detection difficult; some illicit pills with these compounds look like known opioids or benzodiazepines. [f:armenian2018]

In one of three polydrug fatalities, methoxyacetylfentanyl (266 ng/mL in femoral blood) was found with furanylfentanyl, 4-ANPP and alprazolam; the authors note that novel synthetic opioids are often consumed with other opioids and benzodiazepines, which often exert synergistic effects. [f:giorgetti2024]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
