## risks

Xylazine toxicity is associated with significant hypotension and with respiratory and central nervous system depression; chronic use may lead to physiological dependence, withdrawal symptoms and severe skin ulcerations. [f:marshall2025]

A systematic review found no established toxic dose in humans (reported doses ranged from 40 to 4300 mg) and no antidote or evidence-based treatment recommendations. [f:owusu-antwi2025]

Xylazine-associated wounds appeared at and away from injection sites as dark blisters that evolved into dry eschar and sometimes deeper ulcers; superinfection was reported in 8 of the 10 studies reviewed. [f:vaile2026]

## interactions

The effects of xylazine on blood pressure, breathing and the central nervous system may be synergistic with those of opioids, which raises the risk of fatal overdose. [f:marshall2025]

## counselling

Explain that naloxone does not reverse the xylazine part of an intoxication: standard doses are ineffective against its toxicity and no approved antidote exists; in a case series of two accidental injections no reversal was seen. [f:marshall2025] [f:choon2023]

Ask whether the person has skin wounds, also away from the injection sites: reviews describe dark blisters evolving into eschar and ulcers, often with superinfection. [f:vaile2026]
