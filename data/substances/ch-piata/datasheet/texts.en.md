## molecule

CH-PIATA (CH-PIACA, CHX-PIATA, CHX-PIACA), N-cyclohexyl-2-(1-pentylindol-3-yl)acetamide. Base: C21H30N2O, 326.5 g/mol, CAS 3085600-43-4. Identity checked on PubChem. Synthetic cannabinoid with an indole core and, compared with the classic carboxamides, one more methylene group in the linker (acetamide); achiral. [f:pubchem] [f:norman2023-ch-piata]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

CH-PIATA was described in 2022 in the United States, Belgium (a powder seized by customs) and Scottish prisons (twelve times on infused paper or resin). [f:norman2023-ch-piata]

It is the second synthetic cannabinoid with an acetamide linker detected in the EU. [f:zschiesche2024]

In Mayotte, in the 2022 «La Chimique» samples, it was detected 14 times in 80 samples. [f:richeval2023]

## origin

A synthetic substance, an indole-3-acetamide synthetic cannabinoid; the move from the carboxamide linker to the acetamide linker is read as an attempt to circumvent legal regulations. [f:zschiesche2024]

## metabolism

CH-PIATA is extensively metabolised, mainly by mono- and dihydroxylation. Reliable markers of intake are monohydroxylation at the indole core or the linker methylene, the carboxylic acid on the N-pentyl chain and an N-propionic acid metabolite; the parent compound is found in blood but not in urine. [f:zschiesche2024]

In human liver microsomes the pentyl-hydroxylated metabolite (CP9) is the most abundant and is recommended for urinalysis; N-dealkylation and ketone formation are also seen. [f:watanabe2023-ch-piata]

## effects

In β-arrestin 2 recruitment assays CH-PIATA (reference material and seized powder) showed weak activity at both CB1 and CB2 receptors, with signs of antagonism; on these in vitro results the authors judge the expected harm potential to be limited. [f:norman2023-ch-piata]

## doses

The sources used have no dose data in humans, and the weak activity seen in vitro does not allow a dose to be inferred. [f:norman2023-ch-piata]

## risks

In a US blood sample CH-PIATA was present in a mixture of synthetic cannabinoids, together with at least five of its metabolites. [f:norman2023-ch-piata]

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of various recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids increases the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
