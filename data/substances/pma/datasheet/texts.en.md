## molecule

PMA (4-methoxyamphetamine, p-methoxyamphetamine), 1-(4-methoxyphenyl)propan-2-amine. Base: C10H15NO, 165.23 g/mol. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem] Hydrochloride: C10H15NO·HCl, 201.7 g/mol. [f:swgdrug-pma]

Bands of the hydrochloride (diamond ATR) labelled in the SWGDRUG spectrum: N-H2+ envelope with bands at 2707, 2605 and 2519 cm-1, ring bands at 1614 and 1506 cm-1, then 1462, 1387 and 1301 cm-1, and the bands at 1253, 1180, 1111, 1082, 1032 and 1001 cm-1, in the region of the aryl alkyl ether group. [f:swgdrug-pma] [f:smith-ethers] [f:smith-amine-salts]

## category

Chemical class: amphetamines. Expected effect: entactogen. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

PMA appeared on the drug market as a cheaper and more available replacement of MDMA. [f:rojek2016]

The first lethal case of PMA and PMMA intoxication reported in Norway was published in 2003. [f:refstad2003]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a ring-substituted amphetamine made to replace MDMA. [f:rojek2016]

In lethal cases PMA was found together with PMMA, and in an Israeli series it was probably a product of PMMA metabolism. [f:refstad2003] [f:lurie2012]

## metabolism

The sources read give no study of the metabolism of PMA itself. In human liver microsomes and in blood from fatal cases PMA was a minor metabolite of PMMA. [f:vevelstad2017]

## effects

PMA is more potent than MDMA but has a slower onset of action, which encourages users to take more. [f:rojek2016]

## risks

Three fatal cases with very high PMA in blood, 10-27 mg/L, were attributed to overdose with acute cardiorespiratory failure. [f:rojek2016]

In another fatal case PMA was 0.57 mg/L in peripheral blood and 0.59 mg/L in heart blood; ketamine and diazepam were also detected. [f:jang2016]

In a lethal case a 16-year-old was admitted in coma with seizures and hyperthermia; hypoglycaemia and hyperkalaemia may be specific to PMA poisoning. The author says the case reports collectively suggest PMA to be more toxic than MDMA. [f:refstad2003]

## interactions

In an Israeli series of 24 deaths with PMMA and PMA, 17 had co-exposures (MDMA, MDA, cocaine, cannabinoids, cathinone derivatives, ephedrine or pseudoephedrine, opiates, ethanol); the authors say stimulant co-exposures may have contributed to the severity. [f:lurie2012]

## counselling

Ask what the person expects: PMA was sold to replace MDMA, and under the same "ecstasy" label the UBC guidelines note structurally similar substances with different effects, duration and doses. [f:rojek2016] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain that PMA has a slower onset than MDMA, which encourages users to take more. [f:rojek2016]

Explain that hyperthermia, seizures and the search for cooling (undressing, submerging in water) were described in a lethal case. [f:refstad2003]

Ask about other substances: in fatal cases stimulants, ethanol and opiates were found together with PMA and PMMA; avoid presenting this as a causal link. [f:lurie2012]
