## molecule

ADB-FUBIATA, (2S)-2-[[2-[1-[(4-fluorophenyl)methyl]indol-3-yl]acetyl]amino]-3,3-dimethylbutanamide. Base: C23H26FN3O2, 395.5 g/mol, CAS 2938025-73-9. Identity checked against PubChem (the (S) entry). It is an indole-3-acetamide, not a carboxamide: the carboxamide linker of the earlier synthetic cannabinoids is replaced by an acetamide. [f:pubchem] [f:deventer2025]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

ADB-FUBIATA belongs to the compounds that appeared after the Chinese generic ban on synthetic cannabinoids of 2021, replacing the carboxamide linker with an acetamide. [f:deventer2025]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist (indole-3-acetamide of a tert-leucinamide with a fluorobenzyl group). It was found in mixtures with a tail-less precursor in forensic samples. [f:monti2025] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In human liver microsomes the most relevant metabolite for tracking ADB-FUBIATA was the monohydroxylated metabolite M03, with a metabolisation rate of about 10 % estimated from the peak area. [f:gish2025-fubiata]

## effects

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists are potent full agonists. [f:jones2025]

ADB-FUBIATA produced only weak locomotor depression in mice (ED above 6.1 mg/kg, against 3.3 mg/kg for Δ9-THC) and, tested up to 100 mg/kg, did not fully substitute for Δ9-THC in rats trained to discriminate it. [f:shetty2026]

## doses

The rodent data above are not a measure of potency in humans; no human dose data for this compound were found in the sources used. [f:shetty2026]

## risks

In 3D human neuron-like spheroids ADB-FUBIATA reduced cell viability (2000 nM after 24 h, from 800 nM after 48 h) and, with repeated exposure, caused cell death from 200 nM and loss of spheroid compactness from 50 nM. [f:de-simone2026]

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis (43.45 vs 22.91 days). [f:ricci2026]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
