## molecule

Dextromethorphan (DXM), (+)-3-methoxy-17-methylmorphinan. Formula C18H25NO, 271.40 g/mol, CAS 125-71-3 (base). Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

It is made as the crystalline hydrobromide salt. [f:burns2013]

The structure has an aryl methyl ether and a tertiary amine and no carbonyl. No band value is given because no verified reference spectrum is cited. [f:pubchem]

Cough preparations often also contain chlorpheniramine, acetaminophen or pseudoephedrine, which may appear in a mixture. [f:burns2013]

## category

Chemical class: morphinans. Effect: antitussive, dissociative at high doses. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:burns2013] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Misuse of dextromethorphan by children and adolescents has been documented for decades; a systematic review of misuse of over-the-counter medicines found that 54 of 92 articles concerned dextromethorphan. [f:burns2013] [f:schifano2021-otc]

## metabolism

Dextromethorphan is a prodrug: CYP2D6 converts it by O-demethylation to dextrorphan, the active metabolite; N-demethylation gives 3-methoxymorphinan, and both lead to 3-hydroxymorphinan. [f:burns2013]

The half-life is 2-4 hours in people with normal metabolism, with the peak 2.5 hours after intake; about 85 % of the US population are rapid metabolisers. [f:burns2013]

## effects

At the antitussive dose it suppresses cough, as codeine does, without analgesic or addictive properties. [f:burns2013]

At high doses dextrorphan blocks NMDA receptors like phencyclidine and ketamine, producing dissociation. Effects start in 30-60 minutes and last about 6 hours. [f:burns2013]

## doses

User-reported figures quoted by a review, which depend on CYP2D6 type, weight and tolerance: 100-200 mg for a mild stimulant effect, 200-400 mg for intoxication, 300-600 mg for a dissociative state, 600-1500 mg for a full dissociative state. [f:burns2013]

## risks

Intoxication causes tachycardia, hypertension, vomiting, nystagmus, ataxia and, at higher doses, hallucinations; extreme agitation can bring hyperthermia and metabolic acidosis. [f:burns2013]

Tolerance develops rapidly; chronic use can cause toxic psychosis and cognitive deterioration, and heavy use of the hydrobromide has caused bromism. [f:burns2013]

Co-ingredients of cough preparations add toxicity: acetaminophen delayed liver injury, chlorpheniramine anticholinergic signs. [f:burns2013]

## interactions

Serotonin syndrome is the best characterised interaction, mostly with SSRIs and MAO inhibitors, but also with linezolid, meperidine and tramadol. [f:burns2013]

## counselling

Ask about antidepressants (SSRIs, MAO inhibitors), tramadol and similar medicines: serotonin syndrome is the best characterised interaction. [f:burns2013]

Ask whether the product is a cough syrup or tablet with other ingredients (acetaminophen, chlorpheniramine, pseudoephedrine): they add their own toxicity. [f:burns2013]

Explain that the effect varies with the person's CYP2D6 type, weight and tolerance, and that tolerance develops quickly. [f:burns2013]
