## molecule

Procaine (2-(diethylamino)ethyl 4-aminobenzoate), formula C13H20N2O2, molar mass 236.31 g/mol, CAS 59-46-1. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI, which agree on formula and InChIKey. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

## category

Chemical class: local anaesthetic (ester type, grouped under non-psychoactive substances). Expected effect: no psychoactive effect expected. Typical role in samples: pharmacological adulterant. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:checkir-classification]

## metabolism

Procaine is an ester: esters are hydrolysed in the bloodstream by plasma esterases, while amides such as lidocaine are broken down in the liver. [f:becker2012]

Procaine is representative of the esters derived from para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA); hydrolysis releases a moiety that is potentially immunogenic. [f:becker2012]

## cuts

Procaine is on the list of the frequent cuts of cocaine. [f:unodc-cocaine2012]

Procaine is an aromatic ester, like cocaine, with a C=O band in the same region: an ester C=O alone does not point to cocaine. [f:smith-arom-esters]

## risks

Systemic toxicity of local anaesthetics is a potentially fatal complication; a practice advisory of the American Society of Regional Anesthesia and Pain Medicine describes its prevention, diagnosis and treatment. [f:neal2010]

Methemoglobinemia (a reduced capacity of the blood to carry oxygen) is described in a summary of 242 episodes related to local anaesthetics, with chocolate-coloured blood and a discrepancy between pulse-oximeter and blood-gas readings as common features. [f:guay2009]

## counselling

Local anaesthetic cuts weigh more when the cocaine is injected: service guidelines mention collapse, embolism and convulsions. [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines] Systemic toxicity of local anaesthetics can be fatal. [f:neal2010]

Ask about past allergic reactions to local anaesthetics: procaine is an ester derived from PABA and its hydrolysis releases a potentially immunogenic moiety. [f:becker2012]
