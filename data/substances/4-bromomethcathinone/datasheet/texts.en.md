## molecule

4-Bromomethcathinone (4-BMC), 1-(4-bromophenyl)-2-(methylamino)propan-1-one. Base: C10H12BrNO, 242.11 g/mol, CAS 486459-03-4. Identity checked against PubChem (racemic entry; one stereocentre, not specified there). [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cathinones (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

4-BMC (brephedrone) has been on the illicit drug market since 2011; EUDA data cited in a 2026 study indicate renewed availability in Europe, with substantial seizures reported in 2024. [f:nowak2026]

A Brazilian forensic laboratory identified it in 62 capsules seized in 2015 by ATR-FTIR, GC-MS, LC-MS and NMR, reporting earlier seizures in Finland, France and Spain. [f:machado2017]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cathinone: methcathinone with a bromine atom in position 4 of the phenyl ring. [f:pubchem]

## risks

Synthetic cathinones are associated with acute symptomatic seizures, often with lethal outcomes: in 42 reported cases death occurred in nearly half, with higher fatality among cathinone users. [f:machacek2026]

In the first reported fatal case with 4-BMC post-mortem concentrations were 16,601 ng/mL in blood and over 50,000 ng/mL in urine; 3-chloromethcathinone (1.7 ng/mL in blood, 122.2 ng/mL in urine) and bromomethcathinone and chloromethcathinone metabolites were also present, with no other substance detected. [f:nowak2026]

## interactions

In most cases with seizures, serotonin and sympathomimetic toxidromes were present. [f:machacek2026]
