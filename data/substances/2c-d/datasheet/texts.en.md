## molecule

2C-D, 2-(2,5-dimethoxy-4-methylphenyl)ethan-1-amine. Base: C11H17NO2, 195.26 g/mol, CAS 24333-19-5. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

Bands of the hydrochloride in the SWGDRUG library spectrum (ATR), read from the file to about ±2 cm-1: about 1207 cm-1 (the strongest; asymmetric aryl C-O-C), then about 1043 cm-1 (symmetric C-O-C), 1510, 847 and 1400 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-ir-library] [f:smith-ethers]

The 2,5-dimethoxy ring gives the same aryl ether bands in every 2C compound, so the band at about 1205 cm-1 points to the group, not to the substance; the SWGDRUG monograph of 2C-B notes that, with the parameters used, the IR spectrum does not tell 2C-B from 2C-I. [f:smith-ethers] [f:swgdrug-2cb]

## category

Chemical class: phenethylamines, 2C series. Expected effect: psychedelic. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

2C-D is one of the 2C compounds that the UBC guidelines list among those synthesised by Alexander Shulgin. [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Until the summer of 2012 the only 2C compounds in Schedule I of the US Controlled Substances Act were 2C-B and 2C-T-7; 2C-D was added to that list afterwards. [f:dean2013]

## origin

2C-D is a synthetic substance of the 2C series of phenethylamines, among the designer drugs. [f:dean2013]

## metabolism

In rats 2C-D was metabolised by O-demethylation at position 2 or 5 followed by N-acetylation, by deamination to the corresponding acids or alcohol, and by hydroxylation of the methyl group; most metabolites were excreted in conjugated form. [f:theobald2006d]

With human enzymes MAO-A and MAO-B were the major enzymes of deamination, and CYP2D6 contributed only to a very small extent. [f:theobald2007]

## effects

In the 2C series the reported effects combine hallucinogenic and stimulant ones and depend on the dose: stimulation and heightened senses at low doses, hallucinations at moderate doses, unpleasant hallucinations with tachycardia, hypertension and hyperthermia at higher doses. [f:dean2013]

In rats 2C-D substituted for at least one hallucinogen in drug discrimination, but not for methamphetamine; in mice it depressed locomotor activity, with stimulation at low doses; in cell assays it was a full agonist at the 5-HT2A and 5-HT2C receptors. [f:eshleman2014]

## risks

Intoxication with 2C drugs presents with hallucinations, agitation, aggression, dysphoria, hypertension, tachycardia, seizures and hyperthermia; the deaths attributed to 2C drugs showed an excited delirium picture. [f:dean2013]

The review lists no death or case series attributed to 2C-D. [f:dean2013]

## interactions

Because MAO-A and MAO-B deaminate the 2C compounds, monoamine oxidase inhibitors can raise their concentrations and the risk of toxicity. [f:dean2013]

## counselling

Ask which 2C compound the person expects: a product sold as 2C-I turned out to be 2C-E in a series of ten patients (one died), and the UBC guidelines note that 2C-D differs from the other compounds of the group in effects and doses. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Hallucinogenic effects depend on set and setting: ask where and with whom it will be taken, whether the person is well and has slept, and suggest not taking it alone; at moderate doses hallucinations appear and at higher ones they can be unpleasant. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain that the dose of one 2C does not carry over to another: the review quotes 12-24 mg for 2C-B, 10-25 mg for 2C-E and 14-22 mg for 2C-I, and the UBC guidelines, written for 2C-B, say that a few mg more or less change the effects and suggest approaching the dose gradually. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about MAO inhibitors and other medicines: deamination of 2C-D goes through the MAOs, and the UBC guidelines, written for 2C-B, advise against mixing it with alcohol and medicines, MAO inhibitors in particular. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
