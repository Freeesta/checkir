## molecule

Oxymorphone, (4R,4aS,7aR,12bS)-4a,9-dihydroxy-3-methyl-2,4,5,6,7a,13-hexahydro-1H-4,12-methanobenzofuro[3,2-e]isoquinolin-7-one. Formula C17H19NO4, 301.34 g/mol. Identity checked on PubChem. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: morphinans, among the plant-based substances of the UNODC grouping. Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

The medicine is a Schedule II controlled substance in the United States. [f:oxymorphone-label]

## history

Approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 1959. [f:prommer2006]

## origin

A semisynthetic μ-opioid agonist (14-hydroxy-dihydromorphinone), considered more potent than morphine. [f:prommer2006]

## metabolism

Oxymorphone is highly metabolised, mainly in the liver, by reduction or conjugation with glucuronic acid, to active and inactive products. The two major metabolites are oxymorphone-3-glucuronide (plasma AUC about 90 times that of the parent; its activity has not been evaluated) and 6-OH-oxymorphone, which has analgesic activity in animals. [f:oxymorphone-label]

Less than 1% of the dose is excreted unchanged in the urine. [f:oxymorphone-label]
