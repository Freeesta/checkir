## origin

A synthetic substance, an anxiolytic 3-hydroxy-1,4-benzodiazepin-2-one; it is also a metabolite of nordiazepam (see metabolism). [f:pubchem] [f:court2002] [f:stpierre1993]

## metabolism

Oxazepam is metabolised mainly by hepatic glucuronidation. The S form is conjugated by UGT2B15; the R form by UGT2B7 and UGT1A9. The D85Y variant of UGT2B15 lowers the activity on the S form 5-fold. [f:court2002]

In 6 healthy volunteers given 15 mg intravenously the elimination half-life was 6.7 hours (5.8 hours by mouth); urinary recovery was 80% of the dose (71.4% by mouth). [f:sonne1988]

In the perfused murine liver oxazepam is formed from nordiazepam and converted to glucuronides; this was not verified in man. [f:stpierre1993]

## effects

Benzodiazepines act on the GABA-A receptor and produce sedation, hypnosis, anxiolysis, muscle relaxation, euphoria, amnesia and addiction. [f:el-balkhi2023]

They are central nervous system depressants and sedatives that can cause psychomotor impairment. [f:wu2023]

## risks

Relative potency is hard to establish, because it depends on the structure and on the many GABA-A receptor subtypes. [f:el-balkhi2023]

## interactions

Pharmacodynamic interactions with other central nervous system depressants, such as opioids, are extensively reported; pharmacokinetic interactions with opioids are considered less important. [f:el-balkhi2023]

Combined with other sedatives, the risk of overdose increases. [f:wu2023]
