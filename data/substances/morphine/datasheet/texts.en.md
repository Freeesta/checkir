## origin

Morphine is an alkaloid of opium; heroin is derived from it. [f:milella2023]

## metabolism

Morphine is glucuronidated to morphine-3-glucuronide (M3G) and morphine-6-glucuronide (M6G); only M6G penetrates the blood-brain barrier. [f:degregori2012]

In 49 cancer patients the estimated plasma area under the curve of M3G was 29.7 times that of morphine after oral and 11.1 times after subcutaneous doses (M6G: 5.26 and 1.95 times); metabolite clearance rose with kidney function. [f:oosten2017]

## effects

Both the desired effect (analgesia) and the undesired ones (nausea, respiratory depression) are attributed in part to the metabolites, a contribution that is still debated; M3G is not analgesic. [f:wittwer2006]

## risks

In renal failure or in older people the ratios of M3G and M6G to morphine rise, which makes opioid toxicity more likely. [f:ravenscroft2000]

Periods of abstinence that lose tolerance, mixing with sedatives and using alone are risk factors for opioid overdose. [f:kerensky2017]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]

## counselling

Ask how long it has been since any opioid was last used: tolerance is lost after breaks, and amounts taken by regular users are far higher than those a non-habituated person tolerates. [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about benzodiazepines, alcohol and other sedatives: mixed with opioids they are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about kidney problems and age: when the kidneys work less, the glucuronide metabolites accumulate and toxicity becomes more likely. [f:ravenscroft2000] [f:oosten2017]

Talk about naloxone: it is a potent antagonist at the μ-opioid receptor, has no effect in someone who has taken no opioid, and emergency services should be called as well. [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
