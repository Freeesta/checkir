## cuts

Benzocaine is on the list of the frequent cuts of cocaine. [f:unodc-cocaine2012]

Benzocaine is an aromatic ester, like cocaine, with a C=O band in the same region: an ester C=O alone does not point to cocaine. [f:smith-arom-esters]

## risks

A single spray of benzocaine may induce methemoglobinemia, and clinical symptoms may appear at relatively low methemoglobin values. Reported complications include hypoxic encephalopathy, myocardial infarction and death. [f:guay2009]

In a Mayo Clinic series of 28 478 transesophageal echocardiograms with topical benzocaine, 19 patients developed methemoglobinemia (mean level 32 %), all with cyanosis and low oxygen saturation; 18 of them received methylene blue. [f:kane2007]

Systemic toxicity of local anaesthetics is a potentially fatal complication; a practice advisory of the American Society of Regional Anesthesia and Pain Medicine describes its prevention, diagnosis and treatment. [f:neal2010]

## counselling

Explain that benzocaine can cause methaemoglobinaemia (blood that carries less oxygen) even after a single spray, with symptoms at relatively low levels. All 19 patients of a large series were cyanotic, with low oxygen saturation: bluish skin needs urgent medical assessment. [f:guay2009] [f:kane2007]

Remind that cuts with local anaesthetics are described by service guidelines as most dangerous when injected. [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines] Systemic toxicity of local anaesthetics is a potentially fatal complication. [f:neal2010]
