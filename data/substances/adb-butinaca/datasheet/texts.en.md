## molecule

ADB-BUTINACA, (2S)-N-(1-amino-3,3-dimethyl-1-oxobutan-2-yl)-1-butylindazole-3-carboxamide. Base: C18H26N4O2, 330.4 g/mol, CAS 2682867-55-4. Identity checked against PubChem (the (S) entry). Some papers call it ADB-BINACA; PubChem uses that name for the 1-benzyl compound, which has its own sheet. [f:pubchem] [f:monti2025] [f:zschiesche2026]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

ADB-BUTINACA was first identified in Europe in 2019 and entered Singapore's drug scene in January 2020. [f:sia2021]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist (indazole-3-carboxamide of a tert-leucinamide with a butyl chain). It is produced from unscheduled tail-less precursors (ADB-INACA); the precursor remained in all recreated products and was found together with ADB-BUTINACA in forensic samples. [f:monti2025] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In human liver microsomes 15 metabolites were identified (hydroxylation, N-debutylation, dihydrodiol formation, oxidative deamination); the compound was rapidly eliminated via CYP2C19, CYP3A4 and CYP3A5, and four urinary biomarkers were proposed (three hydroxylated metabolites and one oxidatively deaminated). [f:sia2021]

In another microsome study 26 metabolites were identified; two monohydroxylated metabolites of the butyl chain were the most promising targets (41.7 % and 34.2 % relative peak intensity in vitro). [f:gish2025]

In three fatalities the dihydrodiol metabolite was suggested as a biomarker in urine and bile. [f:zschiesche2026]

## effects

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists are potent full agonists. [f:jones2025]

In mice ADB-BUTINACA was more potent than Δ9-THC in depressing locomotion (Δ9-THC ED 3.3 mg/kg), and in rats trained to discriminate Δ9-THC it substituted with greater potency. [f:shetty2026]

For ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA, full CB1 agonism explains altered perception, agitation, anxiety, paranoia, hallucinations, loss of consciousness, chest pain, hypertension, tachycardia and seizures; no data on the effects of this compound in humans were found in the sources used. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## doses

Potency varies widely among synthetic cannabinoids: ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA are up to 140 and 85 times more potent than Δ9-THC and no potency data for this compound were found in the sources used, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## risks

ADB-BUTINACA was implicated in three fatal mixed intoxications; femoral blood concentrations were 4.2-8.2 ng/mL and heart blood 5.7-11 ng/mL. [f:zschiesche2026]

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis (43.45 vs 22.91 days). [f:ricci2026]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
