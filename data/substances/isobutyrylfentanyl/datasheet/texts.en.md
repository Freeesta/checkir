## molecule

Isobutyrylfentanyl, 2-methyl-N-phenyl-N-[1-(2-phenylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]propanamide. Base: C23H30N2O, 350.5 g/mol, CAS 119618-70-1. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a fentanyl analogue. [f:armenian2018]

## metabolism

For the related 4-fluoro-isobutyrylfentanyl, studies in human hepatocytes and in authentic urine found mainly N-dealkylation (cleaving off the phenethyl moiety), monohydroxylation at the ethyl linker and the piperidine ring, and hydroxylation/methoxylation at the phenyl ring; these findings are not transferred to isobutyrylfentanyl itself. [f:watanabe2017]

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

In mice, isobutyrylfentanyl increased locomotion and produced dose-dependent antinociception with an ED50 of 0.0768 mg/kg, against 0.0800 mg/kg for fentanyl in the same test; naltrexone raised the ED50 233-fold, which points to an effect mediated by opioid receptors. [f:varshneya2019]

## doses

In mice the antinociceptive potency of isobutyrylfentanyl was close to that of fentanyl, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:varshneya2019]

## risks

In poisonings with fentanyl analogues larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

As soon as a compound is regulated new ones appear, which makes detection difficult; some illicit pills with these compounds look like known opioids or benzodiazepines. [f:armenian2018]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
