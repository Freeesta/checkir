## molecule

FUB-AMB (also AMB-FUBINACA and MMB-FUBINACA), methyl (2S)-2-[[1-[(4-fluorophenyl)methyl]indazole-3-carbonyl]amino]-3-methylbutanoate. Base: C21H22FN3O3, 383.4 g/mol, CAS 1971007-92-7. Identity checked against PubChem (the three names are synonyms of the same entry); one stereocentre, the entry is the (S)-form derived from L-valine. 5F-AMB is a different substance, with a 5-fluoropentyl chain instead of the 4-fluorobenzyl group. [f:pubchem]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist (indazole-3-carboxamide of a valine methyl ester with a 4-fluorobenzyl group on the indazole). [f:lobato-freitas2021] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In human liver microsomes, the ester hydrolysis metabolite of AMB-FUBINACA was the predominant one. [f:apirakkan2020]

## effects

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists are potent full agonists. [f:jones2025]

For AMB-FUBINACA (this compound) and ADB-FUBINACA, full CB1 agonism explains altered perception, agitation, anxiety, paranoia, hallucinations, loss of consciousness, chest pain, hypertension, tachycardia and seizures. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## doses

AMB-FUBINACA is up to 85 times more potent than Δ9-THC (ADB-FUBINACA up to 140 times), so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## risks

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis (43.45 vs 22.91 days). [f:ricci2026]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
