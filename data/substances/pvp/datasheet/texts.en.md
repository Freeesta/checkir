## molecule

α-PVP (α-pyrrolidinovalerophenone, α-pyrrolidinopentiophenone, O-2387), 1-phenyl-2-(pyrrolidin-1-yl)pentan-1-one. Base: C15H21NO, 231.33 g/mol, CAS 14530-33-7. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem] Hydrochloride: C15H21NO·HCl, 267 g/mol, CAS 5485-65-4, melting point 161.3 °C, UV maximum 253.0 nm; the SWGDRUG monograph describes a white powder (HCl). [f:swgdrug-apvp]

Bands of the hydrochloride (ATR) labelled in the SWGDRUG spectrum: aryl ketone C=O at 1682 cm-1, ring at 1595 and 1579 cm-1, ammonium envelope with bands at 2960, 2933, 2789 and 2690 cm-1, then 1498, 1469, 1450, 1371, 1338, 1234, 1184 and 1136 cm-1, and below 800 cm-1 the bands at 789, 771 and 719 cm-1, in the region of the out-of-plane bands of a monosubstituted ring. ## category

Chemical class: synthetic cathinones. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

α-PVP belongs to the 'second generation' of designer cathinones: after MDPV was listed as illegal, compounds such as α-PVP, with a very similar structure, were developed. [f:karila2018]

It is now a tightly controlled substance, banned in the United States and in other countries worldwide. [f:kolesnikova2019]

## origin

A synthetic substance, an α-pyrrolidinophenone (pyrovalerone-type cathinone). [f:karila2018]

## metabolism

In rat urine 11 metabolites were identified: hydroxylation of the side chain (then to the ketone), oxidation of the pyrrolidine ring to the lactam or its opening to the carboxylic acid, degradation of the ring to the primary amine, and hydroxylation of the phenyl ring. With human enzymes, CYP2B6, CYP2C19, CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 catalysed the side-chain hydroxylation. [f:sauer2009]

In urine the main analytical targets are α-PVP itself, β-hydroxy-α-PVP and the α-PVP lactam. [f:nobrega2018]

## effects

α-PVP is a potent inhibitor of the dopamine and norepinephrine transporters, but not of the serotonin transporter; its clinical effects include hallucinations, arousal, aggression and euphoria. [f:kolesnikova2019]

Reviews describe powerful cocaine-like stimulant effects, high brain penetration and a high liability for abuse. [f:nobrega2018]

The clinical effects of α-PVP and MDPV are individual and depend on the dose and the route of administration. [f:karila2018]

## risks

α-PVP can cause tachycardia, agitation, hypertension, hallucinations, delirium, mydriasis, self-injury, aggressive behaviour and suicidal ideation. [f:nobrega2018]

α-PVP and MDPV have been involved in a growing number of acute intoxications and of deaths. [f:karila2018]

α-PVP cannot be detected with classic routine analysis procedures, so its frequency of use is not known; knowledge of short- and long-term effects is limited. [f:nobrega2018]

## counselling

Ask what the person expects: α-PVP, sold as 'flakka', has a structure very close to MDPV and appeared after MDPV was banned; both are linked to intoxications and deaths. [f:karila2018]

Ask about route and pattern of use: in a survey of 96 cathinone users the nasal and intravenous routes were the most used and about 80% reported binge use; the UBC guidelines say to follow the rules for safer snorting and never share the straw. [f:schmoll2018] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about repeated intakes: α-PVP has powerful cocaine-like effects and a high liability for abuse, and the UBC guidelines warn that cathinone effects seem minimal at first, which makes overdose from repeated intakes easy. [f:nobrega2018] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about other stimulants and alcohol, which the UBC guidelines advise against mixing with cathinones; signs to recognise are agitation, tachycardia, hallucinations and chest pain, and for overheating the guidelines say to take the person into open air and cool them. [f:spiller2011] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
