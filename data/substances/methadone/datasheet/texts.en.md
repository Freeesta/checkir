## molecule

Methadone, 6-(dimethylamino)-4,4-diphenylheptan-3-one. Base: C21H27NO, 309.4 g/mol, CAS 76-99-3. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

In most countries it is given as a racemic mixture of (R)- and (S)-methadone; (R)-methadone accounts for most of the opioid effects. [f:eap2002]

## metabolism

Methadone is eliminated by biotransformation followed by renal and faecal excretion; CYP3A4 and, to a lesser extent, CYP2D6 are probably the main enzymes involved. [f:eap2002]

After an oral dose it is detected in blood after 15–45 minutes, with a peak at 2.5–4 hours and a mean bioavailability of about 75% (range 36–100%). The mean elimination half-life is about 22 hours (range 5–130 hours). [f:eap2002]

## risks

Blood concentrations for the same dose vary up to 17-fold between people, largely because of differences in CYP enzymes, and the half-life is long and variable. [f:eap2002]

Methadone inhibits HERG channels and is associated with QTc prolongation; HERG inhibition by opioids has been linked to torsade de pointes and sudden cardiac death. [f:elsherbini2023]

Periods of abstinence that lose tolerance, mixing with sedatives and using alone are risk factors for opioid overdose. [f:kerensky2017]

## interactions

Rifampicin, phenobarbital, phenytoin, carbamazepine, nevirapine and efavirenz lower methadone concentrations, probably by inducing CYP3A4, and can cause severe withdrawal; fluconazole (CYP3A4) and paroxetine (CYP2D6) raise them. [f:eap2002]

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]

## counselling

Explain that the amount is not transferable: blood concentrations for the same dose differ up to 17-fold between people, so a dose that suits one person cannot be used as a guide for another. [f:eap2002] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask how much time has passed since the last dose: the mean half-life is about 22 hours (range 5–130), so a new dose adds to what is still in the body. [f:eap2002]

Ask about other medicines (antibiotics, antifungals, antidepressants, anticonvulsants, HIV drugs): several of them raise or lower methadone levels. [f:eap2002]

Ask about benzodiazepines, alcohol and other sedatives, and talk about naloxone: mixed with opioids they are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
