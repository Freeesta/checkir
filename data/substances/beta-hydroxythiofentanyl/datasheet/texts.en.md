## molecule

beta-Hydroxythiofentanyl, N-[1-(2-hydroxy-2-thiophen-2-ylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]-N-phenylpropanamide. Base: C20H26N2O2S, 358.5 g/mol, CAS 1474-34-6. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

β-Hydroxythiofentanyl was one of nine fentanyl compounds targeted in 332 syringe extracts from needle-exchange programmes in Washington D.C.; it was not among those detected (fentanyl 72.29%, acetyl fentanyl 11.44%, para-fluorofentanyl 6.63%, furanyl fentanyl 3.61%). [f:giltner2022]

## origin

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

β-Hydroxythiofentanyl had sub-nanomolar affinity for the human μ-opioid receptor and acted as an agonist in vitro. [f:obeng2025]

The β-hydroxy and thiophene substitutions produce minimal to modest changes in potency. [f:canfield2026]

## doses

In mice the antinociceptive ED50 of six fentanyl analogues, β-hydroxythiofentanyl among them, ranged from 0.158 to 3.18 mg/kg against 0.122 mg/kg for fentanyl, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:obeng2025]

## risks

All six analogues tested, β-hydroxythiofentanyl among them, produced morphine-like subjective effects in mice, suggesting abuse liability similar to morphine. [f:obeng2025]

## interactions

Sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
