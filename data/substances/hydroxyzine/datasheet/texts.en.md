## molecule

Hydroxyzine (2-[2-[4-[(4-chlorophenyl)-phenylmethyl]piperazin-1-yl]ethoxy]ethanol), formula C21H27ClN2O2, molar mass 374.9 g/mol, CAS 68-88-2. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI, which agree on formula and InChIKey. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

## category

Chemical class: first-generation H1 antihistamine, used as a sedative and anxiolytic medicine (grouped here under other substances). Expected effect: depressant or sedative. Typical role in samples: pharmacological adulterant. [f:checkir-classification]

## metabolism

The European Medicines Agency's safety committee (PRAC) noted that hydroxyzine is metabolised by alcohol dehydrogenase and CYP3A4/5, and that cetirizine is an active metabolite. [f:prac-hydroxyzine2015]

The PRAC report gives an elimination half-life of about 14 hours in adults, prolonged to 29 hours in the elderly. [f:prac-hydroxyzine2015]

## cuts

Hydroxyzine is on the list of the adulterants and cutting agents that cocaine exhibits commonly contain. [f:unodc-cocaine2012]

## effects

Hydroxyzine is a first-generation antihistamine, authorised as a prescription-only medicine in the European Economic Area for anxiety disorders, skin conditions such as pruritus, dermatitis and urticaria, preoperative sedation and sleep disorders. [f:prac-hydroxyzine2015]

Its sedative effect is described as well known and well characterised; it is considered a useful effect in some indications and an adverse event in others. [f:prac-hydroxyzine2015]

In adults with generalised anxiety disorder, a randomised study of 80 patients found no difference in efficacy from lorazepam at 50 mg of hydroxyzine per day. [f:prac-hydroxyzine2015]

A meta-analysis of five studies (884 participants) found hydroxyzine more effective than placebo for generalised anxiety disorder and equivalent to benzodiazepines and buspirone, with a higher rate of sleepiness or drowsiness than the active comparators. [f:prac-hydroxyzine2015]

## doses

After its review the PRAC recommended, at the lowest effective dose and for the shortest possible duration, maximum daily doses of 100 mg in adults, 2 mg/kg body weight in paediatric patients up to 40 kg and 50 mg in the elderly. [f:prac-hydroxyzine2015]

Before the review the maximum daily dose in the national product information ranged from 75 mg to 400 mg, most often 300 mg. [f:prac-hydroxyzine2015]

## risks

After a review of non-clinical, clinical and post-marketing data, the PRAC concluded that hydroxyzine can block hERG channels and other cardiac channels, with a risk of QT interval prolongation and cardiac arrhythmia confirmed by clinical data. [f:prac-hydroxyzine2015]

The at-risk people identified were those with cardiac history, concomitant medicines that prolong the QT interval or an electrolyte imbalance; a case report describes torsade de pointes after hydroxyzine 50 mg in a patient with a cardioverter defibrillator. [f:prac-hydroxyzine2015] [f:afzal2023]

## interactions

The PRAC recommended that hydroxyzine be contraindicated with other medicines known to prolong the QT interval or to induce torsade de pointes. [f:prac-hydroxyzine2015]

## counselling

Explain that hydroxyzine is a sedative antihistamine found in cocaine and that regulators flagged a risk of QT prolongation and arrhythmia, higher with heart disease, electrolyte imbalance or other medicines that prolong the QT interval. Ask about heart conditions and about the medicines the person takes. [f:prac-hydroxyzine2015]
