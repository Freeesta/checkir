## molecule

Butyrylfentanyl, N-phenyl-N-[1-(2-phenylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]butanamide. Base: C23H30N2O, 350.5 g/mol, CAS 1169-70-6. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

At least 40 confirmed fatalities have been associated with the misuse of butyrylfentanyl. [f:lovrecic2019]

## origin

A synthetic substance, an analogue of fentanyl with an N-butanoyl group in place of the propanoyl group. [f:lovrecic2019]

## metabolism

In fresh human hepatocytes the main metabolite of butyrylfentanyl was nor-butyrylfentanyl (37% of the initial amount at 48 h), followed by the products of hydroxylation of the butanoyl group; CYP3A4 was involved in the formation of nor-butyrylfentanyl and CYP2D6 in that of ω-hydroxy-butyrylfentanyl. [f:kanamori2019]

In human liver microsomes and in an authentic case six pathways were proposed, with hydroxylation and N-dealkylation the major ones in vitro and hydroxylation of the butanamide chain followed by oxidation to the carboxylic acid the major step in vivo. [f:steuer2017]

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

## doses

The potency of fentanyl analogues varies widely between compounds, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lovrecic2019]

## risks

Butyrylfentanyl is a short-acting analogue of fentanyl with around one quarter of its potency. [f:lovrecic2019]

In poisonings with fentanyl analogues larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
