## molecule

α-PVT, 2-pyrrolidin-1-yl-1-thiophen-2-ylpentan-1-one. Base: C13H19NOS, 237.36 g/mol, CAS 1400742-66-6. Identity checked against PubChem (one stereocentre, not specified in the entry). [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cathinones (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

A 2021 study describes 44 forensic cases with analytically confirmed intake of α-PVT: plasma concentrations from about 0.9 to 306 µg/L (median 35.6), with co-consumption of other substances in almost all cases. [f:paul2021]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cathinone: an analogue of α-PVP in which the phenyl ring is replaced by a thiophene ring (2-thienyl). [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In vitro (human liver microsomes and S9 fraction) α-PVT forms hydroxy metabolites and lactams; the first steps are catalysed by CYP1A2, CYP2C9 and CYP2C19, with hydroxylation on the pyrrolidine, the thiophene or the alkyl chain. [f:manier2018]

## effects

Synthetic cathinones have stimulant and, in part, hallucinogenic properties; no data on the effects of this compound in humans were found in the sources used. [f:machacek2026]

## risks

Synthetic cathinones are associated with acute symptomatic seizures, often with lethal outcomes: in 42 reported cases death occurred in nearly half, with higher fatality among cathinone users. [f:machacek2026]

In the series of 44 forensic cases, aggressive behaviour and psychotic changes were frequent, with co-consumption of other substances in almost all cases. [f:paul2021]

## interactions

In most cases with seizures, serotonin and sympathomimetic toxidromes were present. [f:machacek2026]
