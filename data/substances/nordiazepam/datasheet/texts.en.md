## molecule

Nordazepam (nordiazepam, desmethyldiazepam), 7-chloro-5-phenyl-1,3-dihydro-1,4-benzodiazepin-2-one. Base: C15H11ClN2O, 270.71 g/mol, CAS 1088-11-5. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: benzodiazepines. Expected effect: central nervous system depressant (sedative). Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a 1,4-benzodiazepin-2-one; it is also the active metabolite of diazepam (see metabolism). [f:pubchem] [f:mandelli1978]

## metabolism

It is the active metabolite of diazepam, formed by N-demethylation, and it accumulates during prolonged treatment; in healthy volunteers the conversion of diazepam to nordiazepam averaged 53%. [f:mandelli1978] [f:greenblatt1988]

In 12 healthy volunteers (mean age 62 years) given 10 mg intravenously the elimination half-life averaged 93 hours (diazepam: 83 hours). [f:greenblatt1988]

In the perfused murine liver nordiazepam is hydroxylated to oxazepam, which is in turn converted to glucuronides; this was not verified in man. [f:stpierre1993]

## effects

Benzodiazepines act on the GABA-A receptor and produce sedation, hypnosis, anxiolysis, muscle relaxation, euphoria, amnesia and addiction. [f:el-balkhi2023]

They are central nervous system depressants and sedatives that can cause psychomotor impairment. [f:wu2023]

## risks

Relative potency is hard to establish, because it depends on the structure and on the many GABA-A receptor subtypes. [f:el-balkhi2023]

## interactions

Pharmacodynamic interactions with other central nervous system depressants, such as opioids, are extensively reported; pharmacokinetic interactions with opioids are considered less important. [f:el-balkhi2023]

Combined with other sedatives, the risk of overdose increases. [f:wu2023]
