## molecule

3-MMC (3-methylmethcathinone), 2-(methylamino)-1-(3-methylphenyl)propan-1-one. Base: C11H15NO, 177.24 g/mol, CAS 1246911-86-3. Identity checked against PubChem and Wikidata. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] Hydrochloride: C11H15NO·HCl, 213 g/mol, melting point 206.5 °C; the SWGDRUG monograph gives no CAS. [f:swgdrug-3mmc]

Diagnostic bands of the hydrochloride (ATR): aryl ketone C=O at 1687 cm-1 (the strongest band), ring at 1605 and 1585 cm-1, N-H2+ envelope with bands at 2713 and 2443 cm-1, then 1460, 1298, 1259, 1180, 1155, 1041 and 1007 cm-1, and below 900 cm-1 the bands of the meta-disubstituted ring at 897, 804, 754 and 721 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-3mmc] [f:smith-benzene] [f:smith-amine-salts] In KBr: 1686.4, 1603.7, 1585.0, 804.5, 753.7 and 720.2 cm-1. [f:unodc-cathinones2015]

Isomers: 2-MMC and 4-MMC have the same formula and a C=O in the same region (1684-1696 cm-1 in KBr); the out-of-plane ring bands below 900 cm-1 separate them. [f:unodc-cathinones2015] [f:smith-benzene] By NMR too, 4-MMC is easily told apart, while 2-MMC and 3-MMC are hard to separate. [f:unodc-cathinones2015]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cathinones. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

The WHO (ECDD committee) reviewed 3-MMC in 2016, at the 38th meeting, and again in 2022, at the 45th. [f:who-ecdd45-3mmc]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a ring-substituted cathinone, racemic. Its precursors are not controlled; a "masked precursor", N-acetyl-3-MMC, has also been described. [f:who-ecdd45-3mmc]

## metabolism

There are no controlled human pharmacokinetic studies (absorption, half-life, clearance). The only kinetic data are from 3 pigs: oral bioavailability about 7%, peak at 5-10 minutes, plasma half-life 0.8 h; the authors warn against extrapolating to people (small sample, low dose). [f:shimshoni2015]

In human liver microsomes and cytosol three products formed: a hydroxylated, a keto-reduced and an N-desmethyl derivative (nor-3-MMC). All three were found in 31 positive human samples (plasma, urine, saliva, hair): 16%, 52% and 42% of samples, with 61% positive for at least one. Enzymes and activity of the metabolites were not determined. [f:vanwichelen2024]

Nor-3-MMC, dihydro-3-MMC, nor-dihydro-3-MMC, hydroxytolyl-3-MMC, 3-carboxy-3-MMC and 3-carboxy-dihydro-3-MMC were detected in cases; metabolism may resemble that of 4-MMC. [f:who-ecdd45-3mmc]

## cuts

Among the common adulterants of cathinones the UNODC manual lists paracetamol, caffeine, lidocaine and benzocaine. [f:unodc-cathinones2015]

## effects

In vitro, in HEK293 cells with human transporters, 3-MMC inhibited the noradrenaline transporter and acted as a substrate-type monoamine releaser like classic amphetamines. Unlike other mephedrone analogues it did not inhibit the serotonin transporter more than the dopamine one, so the profile is stimulant-like rather than MDMA-like. [f:luethi2018-analogs]

In 14 healthy volunteers (crossover, placebo-controlled) 25, 50 and 100 mg raised the pressure-pain threshold and lowered perceived painfulness, most at 50 and 100 mg and for up to 5 hours; mood rose at 1 hour but not at 5. The abstract does not state the route and reports no other effects. [f:ramaekers2025-3mmc]

The routes described by users are nasal, oral and intravenous; injection is reported in chemsex ("slamsex"). Effects seem shorter and less intense than those of mephedrone, but the finding is uncertain. [f:who-ecdd45-3mmc]

## doses

User-reported oral doses: light 25-75 mg, common 75-150 mg, strong 150 to over 300 mg. Intoxications involve about 0.5-2 g, with a median of 1000 mg per session. [f:who-ecdd45-3mmc]

User-reported duration: orally 4-6 h in total (onset 10-30 min, peak 2-3 h); nasally 2.5-4.5 h (onset 5-10 min). [f:who-ecdd45-3mmc]

## risks

In Sweden, in 50 patients (August 2012 - March 2014), tachycardia (48 %), agitation (44 %), hypertension (38 %), dilated pupils (24 %), seizures (8 %) and hyperthermia above 39 °C (6 %) were seen. [f:who-ecdd45-3mmc]

Blood concentrations in 95 forensic cases in Poland ranged from traces (<1 ng/mL) to 1.6 µg/mL (median 18.5 ng/mL), mostly with other drugs. A review found no correlation between concentration and severity, and most intoxications and deaths involved several substances. [f:adamowicz2016] [f:kelecevic2024]

The EMCDDA counts 27 deaths with confirmed exposure, 8 of them with 3-MMC attributed or contributing; the literature reports 34 deaths. Some are in a chemsex context. [f:who-ecdd45-3mmc]

Dependence: no studies; a review suggests a potential for psychological dependence. [f:who-ecdd45-3mmc] [f:kelecevic2024]

## interactions

Co-exposure was common in the deaths (alcohol, GHB, benzodiazepines); the sources do not prove a causal link. No controlled interaction study was found. [f:who-ecdd45-3mmc] [f:kelecevic2024]

## counselling

Ask the route: the WHO describes nasal, oral and intravenous use, with injection in chemsex; with injection ask about equipment and context. [f:who-ecdd45-3mmc] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Reported effects are short and intoxications involve 0.5-2 g in an evening: ask how many repeated intakes and invite not to redose, because at first effects can seem mild. [f:who-ecdd45-3mmc] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about alcohol, GHB and benzodiazepines: they were common in the deaths; avoid presenting it as a causal link. [f:who-ecdd45-3mmc] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Signs to recognise: tachycardia, agitation, high blood pressure, seizures, temperature above 39 °C; the UBC guidelines say to cool the person and seek help for hyperthermia. [f:who-ecdd45-3mmc] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Remember that 2-MMC, 3-MMC and 4-MMC share a formula: the ring band below 900 cm-1 separates them only against a reference. [f:unodc-cathinones2015] [f:smith-benzene]
