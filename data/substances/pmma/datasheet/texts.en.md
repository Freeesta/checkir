## molecule

PMMA (4-methoxymethamphetamine, p-methoxymethamphetamine), 1-(4-methoxyphenyl)-N-methylpropan-2-amine. Base: C11H17NO, 179.26 g/mol, CAS 22331-70-0. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem] Hydrochloride: C11H17NO·HCl, 215.7 g/mol. [f:swgdrug-pmma]

Bands of the hydrochloride (diamond ATR) labelled in the SWGDRUG spectrum: bands at 2963, 2734 and 2470 cm-1, ring bands at 1610 and 1514 cm-1, then 1478, 1444, 1434, 1388 and 1301 cm-1, and the bands at 1250, 1187, 1174, 1117 and 1029 cm-1, in the region of the aryl alkyl ether group. [f:swgdrug-pmma] [f:smith-ethers] [f:smith-amine-salts]

## category

Chemical class: amphetamines. Expected effect: entactogen. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

PMA and PMMA have been banned in Taiwan since December 2005; eight PMMA deaths in young people were later investigated. [f:lin2007]

In Israel PMMA was not controlled when an outbreak of poisonings began; it was added to the controlled substances act in July 2007, and only three more deaths followed in the next year. [f:lurie2012]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a ring-substituted methamphetamine. [f:vevelstad2017]

In the Israeli deaths PMMA and PMA were found together and PMA was probably a product of PMMA metabolism; in Taiwan PMA was found in the heart blood of all eight PMMA cases. [f:lurie2012] [f:lin2007]

## metabolism

In human liver microsomes PMMA was metabolised mainly to 4-hydroxymethamphetamine, with low amounts of PMA, 4-hydroxyamphetamine, dihydroxymethamphetamine and oxilofrine; in three fatal cases 4-hydroxymethamphetamine and PMA were the main metabolites. CYP2D6 genetics had a significant influence on the metabolism. [f:vevelstad2017]

Formation of the catechol metabolite was two to seven times lower than from an equimolar dose of MDMA, which does not support catechol metabolites as the main cause of PMMA toxicity. [f:vevelstad2017]

## effects

PMMA is described as a hallucinogenic synthetic substituted amphetamine. [f:lurie2012]

## risks

PMMA has caused numerous fatal poisonings worldwide and appears to be more toxic than other ring-substituted amphetamines. [f:vevelstad2017]

In eight deaths in Taiwan (mean age 18.9 years) PMMA in heart blood averaged 4.312 µg/mL, range 1.208-15.824, and PMA averaged 0.213 µg/mL. [f:lin2007]

In 24 fatal cases in Israel post-mortem whole blood PMMA was 0.35 ± 0.24 µg/mL and PMA 2.72 ± 1.67 µg/mL. [f:lurie2012]

## interactions

In the Israeli deaths 17 of 24 had co-exposures (MDMA, MDA, cocaine, cannabinoids, cathinone derivatives, ephedrine or pseudoephedrine, opiates, ethanol); the authors say stimulant co-exposures may have contributed to the severity. [f:lurie2012]

In the Taiwanese deaths MDA, MDMA, ketamine, norketamine, hydroxymidazolam, methamphetamine and pentobarbital were also found. [f:lin2007]

## counselling

Ask what the person expects: PMMA has been found together with PMA in fatal cases, and under the same "ecstasy" label the UBC guidelines note structurally similar substances with different effects, duration and doses. [f:lurie2012] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about other substances: in fatal cases stimulants, ketamine, opiates and ethanol were found together with PMMA; avoid presenting this as a causal link. [f:lurie2012] [f:lin2007]

Explain that in human liver microsomes the metabolism of PMMA depended on CYP2D6 genetics, so the same product may act differently in different people. [f:vevelstad2017]
