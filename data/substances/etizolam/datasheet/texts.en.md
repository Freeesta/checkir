## category

## origin

Synthetic substance: a thienodiazepine derivative. [f:fracasso1991]

## metabolism

The main metabolite is alpha-hydroxyetizolam; at steady state its plasma concentrations are higher than those of the parent compound and it disappears more slowly (mean half-life 8.2 h). [f:fracasso1991]

In animals the metabolite has almost the same pharmacological potency as etizolam, so it may contribute significantly to the clinical effects. [f:fracasso1991]

In healthy volunteers, after therapeutic oral doses, the peak plasma concentration is reached in 0.5-2 h and the mean elimination half-life is 3.4 h. [f:fracasso1991]

## effects

From the kinetics of the compound and its metabolite, etizolam can be regarded as a short-acting benzodiazepine, with elimination between short-intermediate derivatives and ultra-rapidly eliminated ones. [f:fracasso1991]

## risks

Withdrawal appears more severe after short-acting benzodiazepines; etizolam has rapid elimination. [f:petursson1994] [f:fracasso1991]

Physiological dependence on benzodiazepines comes with a withdrawal syndrome of sleep disturbance, irritability, anxiety, tremor, sweating and palpitations; seizures and psychotic reactions are also reported at high doses. Withdrawal appears more severe after high doses or after short-acting benzodiazepines. [f:petursson1994]

## interactions

Concurrent use of an opioid and a benzodiazepine is associated with a higher risk of an emergency room visit or hospital admission for opioid overdose (adjusted odds ratio 2.14, 95% confidence interval 2.05-2.24; privately insured US patients 2001-2013, retrospective study: an association, not proof of causation). [f:sun2017]

## counselling

Ask whether the person also takes opioids or heroin: the UBC guidelines say benzodiazepines can depress breathing and that the risk of respiratory arrest rises with opiates; the combination with opioids is linked to more overdose visits. [f:sun2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain that the active metabolite lasts longer than the parent compound (8.2 h versus 3.4 h): the perceived effect may fade while residual activity continues. [f:fracasso1991]
