## molecule

Acetylfentanyl, N-phenyl-N-[1-(2-phenylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]acetamide. Base: C21H26N2O, 322.4 g/mol, CAS 3258-84-2. Identity checked against PubChem and ChEBI. [f:pubchem] [f:chebi]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Acetyl fentanyl had not been documented before in illicit drug use or overdose deaths when, in 2013, it was identified in overdose deaths in Rhode Island (10 deaths in the first report); it is not available as a prescription drug anywhere. [f:mmwr2013-acetylfentanyl]

A later study counted 14 overdose deaths in Rhode Island between March and May 2013 caused by acetyl fentanyl. [f:lozier2015]

## origin

Acetyl fentanyl is an illegally produced fentanyl analogue. [f:mmwr2014-fentanyl-ri]

## metabolism

Acetyl norfentanyl is the nor-metabolite measured with acetyl fentanyl in postmortem blood and urine. [f:poklis2015] [f:busardo2019]

In urine of 5 cases the mean concentrations were 1874 μg/L for acetyl fentanyl and 6582 μg/L for acetyl norfentanyl. [f:busardo2019]

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

## doses

Animal studies suggest that acetyl fentanyl is up to five times more potent than heroin as an analgesic, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:mmwr2013-acetylfentanyl]

## risks

In the first Rhode Island cases a fentanyl immunoassay (ELISA) was positive in all 10 deaths, but confirmatory GC/MS did not detect fentanyl and found acetyl fentanyl instead. [f:mmwr2013-acetylfentanyl]

In 4 deaths in Tampa involving only acetyl fentanyl, the mean peripheral blood concentration was 0.467 mg/L; when acetyl fentanyl or fentanyl was present, it contributed to the cause of death. [f:poklis2015]

## interactions

Acetyl fentanyl was found together with fentanyl in 3 of the 14 deaths in the Tampa series. [f:poklis2015]

Sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]

## counselling

Explain that an immunoassay positive for fentanyl may hide acetyl fentanyl, a fentanyl analogue not sold as a medicine anywhere, and that the confirmatory result can differ. [f:mmwr2013-acetylfentanyl]

Explain the signs of an overdose (miosis, respiratory and central nervous system depression) and that naloxone should be at hand. [f:armenian2018] [f:kerensky2017]

Ask about sedatives and other opioids taken at the same time: in the Tampa series acetyl fentanyl was also found with fentanyl. [f:poklis2015] [f:kerensky2017]
