## molecule

Quetiapine, 2-[2-(4-benzo[b][1,4]benzothiazepin-6-ylpiperazin-1-yl)ethoxy]ethanol. Formula C21H25N3O2S, 383.5 g/mol, CAS 111974-69-7. Identity checked on PubChem. [f:pubchem]

It is the free base; the marketed drug is the fumarate salt (2:1), and doses are expressed as base. [f:quetiapine-label]

## origin

A synthetic pharmaceutical substance: an atypical antipsychotic of the dibenzothiazepine class, marketed as the fumarate salt in tablets. [f:quetiapine-label]

## metabolism

Quetiapine is extensively metabolized by the liver: less than 1% of an oral dose is excreted unchanged, 73% in urine and 20% in feces in total. The major pathways are sulfoxidation to the sulfoxide metabolite and oxidation to the parent acid metabolite, both pharmacologically inactive. [f:quetiapine-label]

In vitro CYP3A4 is involved in the formation of the sulfoxide and in the metabolism of the active metabolite N-desalkylquetiapine. The mean terminal half-life is about 6 hours. [f:quetiapine-label]

## cuts

In a community drug-checking program in Los Angeles (DART-MS), 13 of 52 heroin samples (25.0%) contained quetiapine; all were identified by clients as expected to contain black tar heroin, and no client expected quetiapine. Fentanyl was present in 4 of the 13 quetiapine-positive samples (30.8%) and in 27 of the 39 quetiapine-negative ones (69.2%). The authors describe it as a novel additive and call for confirmatory studies. [f:friedman2026-quetiapine]
