## molecule

2-Methyl-AP-237, 1-[2-methyl-4-[(E)-3-phenylprop-2-enyl]piperazin-1-yl]butan-1-one: a cinnamylpiperazine, the 2-methyl derivative of bucinnazine. Base: C18H26N2O, 286.4 g/mol, CAS 98608-61-8. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem] [f:resnik2021]

## category

Chemical class: piperazines (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

It is an opioid with a piperazine core and a cinnamyl group, structurally distinct from the fentanyls, so it has no family sheet. [f:fogarty2022]

## history

Bucinnazine, of which 2-methyl-AP-237 is a methylated derivative, was first synthesised in the late 1960s and has been used in China for cancer-related chronic pain; it has been found in seized heroin in the USA and Europe. [f:resnik2021]

## origin

A synthetic opioid. Cinnamylpiperazines emerged as 'legal' opioids after the scheduling of fentanyl-related substances in the United States and China. [f:fogarty2022]

## metabolism

In a fatal case three hydroxylated metabolites of 2-methyl-AP-237 were identified in peripheral blood and urine. [f:truver2023]

## effects

In vitro, 2-methyl-AP-237 activated the μ-opioid receptor with an Emax of 125% (relative to hydromorphone), the highest efficacy of the cinnamylpiperazines tested but substantially lower than fentanyl; the potency of these compounds was lower than that of fentanyl and other novel synthetic opioids. [f:fogarty2022]

## doses

No human dose is established: postmortem concentrations of 2-methyl-AP-237 in four cases ranged from 820 to 5800 ng/mL, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:fogarty2022]

## risks

In a fatal intoxication a powder in a container labelled '2MAP' tested positive for fentanyl on a field test; blood and urine concentrations of 2-methyl-AP-237 were 480 and 4200 ng/mL. [f:truver2023]

## interactions

In the same fatal case alprazolam was also found in blood (55 ng/mL). [f:truver2023]

Sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
