## molecule

2C-H, 2-(2,5-dimethoxyphenyl)ethan-1-amine. Base: C10H15NO2, 181.23 g/mol, CAS 3600-86-0. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

2C-H has no substituent at position 4 of the ring, unlike the other 2C compounds; like them, the 2,5-dimethoxybenzene ring gives strong aryl ether C-O-C bands. [f:pubchem] [f:smith-ethers]

## category

Chemical class: phenethylamines, 2C series. Expected effect: psychedelic. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Until the summer of 2012 the only 2C compounds in Schedule I of the US Controlled Substances Act were 2C-B and 2C-T-7; 2C-H was added to that list afterwards. [f:dean2013]

## origin

2C-H is a synthetic substance of the 2C series of phenethylamines, among the designer drugs. [f:dean2013]

## metabolism

For the 2C series the main metabolic route is O-demethylation at positions 2 and 5, and MAO-A and MAO-B are the major enzymes of deamination. [f:dean2013]

Studies with human enzymes covered 2C-B, 2C-I, 2C-D, 2C-E, 2C-T-2 and 2C-T-7, not 2C-H: for all of them MAO-A and MAO-B were the major enzymes of deamination. [f:theobald2007]

## effects

In the 2C series the reported effects combine hallucinogenic and stimulant ones and depend on the dose: stimulation and heightened senses at low doses, hallucinations at moderate doses, unpleasant hallucinations with tachycardia, hypertension and hyperthermia at higher doses. [f:dean2013]

For 2C-H the verified sources give receptor data only: among the 2C compounds tested, 2C-H interacted with the 5-HT2A, 5-HT2B and 5-HT2C receptors and with rat TAAR1, mostly with Ki or EC50 below 1 μM. [f:rickli2015]

## risks

Intoxication with 2C drugs presents with hallucinations, agitation, aggression, dysphoria, hypertension, tachycardia, seizures and hyperthermia; the deaths attributed to 2C drugs showed an excited delirium picture. [f:dean2013]

The review lists no death or case series attributed to 2C-H. [f:dean2013]

## interactions

Because MAO-A and MAO-B deaminate the 2C compounds, monoamine oxidase inhibitors can raise their concentrations and the risk of toxicity. [f:dean2013]

## counselling

Ask which 2C compound the person expects: a product sold as 2C-I turned out to be 2C-E in a series of ten patients (one died), and the UBC guidelines name 2C-H among the compounds of the 2C-x group that differ in effects and doses. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Hallucinogenic effects depend on set and setting: ask where and with whom it will be taken, whether the person is well and has slept, and suggest not taking it alone; at moderate doses hallucinations appear and at higher ones they can be unpleasant. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain that the dose of one 2C does not carry over to another: the review quotes 12-24 mg for 2C-B, 20-40 mg for 2C-C and 14-22 mg for 2C-I, and the UBC guidelines, written for 2C-B, say that a few mg more or less change the effects and suggest approaching the dose gradually. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about MAO inhibitors and other medicines: deamination of 2C-H goes through the MAOs, and the UBC guidelines, written for 2C-B, advise against mixing it with alcohol and medicines, MAO inhibitors in particular. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
