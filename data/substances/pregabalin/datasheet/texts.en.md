## metabolism

Pregabalin undergoes negligible metabolism in humans: about 90% of the dose is recovered unchanged in the urine and the main metabolite, the N-methylated derivative, is 0.9% of the dose. [f:lyrica-label]

The elimination half-life is about 6.3 hours with normal renal function and oral bioavailability is at least 90%. [f:lyrica-label] [f:bockbrader2010]

Elimination is nearly proportional to creatinine clearance. [f:lyrica-label]

## effects

Pregabalin binds with high affinity to the alpha-2-delta subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels in the central nervous system; the mechanism is not fully elucidated. [f:lyrica-label]

In 15 recreational users of sedatives and hypnotics, a single 450 mg dose scored similarly to 30 mg of diazepam for «good drug effect», «high» and «liking». [f:lyrica-label]

In controlled studies euphoria was reported as an adverse reaction by 4% of treated people and 1% on placebo. [f:lyrica-label]

## doses

As a medicine, for diabetic neuropathy the prescribing information gives 150 mg per day at the start and a maximum of 300 mg per day; 600 mg per day was studied without added benefit and with poorer tolerability. [f:lyrica-label]

The maximum is 450 mg per day for fibromyalgia and 600 mg per day for postherpetic neuralgia and for spinal cord injury pain. [f:lyrica-label]

## risks

The prescribing information reports serious or fatal respiratory depression when pregabalin is combined with central nervous system depressants, opioids included, or in people with respiratory impairment. [f:lyrica-label]

Misuse of gabapentinoids usually occurs with other substances, mostly opioids, and a growing body of evidence indicates they increase opioid-related morbidity and mortality. [f:evoy2021]

A systematic review of 100 studies confirms that use together with opioids or benzodiazepines increases overdose risk. [f:evoy2026]

After abrupt discontinuation some people reported insomnia, nausea, headache or diarrhea, consistent with physical dependence. [f:lyrica-label]

## interactions

With opioids and other central nervous system depressants, respiratory depression and sedation can occur. [f:lyrica-label]

With opioids or benzodiazepines the risk of overdose increases. [f:evoy2026]

With oxycodone, lorazepam or ethanol there are no pharmacokinetic interactions, but the effects on cognitive and gross motor function add up. [f:lyrica-label]

## counselling

Ask whether the person takes it with opioids, benzodiazepines or alcohol: the effects on breathing and sedation add up and the overdose risk increases. [f:lyrica-label] [f:evoy2026]

Ask whether the person has kidney problems: pregabalin leaves through the kidneys and elimination falls with creatinine clearance. [f:lyrica-label]

Ask how long the person has been taking it and explain that stopping suddenly can cause insomnia, nausea, headache or diarrhea. [f:lyrica-label]

Ask why the person uses it: reviews describe misuse to seek euphoria, boost other substances or self-treat withdrawal, pain, anxiety or insomnia. [f:evoy2021]
