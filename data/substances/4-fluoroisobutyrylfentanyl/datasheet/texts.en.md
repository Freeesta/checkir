## molecule

4-Fluoroisobutyrylfentanyl, N-(4-fluorophenyl)-2-methyl-N-[1-(2-phenylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]propanamide. Base: C23H29FN2O, 368.5 g/mol, CAS 244195-32-2. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

A fatal mono-intoxication with 4-fluoroisobutyrylfentanyl was described in a 35-year-old man investigated by the Netherlands Forensic Institute. [f:roosendaal2023]

It is classified as a narcotic in Japan. [f:ono2022]

## origin

A synthetic fentanyl analogue; in the fatal case it was identified in a seized crystalline substance found next to the body. [f:roosendaal2023]

## metabolism

In rats given 4F-iBF intraperitoneally, the N-dephenylethylated metabolite was detected in serum 2 h after the dose, and in 24-h serum about 30% of the maximum 4F-iBF level remained. [f:ono2022]

The desphenethylated metabolites, likely the major ones, showed no μ-opioid receptor signalling response in cell assays. [f:ono2025]

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

In rats 4F-iBF fully substituted for heroin in drug discrimination, with a potency order fentanyl > 4F-iBF ≈ 4F-BF ≈ iBF > heroin. [f:lai2026]

## doses

In rats the reinforcing potency of 4F-iBF was intermediate between that of fentanyl and heroin, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lai2026]

## risks

In the fatal case 4-FIBF concentrations were 0.030 mg/L in femoral blood and 0.12 mg/L in heart blood, and the cause of death was attributed to 4-FIBF mono-intoxication. [f:roosendaal2023]

In rats 4F-iBF (5 mg/kg) caused hepatic and renal ischemic-like damage that naltrexone alleviated. [f:ono2022]

## interactions

Sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
