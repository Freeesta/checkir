## molecule

MDA-19 (BZO-HEXOXIZID), N'-(1-hexyl-2-oxoindolin-3-ylidene)benzohydrazide. Base: C21H23N3O2, 349.4 g/mol, CAS 1048973-47-2. Identity checked against PubChem (main entry, which draws the 2-hydroxy azo tautomer of the isatin hydrazone). Achiral. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## origin

A synthetic substance, an "OXIZID" synthetic cannabinoid (a benzoyl hydrazone of N-hexylisatin), first synthesized in 2008 as a selective cannabinoid CB2 receptor agonist for neuropathic pain. [f:dearaujo2023] [f:lee2022-oxizid] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

The four OXIZIDs studied, MDA-19 among them, gave 42 to 51 metabolites each in human liver microsomes and authentic urine; the main pathways were N-alkyl and phenyl hydroxylation, oxidation to ketone and carboxylate, amide hydrolysis and N-dealkylation. [f:lee2022-oxizid]

They were metabolically unstable, mainly metabolized by CYP3A4, CYP3A5 and CYP2C9, and inactivated CYP3A4 by a mechanism-based process; the parent compound and the N-alkyl and phenyl monohydroxylated metabolites were proposed as urinary biomarkers. [f:lee2022-oxizid]

## cuts

Of 53 seized samples analysed in Brazil, 25 contained only MDA-19 and 28 were mixtures, 11 of them with the CB1 agonist ADB-BUTINACA; cocaine and caffeine were also found in association. [f:dearaujo2023]

## effects

MDA-19 does not exert significant CB1 agonism, the activity behind the psychoactivity of Δ9-THC; no data on its effects in humans were found in the sources used. [f:dearaujo2023]

## doses

Potency varies widely among synthetic cannabinoids: ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA are up to 140 and 85 times more potent than Δ9-THC, and no potency data for this compound were found in the sources used, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## risks

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
