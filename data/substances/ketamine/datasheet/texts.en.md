## molecule

Ketamine, 2-(2-chlorophenyl)-2-(methylamino)cyclohexan-1-one. Base: C13H16ClNO, 237.72 g/mol, CAS 6740-88-1 (racemate). Hydrochloride: C13H16ClNO·HCl, 274.18 g/mol, CAS 1867-66-9. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

Forms: hydrochloride and base; the SWGDRUG monograph gives the spectra of both, in KBr and in ATR. [f:swgdrug-ketamine]

Diagnostic bands of the hydrochloride: cyclohexanone C=O at ~1720 cm-1, a broad, weak N-H2+ envelope near 2685 cm-1 and, as the strongest band, the out-of-plane bend of the ortho-disubstituted ring at ~772 cm-1, with a second one at ~719 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-ketamine] [f:smith-carbonyl] [f:smith-benzene] [f:smith-amine-salts]

Salt or base: in the base the C=O moves down to ~1699 cm-1 and the N-H2+ envelope is missing. [f:swgdrug-ketamine] [f:smith-amine-salts]

Possible confusions: the C=O of a saturated ketone (~1720 cm-1) lies higher than that of cathinones, which is conjugated with the ring (~1686 cm-1). [f:smith-carbonyl] [f:unodc-cathinones2015] Other arylcyclohexylamines with the same cyclohexanone have a C=O in the same region: the band points to the family, while the position of the ring substituents changes the bands below 900 cm-1. [f:smith-carbonyl] [f:smith-benzene] [f:unodc-ewa]

Cuts and mixtures: ketamine itself is often found in amphetamine-type samples in some regions. [f:unodc-ats2006]

## category

Chemical class: arylcyclohexylamines. Expected effect: dissociative. Typical role in samples: active principle. Chiral at C2; the illicit market sells the racemate (S and R), while the S enantiomer (esketamine) is the medicinal form approved for treatment-resistant depression. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification] [f:bonaventura2021]

## history

Ketamine was first synthesised in the United States in 1962 as a dissociative anaesthetic, in a search for an alternative to phencyclidine. [f:kalsi2011] [f:dinis-oliveira2017]

## origin

Synthetic compound, used as a racemate of S and R enantiomers. [f:peltoniemi2016]

## metabolism

In the liver ketamine is N-demethylated to norketamine, mainly by CYP2B6 (high affinity) and CYP3A4 (low affinity); the CYP2B6*6 variant lowers the intrinsic clearance at least 2-fold (in vitro). [f:li2013-cyp2b6] Hydroxylation follows, to hydroxyketamines (CYP2A6, CYP3A4/3A5) and hydroxynorketamines (CYP2A6, CYP2B6, CYP3A4/3A5); dehydronorketamine is inactive. [f:desta2012] [f:hasan2021]

Bioavailability in volunteers: about 20% oral, 30% sublingual or rectal, 45% intranasal (3 volunteers); intravenous elimination half-life about 120 min. [f:yanagihara2003] After oral dosing first pass favours the hydroxynorketamines (AUC ratio to ketamine 18-30, against 1.7-3.1 intravenously). [f:hasan2021] R-ketamine clearance is up to 50% lower than S. [f:kamp2020]

After chronic daily use, urine stayed positive for 22-96 days (cut-off 1 ng/mL, 7 patients). [f:dejong2020]

## cuts

Tablets sold as ketamine are often mixed with amphetamine, caffeine, cocaine and heroin. [f:kalsi2011]

Methoxetamine is sometimes sold as ketamine. [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

## effects

Non-competitive NMDA-receptor antagonist, with no affinity for GABA receptors. [f:peltoniemi2016] In rat brain homogenate (in vitro) the affinity for NMDA receptors is Ki 0.8 µM for S and 5 µM for R; mu opioid receptors bind both at 7 and 19 µM; other targets (sigma, dopamine, serotonin, cholinergic) have lower affinity. [f:bonaventura2021] [f:zanos2018]

It produces dissociative anaesthesia and analgesia, with central sympathetic stimulation and preserved breathing; S-ketamine is about 4 times more potent than R as anaesthetic. [f:peltoniemi2016] In healthy volunteers 0.1-0.5 mg/kg is enough to induce dissociative and schizotypal symptoms. [f:kalsi2011]

Users in an interview study (n = 90) sought "melting into the surroundings", visual hallucinations, out-of-body experiences and giggliness; half disliked memory loss and reduced sociability. [f:muetzelfeldt2008]

## doses

Typical recreational single doses, as a review reports them from the EMCDDA risk assessment: 50-100 mg intravenously, 75-125 mg intramuscularly, 200-300 mg orally and 60-250 mg nasally. [f:kalsi2011]

A typical recreational dose is 10-25 % of the effective general anaesthetic dose. [f:kalsi2011]

Onset and duration of effect by route: intravenous seconds and 30-45 min; intramuscular 1-5 min and 30-45 min; oral 15-20 min and 60-120 min; nasal 5-10 min and 45-60 min. [f:kalsi2011]

## risks

Long-term use is associated with haemorrhagic cystitis and urinary symptoms: among those investigated, reduced bladder volume in 57 of 77 and hydronephrosis in 38 of 70; abstinence is the primary intervention. [f:kalsi2011] [f:zheng2025] Norketamine, abundant in urine, is more cytotoxic than ketamine to human urothelial cells in vitro. [f:lin2022-norketamine]

Frequent use brings craving and high tolerance, without a physical withdrawal syndrome; in frequent users the dose had risen on average six-fold. [f:jansen2001] [f:muetzelfeldt2008] In 14 chronic users, PET showed up-regulation of prefrontal D1 receptors. [f:narendran2005]

In 68 deaths in Australia the median blood concentration was 0.2 mg/L and other drugs were present in 95.5%; the last route was injection in 32.4%. [f:darke2020]

## interactions

Serious interactions are reported with central nervous system depressants (ethanol, opioids, barbiturates, benzodiazepines) and with sympathomimetics. [f:kalsi2011] In the 68 deaths other drugs were opioids (59.1%), hypnosedatives (57.6%), psychostimulants (50.0%) and alcohol (27.3%): an association, not a proven mechanism. [f:darke2020]

Grapefruit juice (CYP3A4 inhibition) raised the AUC of oral S-ketamine 3.0 times and rifampicin (an inducer) lowered it by 86%; intravenous exposure fell by only 14%. [f:peltoniemi2012-grapefruit] [f:peltoniemi2012-rifampicin]

## counselling

Ask about alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines and GHB/GBL: depressants have serious interactions with ketamine, and with heroin the immobility can be mistaken for an overdose. [f:kalsi2011] [f:darke2020] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Suggest not using alone, finding a place to sit or lie, and not insufflating more than 50-100 mg: dissociative anaesthesia impairs movement and masks pain, so injuries go unnoticed. [f:kalsi2011] [f:peltoniemi2016] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain that frequent use raises tolerance and craving, without physical withdrawal. [f:jansen2001] [f:muetzelfeldt2008] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about pain, urgency or blood in the urine: they are symptoms of bladder damage and abstinence is the primary intervention. [f:kalsi2011] [f:zheng2025] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Remember that tablets sold as ketamine are often mixed with other drugs. [f:kalsi2011] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
