## molecule

Ketamine, 2-(2-chlorophenyl)-2-(methylamino)cyclohexan-1-one. Base: C13H16ClNO, 237.72 g/mol, CAS 6740-88-1 (racemate). Hydrochloride: C13H16ClNO·HCl, 274.18 g/mol, CAS 1867-66-9. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

Forms: hydrochloride and base; the SWGDRUG monograph gives the spectra of both, in KBr and in ATR. [f:swgdrug-ketamine]

Diagnostic bands of the hydrochloride: cyclohexanone C=O at ~1720 cm-1, a broad, weak N-H2+ envelope near 2685 cm-1 and, as the strongest band, the out-of-plane bend of the ortho-disubstituted ring at ~772 cm-1, with a second one at ~719 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-ketamine] [f:smith-carbonyl] [f:smith-benzene] [f:smith-amine-salts]

Salt or base: in the base the C=O moves down to ~1699 cm-1 and the N-H2+ envelope is missing. [f:swgdrug-ketamine] [f:smith-amine-salts]

Possible confusions: the C=O of a saturated ketone (~1720 cm-1) lies higher than that of cathinones, which is conjugated with the ring (~1686 cm-1). [f:smith-carbonyl] [f:unodc-cathinones2015] Other arylcyclohexylamines with the same cyclohexanone have a C=O in the same region: the band points to the family, while the position of the ring substituents changes the bands below 900 cm-1. [f:smith-carbonyl] [f:smith-benzene] [f:unodc-ewa]

Cuts and mixtures: ketamine itself is often found in amphetamine-type samples in some regions. [f:unodc-ats2006]
