## molecule

JWH-018, naphthalen-1-yl-(1-pentylindol-3-yl)methanone. Base: C24H23NO, 341.4 g/mol, CAS 209414-07-3. Identity checked against PubChem; achiral. AM-2201, its 5-fluoro analogue, is a different substance. [f:pubchem] [f:chimalakonda2012]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

JWH-018 was among the first aminoalkylindole synthetic cannabinoids to appear on the illicit drug market. [f:holm2016-jwh018]

A 2010 study reported it as one of the most prominent active agents in incenses such as Spice and other herbal blends. [f:wintermeyer2010-jwh018]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist of the aminoalkylindole type (naphthoylindole with a pentyl chain). [f:holm2016-jwh018] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In human liver microsomes, JWH-018 gave monohydroxylated metabolites on the naphthalene ring, the indole and the alkyl chain, plus dihydroxylated, N-dealkylated and side-chain carboxylated products. [f:wintermeyer2010-jwh018]

CYP2C9 and CYP1A2 were the major enzymes in its oxidation (CYP2C19, 2D6, 2E1 and 3A4 contributed little, fraction metabolised below 0.2); in human urine monohydroxylated and carboxylated derivatives were the major metabolites, and the primary metabolites kept high affinity and intrinsic activity at the CB1 receptor. [f:chimalakonda2012]

The ω-carboxylic acid is a major urinary metabolite but is hardly formed in microsomes; in liver cytosol, alcohol and aldehyde dehydrogenases oxidise the ω-hydroxy metabolite to it. [f:holm2016-jwh018]

## effects

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists are potent full agonists. [f:jones2025]

For ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA, full CB1 agonism explains altered perception, agitation, anxiety, paranoia, hallucinations, loss of consciousness, chest pain, hypertension, tachycardia and seizures; no data on the effects of this compound in humans were found in the sources used. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## doses

Potency varies widely among synthetic cannabinoids: ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA are up to 140 and 85 times more potent than Δ9-THC, and no potency data for this compound were found in the sources used, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## risks

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis (43.45 vs 22.91 days). [f:ricci2026]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
