## molecule

Ethyleneoxynitazene, 2-[2-(2,3-dihydro-1-benzofuran-5-ylmethyl)-5-nitrobenzimidazol-1-yl]-N,N-diethylethanamine. Base: C22H26N4O3, 394.5 g/mol, CAS 3085641-39-7. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: nitazenes (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Ethyleneoxynitazene was notified to the EMCDDA early warning system in the first quarter of 2023. [f:vandeputte2024]

It was identified in seizures in Europe; at the time of the metabolism study no intoxication had been reported. [f:taoussi2025]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a 2-benzylbenzimidazole 'nitazene' opioid. [f:vandeputte2024]

Its benzyl group is a dihydrobenzofuran ring instead of an alkoxyphenyl group. [f:taoussi2025]

## metabolism

In human hepatocytes 16 metabolites were identified; the main reactions were N-deethylation of the diethylamine chain, hydroxylation of the dihydrofuran ring and opening of that ring by oxidative cleavage, which gives the corresponding ethanoic acid. [f:taoussi2025]

In human liver microsomes the main reactions for six nitazenes, ethyleneoxynitazene among them, were N-dealkylation, hydroxylation and dehydrogenation. [f:huang2025]

## effects

Nitazenes are agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; in vitro their affinity and potency often exceed those of morphine and of fentanyl, and the real-world potency reported is often lower than the experimental one. [f:stangeland2025]

## doses

For the nitazenes, potency in vitro is very high and varies widely between compounds, while data on potency in humans are scarce, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:stangeland2025]

## risks

In rats ethyleneoxynitazene was one of 16 nitazenes that fully substituted for the discriminative stimulus of morphine, and the substitution was blocked by naltrexone. [f:gatch2025]

Nitazene poisonings often involve other opioids and sedating drugs; naloxone reversed the poisoning (median parenteral dose 1.20 mg), but some patients needed prolonged naloxone infusions, so longer observation may be needed. [f:stangeland2025]

## interactions

In poisoning cases several opioids and other sedatives were present, and polysubstance use complicates diagnosis and management. [f:stangeland2025]
