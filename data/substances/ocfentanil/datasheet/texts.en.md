## molecule

Ocfentanil, N-(2-fluorophenyl)-2-methoxy-N-[1-(2-phenylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]acetamide. Base: C22H27FN2O2, 370.5 g/mol, CAS 101343-69-5. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

The first reported death involving ocfentanil was a 17-year-old man who had snorted a brown powder bought online; the powder held acetaminophen, caffeine and ocfentanil, and femoral blood held 15.3 μg/L of ocfentanil. [f:coopman2016]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a fentanyl analogue. [f:armenian2018]

## metabolism

In mice the O-demethylated derivative was the main metabolite of ocfentanil, while in zebrafish larvae hydroxylation was the major route. [f:bilel2023]

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

In mice trained to discriminate oxycodone, ocfentanil fully substituted for it and was more potent than fentanyl; naltrexone reduced the oxycodone-like responding. [f:walentiny2019]

In a locomotor test in mice, ocfentanil at 1 and 10 mg/kg produced dose-dependent increases in locomotion, with a maximum effect of 79.27% of that of fentanyl. [f:varshneya2023-locomotor]

## doses

In mice ocfentanil was more potent than fentanyl in an oxycodone-discrimination test, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:walentiny2019]

## risks

In poisonings with fentanyl analogues larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

As soon as a compound is regulated new ones appear, which makes detection difficult; some illicit pills with these compounds look like known opioids or benzodiazepines. [f:armenian2018]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
