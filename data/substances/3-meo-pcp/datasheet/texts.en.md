## molecule

3-MeO-PCP (3-methoxyphencyclidine), 1-[1-(3-methoxyphenyl)cyclohexyl]piperidine. Formula C18H27NO, 273.40 g/mol, CAS 72242-03-6. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

It is a phencyclidine derivative with a methoxy group on the aromatic ring. Unlike ketamine and its analogues it has no ketone, so the C=O band that marks them is not expected. No band value is given because no verified reference spectrum is cited. [f:pubchem] [f:copeland2022] [f:smith-carbonyl]

## category

Chemical class: arylcyclohexylamines (phencyclidine type). Expected effect: dissociative. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

3-MeO-PCP was first identified in Europe in 2012. [f:arbouche2021]

In April 2021 the UN Commission on Narcotic Drugs placed it in Schedule II of the 1971 Convention on Psychotropic Substances. [f:copeland2022]

## origin

Synthetic compound, a phencyclidine derivative. [f:copeland2022]

## metabolism

In a fatal case, demethylated and piperidine-hydroxylated metabolites were found in blood and urine; metabolite-to-parent ratios were below 1, so testing for metabolites did not extend the detection window. [f:arbouche2021]

## effects

3-MeO-PCP is a non-competitive antagonist of the NMDA receptor, with stronger binding than phencyclidine and therefore more potent effects even at low concentrations. [f:pepe2024] [f:arbouche2021]

It is a potent dissociative hallucinogen and can cause acute agitation and cardiorespiratory effects. [f:copeland2022]

## risks

Before 2022, 15 fatal intoxications had been reported, only one attributable to 3-MeO-PCP alone; the first UK fatality suggests that toxicity may be worsened by exercise. [f:copeland2022]

Blood concentrations linked to death are unclear: the range reported in the literature is 50-3200 ng/mL, and a fatal case had 3525 ng/mL in femoral blood. [f:arbouche2021]

Persistent psychotic symptoms and cognitive impairment after use have been described; evidence is preliminary. [f:pepe2024]

## counselling

Ask where it will be used: heart rate, body temperature and blood pressure rise with exercise, and the authors of a fatal case caution against use as a club drug. [f:copeland2022]

Explain that the blood concentrations linked to toxicity and death are not well defined (reported fatal range 50-3200 ng/mL), so the amount taken cannot be judged from the effect. [f:arbouche2021]

Ask about psychotic symptoms or memory and concentration problems that persist between uses. [f:pepe2024]
