## molecule

2C-C, 2-(4-chloro-2,5-dimethoxyphenyl)ethan-1-amine. Base: C10H14ClNO2, 215.67 g/mol, CAS 88441-14-9. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

Bands of the hydrochloride in the SWGDRUG library spectrum (ATR), read from the file to about ±2 cm-1: about 1205 cm-1 (the strongest; asymmetric aryl C-O-C), then about 1030 cm-1 (symmetric C-O-C), 1487, 783 and 841 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-ir-library] [f:smith-ethers]

The 2,5-dimethoxy ring gives the same aryl ether bands in every 2C compound, so the band at about 1205 cm-1 points to the group, not to the substance; the SWGDRUG monograph of 2C-B notes that, with the parameters used, the IR spectrum does not tell 2C-B from 2C-I. [f:smith-ethers] [f:swgdrug-2cb]

## category

Chemical class: phenethylamines, 2C series. Expected effect: psychedelic. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

2C-C is one of the 2C compounds that the UBC guidelines list among those synthesised by Alexander Shulgin. [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Until the summer of 2012 the only 2C compounds in Schedule I of the US Controlled Substances Act were 2C-B and 2C-T-7; 2C-C was added to that list afterwards. [f:dean2013]

## origin

2C-C is a synthetic substance of the 2C series of phenethylamines, among the designer drugs. [f:dean2013]

## metabolism

For the 2C series the main metabolic route is O-demethylation at positions 2 and 5, and MAO-A and MAO-B are the major enzymes of deamination. [f:dean2013]

Studies with human enzymes covered 2C-B, 2C-I, 2C-D, 2C-E, 2C-T-2 and 2C-T-7, not 2C-C: for all of them MAO-A and MAO-B were the major enzymes of deamination. [f:theobald2007]

## effects

In the 2C series the reported effects combine hallucinogenic and stimulant ones and depend on the dose: stimulation and heightened senses at low doses, hallucinations at moderate doses, unpleasant hallucinations with tachycardia, hypertension and hyperthermia at higher doses. [f:dean2013]

In cell assays 2C-C was a full agonist at the 5-HT2A and 5-HT2C receptors; in rats it substituted for at least one hallucinogen in drug discrimination, and in mice it depressed locomotor activity. [f:eshleman2014]

## doses

The reported oral dose is 20-40 mg with a duration of 4-8 h; the figures are the anecdotal ones of Shulgin's 1991 book, quoted by a review, not results of controlled studies. [f:dean2013]

Products sold online often do not contain the substance or the dose on the label. [f:dean2013]

## risks

Intoxication with 2C drugs presents with hallucinations, agitation, aggression, dysphoria, hypertension, tachycardia, seizures and hyperthermia; the deaths attributed to 2C drugs showed an excited delirium picture. [f:dean2013]

In the review no death or case series is attributed to 2C-C; in rodents 2C-C produced conditioned place preference and self-administration, and at high doses impaired motor performance and memory and activated microglia in the striatum. [f:dean2013] [f:kim2021]

## interactions

Because MAO-A and MAO-B deaminate the 2C compounds, monoamine oxidase inhibitors can raise their concentrations and the risk of toxicity. [f:dean2013]

## counselling

Ask which 2C compound the person expects: a product sold as 2C-I turned out to be 2C-E in a series of ten patients (one died), and the UBC guidelines note that the compounds of the 2C-x group differ in effects and doses. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Hallucinogenic effects depend on set and setting: ask where and with whom it will be taken, whether the person is well and has slept, and suggest not taking it alone; at moderate doses hallucinations appear and at higher ones they can be unpleasant. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain that the series acts at small amounts: the UBC guidelines, written for 2C-B, say that a few mg more or less change the effects and suggest approaching the dose gradually; the review quotes 20-40 mg for 2C-C against 12-24 mg for 2C-B, so a dose known for one 2C does not carry over. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about MAO inhibitors and other medicines: deamination of 2C-C goes through the MAOs, and the UBC guidelines, written for 2C-B, advise against mixing it with alcohol and medicines, MAO inhibitors in particular. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
