## molecule

MDMB-FUBICA, methyl (2S)-2-[[1-[(4-fluorophenyl)methyl]indole-3-carbonyl]amino]-3,3-dimethylbutanoate. Base: C23H25FN2O3, 396.5 g/mol, CAS 1971007-91-6. Identity checked against PubChem (the (S) entry). It is the indole analogue of MDMB-FUBINACA. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

MDMB-FUBICA was one of four synthetic cannabinoids detected in 10 blood samples from suspected drug-use cases in a Chinese study of 65 compounds; the concentrations of the four compounds ranged from 1.9 to 23.1 ng/mL. [f:xi2026]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist (indole-3-carboxamide of a tert-leucine-derived methyl ester with a 4-fluorobenzyl group), reported as a new psychoactive substance found in Europe in 2014-2015. [f:doi2018-enantiomers] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In incubations with recombinant human carboxylesterases, ester hydrolysis was detected for MDMB-FUBICA and no hydrolysis of the amide linker was seen; with tert-leucine-derived compounds the hydrolysis rates were too low to model enzyme kinetics. [f:wagmann2022-hces]

## effects

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists are potent full agonists. [f:jones2025]

One enantiomer of MDMB-FUBICA acted as a CB1 receptor agonist in vitro but lacked CB2 receptor activity, unlike the other carboxamide-type compounds tested in the same study. [f:doi2018-enantiomers]

For ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA, full CB1 agonism explains altered perception, agitation, anxiety, paranoia, hallucinations, loss of consciousness, chest pain, hypertension, tachycardia and seizures; no data on the effects of this compound in humans were found in the sources used. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## doses

Potency varies widely among synthetic cannabinoids: ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA are up to 140 and 85 times more potent than Δ9-THC, and no potency data for this compound were found in the sources used, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## risks

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis (43.45 vs 22.91 days). [f:ricci2026]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
