## molecule

Diphenidine, 1-(1,2-diphenylethyl)piperidine. Formula C19H23N, 265.4 g/mol, CAS 36794-52-2. Identity checked on PubChem (one stereocentre, no stereochemistry in the entry). [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: others (diarylethylamines, a dissociative class distinct from arylcyclohexylamines). Expected effect: dissociative. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:checkir-classification]

## origin

Synthetic diarylethylamine, sold as a 'legal high' since 2013. [f:wink2016]

## metabolism

In rat urine the main steps were mono- and bis-hydroxylation at different positions, N,N-bis-dealkylation and combinations of them, followed by glucuronidation or methylation. [f:wink2016]

In human liver preparations hydroxy-aryl, hydroxy-piperidine and bis-hydroxy-piperidine metabolites formed; CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 each formed all three, CYP2D6 the first two. [f:wink2016]

## effects

Diphenidine is a dissociative substance; in 17 patients positive for diphenidine or methoxphenidine the adverse effects were similar to those of other dissociatives such as ketamine and methoxetamine. [f:helander2015]

## risks

In 750 suspected NPS intoxications in Sweden (2014), 14 tested positive for diphenidine and 3 for methoxphenidine; of those 17 patients, 76 % had hypertension, 47 % tachycardia, 65 % anxiety and 65 % altered mental status, and 47 % were severe (Poisoning Severity Score 3). [f:helander2015]

In 87 % of the cases other substances were also present, so the picture is not due to diphenidine alone. [f:helander2015]
