## molecule

Lidocaine (2-(diethylamino)-N-(2,6-dimethylphenyl)acetamide), formula C14H22N2O, molar mass 234.34 g/mol, CAS 137-58-6. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI, which agree on formula and InChIKey. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

## category

Chemical class: local anaesthetic (amide type, grouped under non-psychoactive substances). Expected effect: no psychoactive effect expected. Typical role in samples: pharmacological adulterant. [f:checkir-classification]

## metabolism

Lidocaine is an amide, broken down in the liver; its major metabolite is monoethylglycinexylidide (MEGX). [f:olkkola2005] [f:becker2012]

Blocking CYP1A2 with fluvoxamine reduced the clearance of intravenous lidocaine by 41 %, while inhibitors of CYP3A4 have a minor effect. The elimination half-life was 2.6 hours in volunteers without inhibitors. [f:olkkola2005]

## cuts

Lidocaine is on the list of the frequent cuts of cocaine. [f:unodc-cocaine2012]

Internal guidelines of a drug checking service call local anaesthetics such as lidocaine, procaine and benzocaine cosmetic cuts, because they mimic some effects of cocaine, and report lidocaine and mannitol as the most frequent cuts of cocaine in Italy. [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

## risks

Systemic toxicity of local anaesthetics is a potentially fatal complication; a practice advisory of the American Society of Regional Anesthesia and Pain Medicine describes its prevention, diagnosis and treatment. [f:neal2010]

## interactions

Fluvoxamine (a CYP1A2 inhibitor) decreased the clearance of intravenous lidocaine by 41 % in volunteers, and the authors concluded that it may increase the risk of lidocaine toxicity. [f:olkkola2005]

## counselling

Explain that injecting cocaine cut with a local anaesthetic is the riskier route: service guidelines name collapse, embolism and convulsions, and cardiac arrhythmias with lidocaine. [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines] Systemic toxicity of local anaesthetics is a potentially fatal complication. [f:neal2010]

Ask whether the person takes fluvoxamine, an antidepressant: it reduced lidocaine clearance by 41 % and may increase the risk of lidocaine toxicity. [f:olkkola2005]
