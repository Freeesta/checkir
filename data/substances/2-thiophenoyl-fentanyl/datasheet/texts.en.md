## molecule

2-Thiophenoyl-fentanyl (thiofuranylfentanyl), N-phenyl-N-[1-(2-phenylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]thiophene-2-carboxamide: fentanyl with the propanoyl group replaced by a thiophene-2-carbonyl group. Base: C24H26N2OS, 390.5 g/mol, CAS 2306823-38-9. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## origin

A synthetic fentanyl analogue. [f:varshneya2019]

## metabolism

No metabolism study of this compound was found. For new fentanyl analogues, metabolism is anticipated to involve hydrolysis, hydroxylation, N- and O-dealkylation and O-methylation, followed by glucuronide or sulfate conjugation. [f:wilde2019]

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

In mice thiophenefentanyl produced dose-dependent antinociception (ED50 4.66 mg/kg, against 0.0800 mg/kg for fentanyl and 7.82 mg/kg for morphine) and increased locomotion at 100 mg/kg; naltrexone raised the ED50 34.6 times, so the effect is opioid-receptor mediated. [f:varshneya2019]

## doses

Potency varies widely between fentanyl analogues, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lovrecic2019]

## risks

In poisonings with these compounds larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

## interactions

Sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
