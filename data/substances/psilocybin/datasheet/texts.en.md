## molecule

Psilocybin, [3-[2-(dimethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-yl] dihydrogen phosphate. C12H17N2O4P, 284.25 g/mol, CAS 520-52-5. Identity checked on PubChem. [f:pubchem]

Structure: a 4-phosphate ester of an indole with a dimethylaminoethyl chain. Expected bands: indole N-H at 3380-3490 cm-1 and a broad phosphate stretch at 990-1100 cm-1. There is no reference spectrum of psilocybin in the public data, so these ranges only indicate the groups. [f:coates2000] [f:nist-webbook] [f:smith-inorg3]

Psilocin, the active form formed in the body, is a separate substance: this sheet covers psilocybin. [f:euda-mushrooms]

## category

Chemical class: tryptamines. Expected effect: psychedelic. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Psilocybin, contained in the sacred mushrooms of the Aztecs, and DMT, present in the South American beverage ayahuasca, have been used since ancient times in sociocultural and ritual contexts. [f:araujo2015]

## origin

Natural: psilocybin is the main psychoactive ingredient of hallucinogenic mushrooms, fungi that contain psilocybin and psilocin. [f:euda-mushrooms]

## metabolism

Psilocybin is rapidly dephosphorylated to psilocin, even when given by injection: after 1 mg intravenously psilocin peaked 1.9 ± 1.0 minutes after the injection. [f:hasler1997]

After 10-20 mg (0.224 mg/kg) by mouth, psilocin peaked at 8.2 ± 2.8 ng/mL after 105 ± 37 minutes and 4-hydroxyindole-3-acetic acid at 150 ± 61 ng/mL after 113 ± 41 minutes. The absolute bioavailability of psilocin was 52.7 ± 20% (3 subjects). [f:hasler1997]

Psilocin is mainly glucuronidated or converted to 4-hydroxyindole-3-acetic acid. In vitro MAO-A, CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 take part; UGT1A10 did not extensively metabolise psilocin, and CYP2D6 genotype did not change psilocin plasma levels in the people studied. [f:thomann2024]

## effects

In 8 volunteers psilocybin at 45-315 µg/kg increased the scores of all core dimensions of altered states of consciousness in a dose-dependent way. [f:hasler2004]

Psilocin is the pharmacologically active form; at low doses the main effects are changes in perception and mood while the person stays lucid. [f:euda-mushrooms]

## doses

Doses in the dose-effect study: 45, 115, 215 and 315 µg/kg of body weight. Mean arterial pressure rose moderately only 60 minutes after the highest dose; ECG and body temperature were not affected by any dose. [f:hasler2004]

In the pharmacokinetic study psilocybin was given at 0.224 mg/kg by mouth (10-20 mg) and at 1 mg intravenously. [f:hasler1997]

## risks

Classical hallucinogens, psilocybin among them, are relatively well tolerated physiologically and are not considered drugs of dependence, but they carry unique psychological risks. The most likely is overwhelming distress during drug action (a 'bad trip'); prolonged psychoses are less common. [f:johnson2008]

In the dose-effect study one volunteer had transient anxiety at the highest dose, and the performance on an attention test fell by 50% after the medium and high doses. [f:hasler2004]

## interactions

Among 62 online reports of lithium taken with classic psychedelics such as LSD or psilocybin, 47% involved seizures; none of 34 reports with lamotrigine did. These are self-reports, not controlled data. [f:nayak2021]

## counselling

Ask about state of mind, sleep and company: the most likely risk of classical hallucinogens is overwhelming distress, and the UBC guidelines add that mushrooms are not a party drug and should not be eaten alone. [f:johnson2008] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain that psilocin peaks in plasma on average 105 minutes after an oral dose, so the full effect is not immediate; the UBC guidelines give onset after 15-60 minutes and effects of 3-7 hours, depending on variety and route. [f:hasler1997] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Say that effects grow with the dose, and that the UBC guidelines give different dried-mushroom ranges for each species and ten times more for fresh ones. [f:hasler2004] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about medicines, above all lithium: seizures were reported in 47% of 62 online reports of lithium with classic psychedelics. [f:nayak2021]
