## molecule

Methoxetamine (MXE), 2-(ethylamino)-2-(3-methoxyphenyl)cyclohexan-1-one. Base: C15H21NO2, 247.33 g/mol, CAS 1239943-76-0. Identity checked on PubChem. [f:pubchem]

Structure: the same cyclohexanone as ketamine, but with ethylamino in place of methylamino and a meta-disubstituted ring carrying an aryl methyl ether in place of the ortho chlorine. By analogy with ketamine the C=O is expected in the 1700-1720 cm-1 zone; there is no verified reference spectrum here and the band only indicates the family. [f:swgdrug-ketamine] [f:smith-carbonyl] [f:smith-ethers] [f:smith-benzene]

## category

Chemical class: arylcyclohexylamines. Expected effect: dissociative. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:checkir-classification]

## history

The risks of methoxetamine were assessed by the EMCDDA in April 2014. A Council Implementing Decision of 25 September 2014 subjected it to control measures, together with 25I-NBOMe, AH-7921 and MDPV. [f:euda-mxe-ra]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a ketamine analogue of the arylcyclohexylamine class. [f:zawilska2014]

## metabolism

In the urine of 3 acute-toxicity cases, normethoxetamine, O-desmethylmethoxetamine, dihydro- and dehydro-methoxetamine, hydroxylated isomers and glucuronides were confirmed. [f:menzies2014]

## effects

It is a ketamine analogue with a much longer duration of action. [f:zawilska2014]

## risks

It poses a significant health risk, including fatal intoxication. [f:zawilska2014]

Among 47 telephone enquiries to a poisons information service, the pictures were stimulant (36 %), reduced consciousness (17 %), dissociative (11 %) and cerebellar (6.4 %); mental-health disturbance in 43 %. [f:hill2013]

A fatal case with methoxetamine is described. [f:adamowicz2014]

## counselling

Ask whether the person is taking it as ketamine: the UBC guidelines say it is sold as ketamine and ketamine as it, and that dosing it like ketamine risks overdose; the duration is much longer. [f:zawilska2014] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Signs to recognise: stimulation, reduced consciousness, balance and coordination disturbances; the UBC guidelines also mention slowed breathing and heart rate at high doses. [f:hill2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask whether the person knows it has been under control measures in the EU since 2014. [f:euda-mxe-ra]
