## molecule

3-Furanyl-fentanyl, N-phenyl-N-[1-(2-phenylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]furan-3-carboxamide: fentanyl with the propanoyl group replaced by a furan-3-carbonyl group (a positional isomer of furanylfentanyl, which has the furan-2-carbonyl group). Base: C24H26N2O2, 374.5 g/mol, CAS 101343-82-2. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

3-Furanyl fentanyl was placed in Schedule I of the US Controlled Substances Act by a DEA final rule effective 7 December 2023. [f:dea-fr2023-26694]

## origin

A synthetic fentanyl analogue; the DEA rule lists it among nine fentanyl-related substances with no accepted medical use in the United States. [f:dea-fr2023-26694]

## metabolism

No metabolism study of this compound was found. For new fentanyl analogues, metabolism is anticipated to involve hydrolysis, hydroxylation, N- and O-dealkylation and O-methylation, followed by glucuronide or sulfate conjugation. [f:wilde2019]

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

In mice 3-furanylfentanyl produced dose-dependent antinociception (ED50 0.51 mg/kg, against 0.08 mg/kg for fentanyl) and increased locomotion at 10 mg/kg; naltrexone raised the ED50 20.6 times. [f:varshneya2021]

The US DEA found that this substance and eight other fentanyl-related substances have been shown to bind and act as μ-opioid receptor agonists. [f:dea-fr2023-26694]

## doses

Potency varies widely between fentanyl analogues, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lovrecic2019]

## risks

In poisonings with these compounds larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

## interactions

Sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
