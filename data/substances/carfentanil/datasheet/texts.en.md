## molecule

Carfentanil, methyl 1-(2-phenylethyl)-4-(N-propanoylanilino)piperidine-4-carboxylate. Base: C24H30N2O3, 394.5 g/mol, CAS 59708-52-0. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

In 2016, in ten US states, 700 deaths tested positive for fentanyl analogues, most frequently carfentanil, furanylfentanyl and acetylfentanyl. [f:lovrecic2019]

## origin

A synthetic substance, an analogue of fentanyl with a methyl ester on the piperidine ring. [f:lovrecic2019]

## metabolism

In pooled human hepatocytes twelve metabolites of carfentanil were identified; N-dealkylation and monohydroxylation of the piperidine ring were the dominant pathways, two N-oxides and one glucuronide were observed, and ester hydrolysis was not a major pathway. [f:feasel2016]

Clearance was rapid in human liver microsomes (CYP enzymes) but much slower in hepatocytes, which may relate to the long duration of its effects. [f:feasel2016]

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

## doses

The potency of fentanyl analogues varies widely between compounds, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lovrecic2019]

## risks

Carfentanil may be 10000 times more potent than morphine, and sufentanil, the strongest fentanyl analogue registered for human use, is 10–20 times less potent than carfentanil. [f:lovrecic2019]

In poisonings with fentanyl analogues larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
