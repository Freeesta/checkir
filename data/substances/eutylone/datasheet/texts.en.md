## origin

A synthetic substance, a ring-substituted cathinone with a methylenedioxy group: butylone with an ethyl group instead of the methyl on the nitrogen. [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In liver microsomes and hepatocytes of four species 20 metabolites were identified (O-demethylenation, N-deethylation, β-ketone reduction, hydroxylation, O-methylation, glucuronidation, sulfation); sixteen were conserved across species, four catechol metabolites only in rodents. CYP2D6 was the main enzyme for O-demethylenation, with CYP2C19 and CYP2B6 secondary. [f:lee2026]

In rat liver microsomes eutylone had low metabolic stability (in vitro half-life 2.27 min) and eight metabolites; demethylenation and O-glucuronidation were pivotal. [f:godoi2025]

In an ex vivo perfused pig liver its plasma half-life was 0.25 min and the main plasma metabolites also reached the bile. [f:pelletier2025]

## effects

Synthetic cathinones have stimulant and, in part, hallucinogenic properties; no data on the effects of this compound in humans were found in the sources used. [f:machacek2026]

## risks

Synthetic cathinones are associated with acute symptomatic seizures, often with lethal outcomes: in 42 reported cases death occurred in nearly half, with higher fatality among cathinone users. [f:machacek2026]

## interactions

In most cases with seizures, serotonin and sympathomimetic toxidromes were present. [f:machacek2026]

In vitro eutylone caused time-dependent inhibition of CYP2D6 (Ki 4.2 µM) and CYP2B6 (Ki 32.9 µM), with an efficiency comparable to that reported for MDMA, which suggests it can raise exposure to co-used substrates of these enzymes. [f:lee2026]
