## molecule

Arketamine, (2R)-2-(2-chlorophenyl)-2-(methylamino)cyclohexan-1-one. Base: C13H16ClNO, 237.72 g/mol, CAS 33643-49-1. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

It is the R enantiomer of ketamine, which is a racemate of two optical isomers: same formula and connectivity as the racemic ketamine sheet, with a different configuration at the quaternary carbon. This sheet is not an alias of ketamine. [f:peltoniemi2016] [f:pubchem]

Like ketamine, it has a cyclohexanone: the C=O gives a band in the region of saturated ketones, and the position of the ring substituent changes the bands below 900 cm-1. No band value is given here because no verified reference spectrum is cited. [f:smith-carbonyl] [f:smith-benzene]

## category

Chemical class: arylcyclohexylamines. Expected effect: dissociative. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Arketamine is studied for depression and neurological disorders; its safety, efficacy and optimal dosing still need research. [f:kawczak2024]

In a placebo-controlled study, 50 healthy subjects received single intravenous doses of (R)-ketamine hydrochloride of 10 to 180 mg. [f:wang2025-rketamine]

## origin

A synthetic compound: the R enantiomer of ketamine. [f:peltoniemi2016] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In human, monkey and mouse liver microsomes the predominant metabolite of (R)-ketamine is (R)-norketamine; in rat and dog, demethylated and mono-oxidized metabolites. [f:wang2025-rketamine-preclinical]

In healthy subjects given single intravenous doses, (R)-norketamine was measured in plasma together with (R)-ketamine. [f:wang2025-rketamine]
