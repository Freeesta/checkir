## molecule

mCPP (meta-chlorophenylpiperazine), 1-(3-chlorophenyl)piperazine. Base: C10H13ClN2, 196.67 g/mol, CAS 6640-24-0. Identity checked on PubChem. [f:pubchem]

There is no verified reference spectrum here: by structure a meta-disubstituted ring (C-H wag at 810-750 cm-1 with a ring bend near 690 cm-1), a C-Cl stretch at lower wavenumber than a C-F one and, in the hydrochloride, an N-H2+ envelope are expected; these bands only indicate the family. [f:smith-benzene] [f:smith-halogen] [f:smith-amine-salts]

## category

Chemical class: piperazines. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: pharmacological adulterant. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

A 2005 report described mCPP, used in psychiatric research as a probe of serotonin function, as newly found in street drugs in the Netherlands, both in tablets and powders. [f:bossong2005]

In Dutch ecstasy tablets, a growing share containing mCPP after 2004 lowered the proportion of tablets with MDMA only. [f:vogels2009]

## origin

A synthetic substance; it is also an active metabolite of several antidepressants, among them trazodone, nefazodone and etoperidone. [f:wen2008]

## metabolism

Trazodone is converted to mCPP mainly by CYP3A4. [f:rotzinger1998]

mCPP is hydroxylated to hydroxy-mCPP by CYP2D6; in a documented intake the mCPP/hydroxy-mCPP ratio suggested slower metabolism, possibly through the genotype or co-ingested CYP2D6 substrates such as cocaine and diltiazem. [f:staack2007]

In 12 healthy men the elimination half-life was 2.4-6.8 h after intravenous and 2.6-6.1 h after oral administration, and oral bioavailability ranged from 12 % to 84 %. [f:feuchtl2004]

## cuts

mCPP turns up in tablets sold as ecstasy and, in one documented case, as an adulterant of cocaine. [f:vogels2009] [f:staack2007]

## effects

mCPP acts on monoaminergic systems, resembling MDMA but mainly on serotonin; the subjective effects show subtle differences from those of MDMA. [f:bossong2005]

It is an agonist at serotonin 2C, 1A, 1B and 1D receptors. [f:feuchtl2004]

## risks

Given by mouth to healthy men, mCPP raised ACTH, cortisol and prolactin and caused anxiety, shivering, dizziness, heightened sensitivity to light and noise and fear of losing control; concentrations and responses varied widely between people. [f:feuchtl2004]

Little is known about its harmfulness, and risks common to MDMA cannot be excluded. [f:bossong2005]

## interactions

Used as an adulterant of cocaine, mCPP may interact with CYP2D6 substrates such as cocaine and diltiazem; this is a hypothesis from one documented intake, not a proven interaction. [f:staack2007]

## counselling

If the sample was sold as MDMA, explain that mCPP has been found in tablets and powders sold as ecstasy, with effects partly MDMA-like but different, and that the UBC guidelines list it among the other substances found in MDMA samples. [f:bossong2005] [f:vogels2009] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about anxiety, dizziness, shivering and sensitivity to light and noise after use: they were all seen in healthy volunteers, with large differences between people. [f:feuchtl2004]

Ask whether cocaine or other CYP2D6 substrates were taken together: a possible effect on mCPP metabolism was described in one case. [f:staack2007]
