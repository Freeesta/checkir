## molecule

Levomethorphan, the levorotatory enantiomer of methorphan (3-methoxy-17-methylmorphinan). Formula C18H25NO, 271.4 g/mol, CAS 125-70-2. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

It is the mirror image of dextromethorphan (its own sheet) with the same formula; methorphan is chiral and its enantiomers have distinct effects and toxicological profiles. Enantioselective LC-MS/MS methods tell them apart in blood and in seized material. [f:salerno2025] [f:lofaro2023]

## category

Chemical class: morphinans. Expected effect: opioid, through its metabolite levorphanol. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:lofaro2023] [f:prommer2007] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Post-mortem blood analyses confirmed cases of severe overdose with dextromethorphan, with levomethorphan, and less commonly with both. [f:lofaro2023]

All 45 heroin samples seized in north-east Italy contained methorphan: dextromethorphan in every sample, levomethorphan only at trace levels (0.21 and 0.038 mg/L) in two. [f:salerno2025]

## origin

A synthetic morphinan: the 3-methyl ether of levorphanol. [f:pubchem] [f:lofaro2023]

## metabolism

Levomethorphan is O-demethylated to levorphanol, as dextromethorphan is to dextrorphan; both metabolites are pharmacologically relevant and are measured in blood. [f:lofaro2023]

Levorphanol is glucuronidated in the liver and the glucuronides are excreted by the kidney; its long half-life favours accumulation. [f:prommer2007]
