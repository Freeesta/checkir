## molecule

5F-PB-22, quinolin-8-yl 1-(5-fluoropentyl)indole-3-carboxylate (5-fluoro-PB-22, 5F-QUPIC). Base: C23H21FN2O2, 376.4 g/mol, CAS 1400742-41-7. Identity checked against PubChem; achiral. It is the indole compound: 5F-NPB-22 has an indazole in place of the indole and is a different substance (sheet 5f-npb-22). [f:pubchem] [f:wohlfarth2014]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

5F-PB-22, with PB-22, was among the first marketed synthetic cannabinoids with a quinoline substructure and an ester bond linkage. [f:wohlfarth2014]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist (indole-3-carboxylate ester of 8-hydroxyquinoline with a fluoropentyl chain). [f:wohlfarth2014] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In human hepatocytes the predominant pathway of 5F-PB-22 was ester hydrolysis, giving a wide variety of 5-fluoropentylindole-3-carboxylic acid metabolites; 22 metabolites were identified, most generated by oxidation with or without glucuronidation. [f:wohlfarth2014]

Oxidative defluorination formed PB-22 metabolites; epoxide formation followed by internal hydrolysis and a cysteine conjugate were also observed. Proposed urine targets: 5'-fluoropentylindole-3-carboxylic acid, PB-22 pentanoic acid and the hydroxy-5F-PB-22 metabolite oxidised at the quinoline system. [f:wohlfarth2014]

## effects

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists are potent full agonists. [f:jones2025]

For ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA, full CB1 agonism explains altered perception, agitation, anxiety, paranoia, hallucinations, loss of consciousness, chest pain, hypertension, tachycardia and seizures; no data on the effects of this compound in humans were found in the sources used. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## doses

Potency varies widely among synthetic cannabinoids: ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA are up to 140 and 85 times more potent than Δ9-THC, and no potency data for this compound were found in the sources used, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## risks

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis (43.45 vs 22.91 days). [f:ricci2026]

In a HepG2 cell assay 5F-PB-22 showed strong cytotoxic potential. [f:richter2019]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
