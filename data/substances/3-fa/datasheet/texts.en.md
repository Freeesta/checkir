## molecule

3-FA, 1-(3-fluorophenyl)propan-2-amine (3-fluoroamphetamine). Base: C9H12FN, 153.20 g/mol, CAS 1626-71-7. Hydrochloride: 189 g/mol, CAS 1716-59-2, white powder. Identity checked against PubChem and Wikidata; SWGDRUG gives the diamond ATR spectrum of the hydrochloride. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:swgdrug-3fa]

Positional isomers: 2-FA, 3-FA and 4-FA have the same formula and differ only in the position of the fluorine. For the out-of-plane C-H bending band of the ring, meta-disubstituted rings absorb at 810-750 cm-1, with a ring bend near 690 cm-1; the full spectrum must be compared with the reference one. [f:smith-benzene] [f:swgdrug-3fa]

Isomer identification: in LC-MS/MS casework 2-FA and 4-FA are told apart from 3-FA by retention time and ion ratio, and infrared ion spectroscopy coupled to mass spectrometry has been shown to separate the three isomers. [f:johansen2012] [f:kranenburg2020]

## counselling

Ask which fluoroamphetamine the person expects: 2-FA, 3-FA and 4-FA are positional isomers, and 3-FA and 4-FA co-elute in LC-MS/MS and need the ion ratio to be told apart; 4-FA also inhibits the serotonin transporter more than amphetamine does, and the UBC guidelines describe entactogenic effects only for 4-FA, so the three should not be treated as interchangeable. [f:johansen2012] [f:luethi2019] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about other substances and medicines: the UBC guidelines advise against combining with alcohol and other stimulants because the effects of interactions are practically unknown; for 4-FA, the closest studied isomer, a review anticipates pharmacodynamic interactions with ADHD medication and antidepressants. [f:inan2020] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
