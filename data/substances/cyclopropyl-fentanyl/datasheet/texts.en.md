## molecule

Cyclopropylfentanyl, N-phenyl-N-[1-(2-phenylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]cyclopropanecarboxamide. Base: C23H28N2O, 348.5 g/mol, CAS 1169-68-2. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Cyclopropylfentanyl was first reported to the EU Early Warning System in August 2017 and was linked to more than 100 deaths in the US alone. [f:cutler2019]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a fentanyl derivative with an N-cyclopropanecarbonyl group. [f:cutler2019]

## metabolism

In human liver microsomes eleven metabolites were identified, the major ones formed by N-dealkylation, monohydroxylation and N-oxidation; the N-dealkylated nor-metabolite was the major one in vitro and in blood from authentic cases. [f:cutler2019]

In post-mortem urine norcyclopropylfentanyl was the most abundant metabolite (12 of 13 samples); 4-hydroxyphenethyl, 3,4-dihydroxyphenethyl and 4-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenethyl cyclopropylfentanyl are suitable urinary markers, and apart from the nor-metabolite 40% to 90% of the metabolites were conjugated. [f:vikingsson2019]

In hepatocytes β-hydroxylation gave the major hydroxylated metabolite. [f:wallgren2021]

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

## doses

The potency of fentanyl analogues varies widely between compounds, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lovrecic2019]

## risks

In poisonings with fentanyl analogues larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
