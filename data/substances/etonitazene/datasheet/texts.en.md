## molecule

Etonitazene, 2-[2-[(4-ethoxyphenyl)methyl]-5-nitrobenzimidazol-1-yl]-N,N-diethylethanamine. Base: C22H28N4O3, 396.5 g/mol, CAS 911-65-9. Identity checked against PubChem and ChEBI. [f:pubchem] [f:chebi]

## category

Chemical class: nitazenes (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Etonitazene is the prototypical drug of a series of 2-benzylbenzimidazole analgesics made in the late 1950s by the Swiss company CIBA; in a mouse tail-flick assay its antinociceptive potency was 1000-fold higher than that of morphine, and the series was never marketed, possibly because of a high risk of adverse effects. [f:vandeputte2024]

Apart from occasional identifications between 1966 and 2003, no nitazene appeared on the recreational drug market until 2019; etonitazene is among the nitazenes now under international control. [f:vandeputte2024]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a 2-benzylbenzimidazole 'nitazene' opioid. [f:vandeputte2024]

## metabolism

4'-hydroxy nitazene is described as a metabolite common to all nitazenes that differ only in the alkoxy chain, a group that includes etonitazene (ethoxy chain). [f:vandeputte2024]

## effects

Nitazenes are agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; in vitro their affinity and potency often exceed those of morphine and of fentanyl, and the real-world potency reported is often lower than the experimental one. [f:stangeland2025]

## doses

For the nitazenes, potency in vitro is very high and varies widely between compounds, while data on potency in humans are scarce, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:stangeland2025]

## risks

In two in vitro μ-opioid receptor assays etonitazene was among the most potent nitazenes tested, with EC50 0.509 nM and 0.0398 nM, against 327 and 13.2 nM for morphine and 25.7 and 2.20 nM for fentanyl in the same assays. [f:vandeputte2024]

Nitazene poisonings often involve other opioids and sedating drugs; naloxone reversed the poisoning (median parenteral dose 1.20 mg), but some patients needed prolonged naloxone infusions, so longer observation may be needed. [f:stangeland2025]

## interactions

In poisoning cases several opioids and other sedatives were present, and polysubstance use complicates diagnosis and management. [f:stangeland2025]
