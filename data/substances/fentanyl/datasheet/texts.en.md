## molecule

Fentanyl, N-phenyl-N-[1-(2-phenylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]propanamide. Base: C22H28N2O, 336.5 g/mol, CAS 437-38-7. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Fentanyl was introduced more than 50 years ago and became the opioid most used for analgesia during surgery; a patch for chronic pain has been available since the early 1990s, followed by rapid-onset transmucosal products for breakthrough pain. [f:stanley2014]

Until 2013 there were sporadic outbreaks of fentanyl in the heroin supply; since then it has caused deaths in every US state, and in 2014 the first illicit pills containing it were detected. [f:armenian2018]

## origin

Fentanyl is a synthetic opioid. Its analogues and other novel synthetic opioids have reappeared on the drug market in recent years. [f:armenian2018] [f:lovrecic2019]

## metabolism

Fentanyl is highly lipophilic: volume of distribution at steady state 287 ± 79 L, terminal half-life 425 ± 102 minutes and clearance 0.67 ± 0.15 L/min after intravenous dosing (12 volunteers); 84.4% is bound to proteins in human plasma (in vitro). [f:streisand1991] [f:meuldermans1982]

In human liver microsomes the only major metabolite is norfentanyl, formed by N-dealkylation mainly through CYP3A4 (ketoconazole and erythromycin inhibited it by 90% or more, in vitro). [f:feierman1996]

The label reports about 75% of an intravenous dose in urine, mostly as metabolites, and about 9% in faeces; its terminal half-life is 219 minutes. [f:fentanyl-injection-label]

Bioavailability in 12 volunteers: 0.52 oral transmucosal, 0.32 swallowed; the swallowed dose peaked later (101 against 22 minutes). [f:streisand1991]

In 12 volunteers ritonavir (CYP3A4 inhibitor) cut fentanyl clearance by 67%. [f:olkkola1999] Rifampicin lowered and troleandomycin raised the exposure to oral transmucosal fentanyl. [f:kharasch2004]

## cuts

Xylazine, a veterinary sedative, has been reported as an adulterant of illicit fentanyl. [f:marshall2025]

## effects

Fentanyl is a full agonist at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. In vitro its binding constant (Ki) at the recombinant human μ receptor lies between 1 and 100 nM, in the same band as morphine. [f:armenian2018] [f:volpe2011]

Intravenous onset is almost immediate; the analgesic effect lasts 30-60 minutes after a single dose of up to 100 µg because the drug redistributes. [f:fentanyl-injection-label]

Fentanyl also causes muscle and chest-wall rigidity: in 12 volunteers given 15 µg/kg intravenously, 6 developed rigidity and apnoea and were unresponsive, starting at 16-28 ng/mL. [f:streisand1993]

Effects reported for the novel synthetic opioids also include sedation, hypothermia and inhibition of gastrointestinal propulsion. [f:lovrecic2019]

## doses

Fentanyl is about 50–100 times more potent than morphine, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lovrecic2019]

Medical reference points, not street doses: 100 µg of the injectable form is about equivalent in analgesia to 10 mg of morphine; a buccal tablet was 46 times as potent as intravenous morphine (95% CI 17.6-575.3) in 33 evaluable volunteers. [f:fentanyl-injection-label] [f:saunders2011]

## risks

Respiratory depression is the main danger; in poisonings with fentanyl and its analogues larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

Fentanyl has been found mixed into products sold as heroin and in counterfeit pills that look like known opioids or benzodiazepines, so people can take it without knowing. [f:armenian2018] [f:lovrecic2019]

In 48 deaths from illicit fentanyl norfentanyl was not detectable in 20, which the authors read as very rapid death, possibly from chest-wall rigidity after fast intravenous use; some deaths had levels of 1-2 ng/mL in people who were apparently not opioid-naive. [f:burns2016]

In the United States fentanyl has been recognised as a major contributor to the rise in opioid overdose deaths since 2013. [f:kerensky2017]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines, alcohol and other CNS depressants with opioids may cause profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma and death. [f:fentanyl-injection-label] [f:kerensky2017]

Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors raise fentanyl levels: ritonavir cut clearance by 67% in volunteers. Inducers such as rifampicin lower them. [f:olkkola1999] [f:kharasch2004]

In a systematic review fentanyl was among the opioids that inhibit HERG channels and prolong the QTc interval; morphine, codeine and buprenorphine were not. [f:elsherbini2023]

Sustained high buprenorphine concentrations reduced the fall in ventilation caused by fentanyl boluses (14 healthy volunteers and 8 opioid-tolerant patients). [f:moss2022]

## counselling

Ask what amount the person expects: fentanyl is 50–100 times more potent than morphine, tiny amounts can be fatal, and the amount used for another opioid cannot be carried over. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain the signs of an overdose (miosis, respiratory and central nervous system depression) and that naloxone should be at hand: larger doses than for heroin may be needed, and emergency services should be called. [f:armenian2018] [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about the route: given its potency [f:lovrecic2019], the UBC guidelines advise against injecting fentanyl, because the gap between the wanted effect and an overdose is hard to judge, and recommend sterile equipment that is never shared. [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask how long it has been since any opioid was last used: tolerance is lost after breaks, and amounts taken by regular users can be fatal for those who are not used to them. [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about benzodiazepines, other sedatives and pills of unknown content: counterfeit pills can contain fentanyl and look like benzodiazepines or opioids. [f:kerensky2017] [f:armenian2018] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
