## origin

Fentanyl is a synthetic opioid. Its analogues and other novel synthetic opioids have reappeared on the drug market in recent years. [f:armenian2018] [f:lovrecic2019]

## metabolism

Fentanyl is metabolised by CYP3A4; norfentanyl is the metabolite measured in plasma. [f:kharasch2004]

In 12 volunteers given oral transmucosal fentanyl, rifampicin (induction of CYP3A) lowered the area under the plasma curve (2.2 against 5.9 h·ng/ml in controls) and troleandomycin (inhibition of CYP3A) raised it (10.4 h·ng/ml); peak concentration and time to peak did not change. [f:kharasch2004]

## effects

Fentanyl, like its analogues, is a full agonist at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

Effects reported for the novel synthetic opioids also include sedation, hypothermia and inhibition of gastrointestinal propulsion. [f:lovrecic2019]

## doses

Fentanyl is about 50–100 times more potent than morphine, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lovrecic2019]

## risks

Respiratory depression is the main danger; in poisonings with fentanyl and its analogues larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

Fentanyl has been found mixed into products sold as heroin and in counterfeit pills that look like known opioids or benzodiazepines, so people can take it without knowing. [f:armenian2018] [f:lovrecic2019]

In the United States fentanyl has been recognised as a major contributor to the rise in opioid overdose deaths since 2013. [f:kerensky2017]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]

Drugs that inhibit or induce CYP3A change fentanyl exposure (see metabolism). [f:kharasch2004]

In a systematic review fentanyl was among the opioids that inhibit HERG channels and prolong the QTc interval; morphine, codeine and buprenorphine were not. [f:elsherbini2023]

## counselling

Ask what amount the person expects: fentanyl is 50–100 times more potent than morphine, tiny amounts can be fatal, and the amount used for another opioid cannot be carried over. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain the signs of an overdose (miosis, respiratory and central nervous system depression) and that naloxone should be at hand: larger doses than for heroin may be needed, and emergency services should be called. [f:armenian2018] [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about the route: given its potency [f:lovrecic2019], the UBC guidelines advise against injecting fentanyl, because the gap between the wanted effect and an overdose is hard to judge, and recommend sterile equipment that is never shared. [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask how long it has been since any opioid was last used: tolerance is lost after breaks, and amounts taken by regular users can be fatal for those who are not used to them. [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about benzodiazepines, other sedatives and pills of unknown content: counterfeit pills can contain fentanyl and look like benzodiazepines or opioids. [f:kerensky2017] [f:armenian2018] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
