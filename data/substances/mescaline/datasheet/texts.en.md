## molecule

Mescaline, 2-(3,4,5-trimethoxyphenyl)ethanamine. Base: C11H17NO3, 211.26 g/mol, CAS 54-04-6. Identity checked on PubChem. [f:pubchem]

Structure: a primary amine on an ethyl chain attached to a ring with three methoxy groups. Expected bands in the free base: N-H of the primary amine at 3250-3510 cm-1 and aryl ether C-O at 1190-1300 cm-1. There is no reference spectrum of mescaline in the public data, so these ranges only indicate the groups. [f:coates2000] [f:smith-nitrogen] [f:smith-ethers]

Forms: extracted mescaline is a powder, mostly as sulfate or hydrochloride and more rarely as citrate or acetate. [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

## category

Chemical class: phenethylamines. Expected effect: psychedelic. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Classic psychedelics have been used by various cultures for millennia for healing and religious purposes. [f:nichols2025]

The modern era of psychedelic science began with the experiments of Arthur Heffter in 1898, who identified the active alkaloid of the peyote cactus and named it mescaline. [f:nichols2025]

## origin

Natural: mescaline is the active alkaloid of the peyote cactus. The UBC guidelines also state that it is found in Trichocereus cacti (T. pachanoi, T. bridgesii, T. peruvianus) and other succulents. [f:nichols2025] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

## metabolism

Over 24-30 hours 53% of an oral dose was excreted in urine unchanged and 31% as the main metabolite, 3,4,5-trimethoxyphenylacetic acid. The oral bioavailability is at least 53%, limited by first-pass metabolism. [f:mueller2025]

The plasma half-life is about 3.5 hours; the peak concentration is reached within about 2 hours and exposure is proportional to the dose. [f:mueller2025] [f:klaiber2024]

## effects

In 16 healthy subjects mescaline above 100 mg induced dose-dependent subjective effects; their average duration rose from 6.4 hours at 100 mg to 14 hours at 800 mg. [f:klaiber2024]

Modelling gave an onset of any drug effect at about 1 hour; from 100 to 800 mg the maximum effect rose from 13% to 89% and the duration from 2.8 to 15 hours. [f:mueller2025]

Ketanserin, a 5-HT2A receptor antagonist, attenuated and shortened the effects of 800 mg mescaline, which suggests that they are mediated mainly by that receptor. [f:klaiber2024]

## doses

Doses in the trial: 100, 200, 400 and 800 mg by mouth. In the pharmacokinetic analysis, 105 administrations of 100-800 mg were studied in 49 participants. [f:klaiber2024] [f:mueller2025]

The UBC guidelines say that effects are noticeable from about 200-400 mg, or 3-5 mg per kg of body weight; in the trial, effects were dose-dependent above 100 mg. [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines] [f:klaiber2024]

## risks

Mescaline raised systolic and diastolic blood pressure at doses above 100 mg, with no difference between 200 and 800 mg, and raised heart rate in a dose-dependent way. [f:klaiber2024]

Nausea and vomiting were frequent at 800 mg and tolerability was lower at the highest doses; the subjective response showed no ceiling between 100 and 800 mg. [f:klaiber2024]

Hallucinogens are relatively well tolerated physiologically and are not considered drugs of dependence, but they carry unique psychological risks; the most likely is overwhelming distress during drug action. [f:johnson2008]

## interactions

Ketanserin (40 mg) given with 800 mg of mescaline attenuated and shortened its effects, which became comparable to those of 100 and 200 mg. [f:klaiber2024]

## counselling

Ask about food: nausea and vomiting were frequent at 800 mg in the trial; the UBC guidelines suggest fasting beforehand and small doses to reduce nausea, and warn of a high choking risk if vomiting. [f:klaiber2024] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Say that the effects are long and grow with the dose, from 6.4 hours at 100 mg to 14 hours at 800 mg; the onset is slow, and the UBC guidelines advise not raising the dose while waiting. [f:klaiber2024] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about heart, circulation and psychiatric conditions: blood pressure rose above 100 mg, and the UBC guidelines advise people with mental illness, especially those taking neuroleptics, or heart or circulation problems to abstain. [f:klaiber2024] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about experience and setting: the UBC guidelines say mescaline is not a party drug and is meant for people who already know other psychedelics, in a place where they feel at ease and with trusted company. [f:johnson2008] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
