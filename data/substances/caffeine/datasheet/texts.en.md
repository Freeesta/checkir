## molecule

Caffeine (1,3,7-trimethylpurine-2,6-dione), formula C8H10N4O2, molar mass 194.19 g/mol, CAS 58-08-2. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI, which agree on formula and InChIKey. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

## category

Chemical class: xanthine alkaloid (grouped here under other substances). Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: pharmacological adulterant, also found as a declared ingredient. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:checkir-classification]

## origin

Caffeine is found in coffee, tea, soft drinks, cocoa and chocolate products and in medications. [f:nawrot2003]

## metabolism

Caffeine is almost exclusively metabolised in the liver. The main product is paraxanthine, formed only by CYP1A2 (about 80-90 % of the demethylation); theobromine and theophylline are minor products (about 11 % and 4 %). [f:grzegorzewski2022]

Smoking increases the clearance of caffeine and shortens its elimination half-life. [f:grzegorzewski2022]

## cuts

Caffeine is on the list of the frequent cuts of cocaine. [f:unodc-cocaine2012]

Internal guidelines of a drug checking service describe caffeine as an active cut: a stimulant that adds an effect the person can feel. They also name it among the substances found in speed and in tablets sold as MDMA. [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

## risks

In dietary use, a review of the literature on healthy adults concluded that intake up to 400 mg per day is not associated with general toxicity or with cardiovascular, bone, behavioural or male fertility effects. This describes a known dietary amount and does not describe an unknown amount in a sample. [f:nawrot2003]

The same review identified women of reproductive age and children as subgroups at risk who may need specific advice, with suggested limits of 300 mg per day and 2.5 mg/kg of body weight per day. [f:nawrot2003]

## interactions

Fluvoxamine, an antidepressant that inhibits the enzyme CYP1A2, greatly reduced the clearance of caffeine in healthy volunteers given a single dose; the study found no change in caffeine's effects but could not rule out adverse effects from accumulation with daily intake. [f:culm-merdek2005]
