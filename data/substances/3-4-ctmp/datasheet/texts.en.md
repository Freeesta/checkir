## molecule

3,4-Dichloromethylphenidate (3,4-CTMP), methyl 2-(3,4-dichlorophenyl)-2-piperidin-2-ylacetate. Base: C14H17Cl2NO2, 302.2 g/mol, CAS 1400742-68-8. Identity checked against PubChem (entry without stereochemistry; the named-compound entries carry specific stereoisomers). [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: phenidates (UNODC grouping), a methylphenidate analogue and not a cathinone, so no cathinone family sheet is attached. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:davidson2018] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

3,4-CTMP appeared on the UK drug market in 2013, as a new psychoactive substance analogue of methylphenidate. [f:davidson2018]

## origin

A synthetic substance, an analogue of methylphenidate. [f:davidson2018]

## metabolism

In rat urine seven phase I metabolites and no phase II metabolites of 3,4-CTMP were found; in pooled human S9 fraction the compound was mainly hydrolysed to the carboxy metabolite, proposed as an additional analytical target. [f:manier2020]

## effects

In rat brain slices 3,4-CTMP increased evoked dopamine efflux about 6-fold and noradrenaline efflux about 2-fold, an effect on dopamine greater than that of methylphenidate. [f:davidson2018]
