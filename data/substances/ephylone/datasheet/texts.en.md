## molecule

Ephylone (N-ethylpentylone), 1-(1,3-benzodioxol-5-yl)-2-(ethylamino)pentan-1-one. Base: C14H19NO3, 249.30 g/mol, CAS 727641-67-0. Identity checked against PubChem and Wikidata. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata]

Hydrochloride: C14H19NO3·HCl, 285.77 g/mol, off-white powder, CAS 17763-02-9; the monograph gives no melting point. The reference spectrum is a diamond ATR one (1 bounce, 4 cm-1 resolution). [f:swgdrug-ephylone]

Isomers: N,N-dimethylpentylone (dipentylone) may be mistaken for N-ethylpentylone, and other isomers exist (N-isopropylbutylone, hexylone, diethylone, tertylone); the method used must be able to tell them apart. [f:fogarty2023]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cathinones. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

N-ethylpentylone was described in 2018 as the most recent novel stimulant to emerge among the new psychoactive substances; fatal cases have been published since 2017. [f:krotulski2018] [f:thirakul2017]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a ring-substituted cathinone with a methylenedioxy group. [f:costa2019]

## metabolism

In human liver microsomes and in blood and oral fluid, four metabolites were confirmed; the reduction of the beta-ketone to an alcohol gave the most prominent one, specific to N-ethylpentylone, and it is proposed as a marker of ingestion. [f:krotulski2018]

## effects

In vitro N-ethylpentylone strongly inhibits the dopamine (IC50 37 nM), noradrenaline (105 nM) and serotonin (383 nM) transporters and does not release neurotransmitters: it acts by blocking, mainly dopamine and noradrenaline uptake. [f:costa2019]

## risks

In 26 blood samples from death investigations or drugged-driving cases the concentrations were 12-1200 ng/mL (median 125 ng/mL), apart from one case at 50000 ng/mL; N-ethylpentylone was often found with other drugs, including fentanyl analogues. [f:krotulski2018]

In clinical cases (7-170 ng/mL) the symptoms included palpitations, tachycardia, agitation, hallucinations, coma and death. [f:costa2019]

In one fatality the body temperature was 105.5 °F and the blood pH 6.7, with rhabdomyolysis, liver and kidney injury and disseminated intravascular coagulation; death came about 36 h after admission. [f:thirakul2017]

## counselling

Say that a result for this substance needs a reference or a method that separates its isomers: dipentylone and other isomers can be mistaken for it. [f:fogarty2023]

Ask about other drugs on the same night: in the published cases it was often found with other drugs, including fentanyl analogues; the UBC guidelines advise against mixing stimulants and against alcohol. [f:krotulski2018] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Signs to recognise: palpitations, fast heartbeat, agitation, hallucinations, very high temperature, coma; the UBC guidelines say to cool the person and seek help for hyperthermia. [f:costa2019] [f:thirakul2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
