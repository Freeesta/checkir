## molecule

Bromazolam, 8-bromo-1-methyl-6-phenyl-4H-[1,2,4]triazolo[4,3-a][1,4]benzodiazepine. Formula C17H13BrN4, 353.2 g/mol. Identity checked on PubChem. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: benzodiazepines. Expected effect: central nervous system depressant (sedative). Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. It is a designer benzodiazepine (a non-pharmaceutical triazolobenzodiazepine). [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

In San Francisco bromazolam-related deaths rose to 44 in 2023, while deaths related to other novel designer benzodiazepines were relatively few. [f:rodda2024]

## effects

Bromazolam is described as a novel designer benzodiazepine with potent sedative, hypnotic and anxiolytic effects. [f:rodda2024]

## risks

In bromazolam deaths in San Francisco (2020-2023) co-ingestion with opioids, mainly fentanyl, and with stimulants such as methamphetamine and cocaine was frequent; blood concentrations rose over the period. [f:rodda2024]

In a postmortem case of a 24-year-old man bromazolam was 352 ng/mL in blood, together with fentanyl (11 ng/mL) and desalkylgidazepam; it is a single case, not a threshold. [f:ballotari2024]

## interactions

Bromazolam was found together with opioids in many deaths, and for benzodiazepines in general concurrent use with an opioid is associated with more opioid overdoses. [f:rodda2024] [f:sun2017]

## counselling

Ask whether the person also takes opioids, fentanyl or stimulants: the combination was frequent in bromazolam deaths; the UBC guidelines say benzodiazepines can depress breathing. [f:rodda2024] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain that bromazolam may not be part of routine testing, so intoxication cases are under-reported. [f:rodda2024]
