## molecule

APP-BINACA (APP-BUTINACA), N-[(2S)-1-amino-1-oxo-3-phenylpropan-2-yl]-1-butylindazole-3-carboxamide. Base: C21H24N4O2, 364.4 g/mol, CAS 2682867-56-5. Identity checked on PubChem. Synthetic cannabinoid with an indazole core, a butyl chain and a phenylalaninamide head; one chiral centre (S). [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

APP-BINACA (or APP-BUTINACA) was first reported in Europe in January 2019 and in the United States in March 2019. [f:krotulski2019-app-binaca]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cannabinoid (indazole-3-carboxamide with a butyl chain and a phenylalaninamide head). [f:krotulski2019-app-binaca] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

Five metabolites were identified in biological specimens, including 4-HO-APP-BINACA and APP-BINACA 3-phenylpropanoic acid. [f:krotulski2019-app-binaca]

## effects

In a study of nine synthetic cannabinoids, all of them, APP-BINACA included, activated CB1 and CB2 receptors, caused hypothermia in mice and fully substituted for Δ9-THC in drug discrimination; APP-BINACA was efficacious but less potent than most past synthetic cannabinoids. [f:marusich2022]

## doses

The sources used have no dose data in humans; the lower potency seen in mice does not allow a dose to be inferred. [f:marusich2022]

## risks

APP-BINACA was identified in 11 forensic toxicology cases from five US states, in both death investigations and drug-impaired driving investigations, and was named as a possible contributory factor in adverse events, including death. [f:krotulski2019-app-binaca]

## interactions

In the forensic cases APP-BINACA was commonly found together with 4F-MDMB-BINACA. [f:krotulski2019-app-binaca]

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids increases the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
