## molecule

5-Fluoro-EDMB-PINACA, ethyl (2S)-2-[[1-(5-fluoropentyl)indazole-3-carbonyl]amino]-3,3-dimethylbutanoate. Base: C21H30FN3O3, 391.5 g/mol, CAS 2504100-69-8. Identity checked against PubChem (entry with the (S) stereocentre). It is the ethyl ester of an indazole-3-carboxamide of tert-leucine; the indole analogue is 5F-EDMB-PICA. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cannabinoid of the indazole-3-carboxamide type with a tert-leucine ethyl ester group and a 5-fluoropentyl chain. [f:pubchem] [f:liu2017-edmb-pinaca]

## metabolism

In human liver microsomes with ethanol, 5F-MDMB-PINACA is transesterified by carboxylesterase to 5F-EDMB-PINACA, which is hydrolysed to the same acid as its parent and oxidised to metabolites with the ethyl ester intact; its oxidative defluorination metabolite was proposed as a biomarker of 5F-MDMB-PINACA and alcohol co-use. [f:wang2023-transesterification]

## effects

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists are potent full agonists. [f:jones2025]

5F-EDMB-PINACA bound to and activated CB1 and CB2 receptors with high affinity, produced hypothermia in mice and fully substituted for Δ9-THC in drug discrimination. [f:marusich2022]

## doses

No human dose data for this compound were found in the sources used, and potency varies widely among synthetic cannabinoids (ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA are reported up to 140 and 85 times as potent as Δ9-THC), so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## risks

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

5F-EDMB-PINACA was among three synthetic cannabinoids found in US blood extracts and previously unreported in US forensic toxicology casework. [f:krotulski2020]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis. [f:ricci2026]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
