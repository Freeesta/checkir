## molecule

Salvinorin A, C23H28O8, 432.5 g/mol, CAS 83729-01-5. Identity checked on PubChem. [f:pubchem]

Structure: a terpene with a furan ring, a lactone, a methyl ester, an acetate and a ketone; with no nitrogen it is not an alkaloid and forms no salts. C=O absorptions are expected for the carbonyl groups; there is no verified reference spectrum here and the band only indicates the type of group. [f:pubchem] [f:maqueda2016] [f:smith-carbonyl] [f:smith-esters]

## category

Chemical class: plant-based substance. Expected effect: psychedelic, but through kappa opioid receptor agonism, not 5-HT2A. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:checkir-classification] [f:maqueda2016]

## history

Salvia divinorum has been used for centuries for its psychomimetic effects in religious rituals; it has become popular mainly with adolescents and young adults as a «legal high» bought on the internet. [f:mahendran2015]

## origin

Natural product: it is the main psychoactive component of Salvia divinorum, a Mexican plant. [f:tsujikawa2009]

It is found in the leaves of the plant; the UBC guidelines also describe extracts of different concentrations (x5, x10). [f:maqueda2016] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

## metabolism

In vitro, in rat plasma salvinorin A is degraded mainly by carboxylesterases; the products include the deacetylated form (salvinorin B) and forms with the lactone ring open. The study has no human data. [f:tsujikawa2009]

In humans, after inhalation of vaporised salvinorin A, plasma levels peak at 1–2 minutes and then fall rapidly. [f:johnson2016] [f:maqueda2016]

## effects

It is a kappa opioid receptor agonist: in humans it induces an intense but short-lasting altered state of awareness. [f:maqueda2016]

Inhaled as vapour, it markedly reduced external sensory perception, with intense visual and auditory modifications, raised systolic blood pressure and cortisol and prolactin release; naltrexone blocked these effects, ketanserin did not. [f:maqueda2016]

Effects rated by the participants and by the monitor were correlated with plasma levels. [f:johnson2016]

## doses

Laboratory studies in healthy adults used inhaled doses of vaporised salvinorin A of 1 mg [f:maqueda2016] and of 18–21 mcg/kg, with peak plasma levels after 1–2 minutes. [f:johnson2016]

## risks

It is believed to have low toxicity and low addictive potential, but its short- and long-term effects have not been examined in enough detail. [f:mahendran2015]

It can precipitate psychiatric symptoms and worsen cognition. [f:mahendran2015]

An association between Salvia divinorum use and psychotic disorders has been proposed, based on a review and two case reports; more research is needed. [f:elkhoury2015]

For hallucinogens in general, psychomimetic effects are usually transient and end with the action of the drug, but sometimes they persist. [f:elkhoury2015]

## interactions

Naltrexone (an opioid antagonist) blocked the subjective, cardiovascular and neuroendocrine effects of salvinorin A; ketanserin (a 5-HT2A antagonist) did not. [f:maqueda2016]

## counselling

Ask where and with whom the person plans to use it: it strongly reduces the perception of the surroundings, and the UBC guidelines recommend a familiar place, trusted people and sitting or lying down to reduce the risk of falls. [f:maqueda2016] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about psychiatric history: a review reports that it can precipitate psychiatric symptoms and an association with psychosis has been proposed; the UBC guidelines advise against use by people with psychiatric illness or heart or circulation problems (systolic pressure rises). [f:mahendran2015] [f:elkhoury2015] [f:maqueda2016] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain that, when inhaled, the plasma peak arrives in about 2 minutes and then falls rapidly, and that with extracts the amount has to be scaled to the concentration (x5, x10). [f:johnson2016] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
