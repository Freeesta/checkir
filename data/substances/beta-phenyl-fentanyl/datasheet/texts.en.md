## molecule

β'-Phenylfentanyl (beta'-phenyl fentanyl), N,3-diphenyl-N-[1-(2-phenylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]propanamide. Base: C28H32N2O, 412.6 g/mol, CAS 79279-02-0. Identity checked against PubChem. The name refers to the 3-phenylpropanoyl (phenyl on the second carbon of the acyl chain) analogue, also called 3-phenylpropanoylfentanyl. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

## doses

The potency of fentanyl analogues varies widely between compounds, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lovrecic2019]

## risks

In mice β'-phenylfentanyl produced dose-dependent antinociception, with an ED50 of 19.4 mg/kg against 0.08 mg/kg for fentanyl and 7.82 mg/kg for morphine; naltrexone raised its ED50 21.0-fold, so the effect is mediated at least in part by the μ-opioid receptor. [f:varshneya2021]

In poisonings with fentanyl analogues larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
