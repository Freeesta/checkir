## molecule

MMB-CHMICA, methyl (2S)-2-[[1-(cyclohexylmethyl)indole-3-carbonyl]amino]-3-methylbutanoate. Base: C22H30N2O3, 370.5 g/mol, CAS 1971007-94-9. Identity checked against PubChem (the (S) entry). Same compound as AMB-CHMICA; it is the valine-derived analogue of MDMB-CHMICA. [f:pubchem] [f:fabregat2022]

## category

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist (indole-3-carboxamide of a valine-derived methyl ester with a cyclohexylmethyl group); AMB-CHMICA was tentatively identified by GC-MS in seized herbal material. [f:antonides2019] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In pooled human hepatocytes the main metabolic pathway of AMB-CHMICA was O-demethylation of the ester and hydroxylation of the methylhexyl moiety (the authors' wording for the cyclohexylmethyl group). [f:mardal2018-amb-chmica]

In rats, O-demethylation also occurred; the parent compound and the O-demethyl metabolites accumulated in the liver and were still detected there 48 h after injection. [f:fabregat2022]

With recombinant human carboxylesterases, ester hydrolysis of MMB-CHMICA was detected and followed Michaelis-Menten kinetics, as for the other valine-derived compounds tested. [f:wagmann2022-hces]

## effects

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists are potent full agonists. [f:jones2025]

For ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA, full CB1 agonism explains altered perception, agitation, anxiety, paranoia, hallucinations, loss of consciousness, chest pain, hypertension, tachycardia and seizures; no data on the effects of this compound in humans were found in the sources used. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## doses

Potency varies widely among synthetic cannabinoids: ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA are up to 140 and 85 times more potent than Δ9-THC, and no potency data for this compound were found in the sources used, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## risks

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis (43.45 vs 22.91 days). [f:ricci2026]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
