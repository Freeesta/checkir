## origin

Buprenorphine is a semi-synthetic opioid derived from thebaine, a naturally occurring alkaloid of the opium poppy. [f:elkader2005]

## metabolism

Buprenorphine undergoes extensive first-pass metabolism, so its oral bioavailability is very low, while the sublingual one is extensive. It is metabolised by N-dealkylation to norbuprenorphine, mainly by CYP3A4. [f:elkader2005]

The terminal half-life is long and variable (mean values from 3 to 44 hours); most of a dose is eliminated in the faeces and about 10–30% in the urine. [f:elkader2005]

## effects

Buprenorphine is a partial agonist at the μ-opioid receptor with high affinity and low intrinsic activity; it shows no ceiling effect for analgesia but a ceiling for respiratory depression and euphoria. [f:wong2023]

## risks

In Rhode Island (2016–2018) 29 of 534 opioid-involved deaths (5.4%) had buprenorphine on toxicology; all had other co-exposures, most often fentanyl (75.9%) and ethanol (31.0%). The authors suggest buprenorphine may mitigate the risk of full-agonist overdose, with limited data. [f:wightman2020]

Buprenorphine crosses the placenta and passes into breast milk; severe chronic liver disease may alter its metabolism. [f:elkader2005]

## interactions

The interaction with benzodiazepines is more likely pharmacodynamic (additive or synergistic) than pharmacokinetic. [f:elkader2005]

Drugs that inhibit or induce CYP3A4 can reduce or increase its N-dealkylation; the evidence is limited. [f:elkader2005]

## counselling

Ask how the person takes it: the oral bioavailability is very low and the sublingual one is extensive, so swallowing instead of holding it under the tongue changes the effect. [f:elkader2005]

Ask about other substances: buprenorphine has a ceiling on respiratory depression, yet opioid deaths with buprenorphine on toxicology all involved other substances, most often fentanyl and ethanol. [f:wightman2020] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain that the half-life is long and variable (3–44 hours in the mean values reported), so ask how much time has passed before any further dose. [f:elkader2005]

Ask about benzodiazepines and talk about naloxone: the interaction with benzodiazepines is mainly additive, and naloxone has no effect in someone who has taken no opioid. [f:elkader2005] [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
