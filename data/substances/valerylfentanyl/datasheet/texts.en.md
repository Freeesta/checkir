## molecule

Valerylfentanyl, N-phenyl-N-[1-(2-phenylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]pentanamide. Base: C24H32N2O, 364.5 g/mol, CAS 122882-90-0. Identity checked against PubChem (one entry, no stereocentres). The N-acyl group is the unbranched pentanoyl (valeryl) group; the isovaleryl and pivaloyl isomers are separate substances. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a fentanyl analogue with an N-pentanoyl (valeryl) group in place of the propanoyl group. [f:pubchem] [f:armenian2018]

## metabolism

In human liver microsomes nineteen metabolites of valerylfentanyl were detected, with N-dealkylation (valeryl norfentanyl) and hydroxylation as the major pathways; 4-ANPP was also detected, and the major metabolites were also found in zebrafish larvae. [f:cooman2021]

Across fentanyl analogues with longer N-acyl chains the half-life in human liver microsomes became shorter, and metabolites hydroxylated on the acyl chain and carboxylated metabolites increased. [f:murakami2026]

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are generally full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

In vitro valerylfentanyl was a partial agonist at the μ-opioid receptor (EC50 179.8 nM, Emax 60%; fentanyl: EC50 10.3 nM, Emax 113%); in mice it still produced naloxone-reversible antinociception and respiratory suppression. [f:xie2023]

## doses

Potency varies widely between fentanyl analogues: in mice valerylfentanyl was about 100 times less potent than cyclopropylfentanyl as an antinociceptive (ED50 4.0 versus 0.04 mg/kg s.c.), which does not allow a dose to be suggested. [f:xie2023] [f:lovrecic2019]

## risks

In poisonings with fentanyl analogues larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

As soon as a compound is regulated new ones appear, which makes detection difficult; some illicit pills with these compounds look like known opioids or benzodiazepines. [f:armenian2018]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
