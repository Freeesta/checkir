## molecule

Paracetamol (N-(4-hydroxyphenyl)acetamide), formula C8H9NO2, molar mass 151.16 g/mol, CAS 103-90-2. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI, which agree on formula and InChIKey. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

## category

Chemical class: analgesic (non-opioid, grouped under non-psychoactive substances). Expected effect: no psychoactive effect expected. Typical role in samples: pharmacological adulterant. [f:checkir-classification]

## metabolism

Most of a therapeutic amount is converted to inactive glucuronide (52-57 % of the urinary metabolites) and to sulfate (30-44 %). A minor fraction (5-10 %) is oxidised, mainly by CYP2E1 and CYP1A2, to the reactive metabolite NAPQI, which is neutralised by glutathione. [f:mazaleuskaya2015]

The plasma half-life is 1.5-2.5 hours at recommended doses and 4-8 hours after an overdose. Above the therapeutic range the sulfation pathway saturates first; after a highly toxic dose glucuronidation saturates as well. [f:mazaleuskaya2015]

## cuts

Paracetamol is a frequent adulterant of street heroin. In 415 retail samples seized in Vienna in 1999 all contained a diluent, mainly lactose, and adulterants such as caffeine and/or paracetamol. [f:risser2007]

In 962 heroin samples seized in Luxembourg in 2005-2010, paracetamol and caffeine were by far the most abundant adulterants. [f:schneider2011]

In street heroin in Denmark (2006-2024) caffeine was found in 87.8 % of samples and acetaminophen in 71.4 %, often together (70.6 %). [f:hesse2025]

## risks

Hepatic injury and liver failure can follow both intentional and non-intentional overdose of paracetamol (acetaminophen). In the United States it accounts for more than 50 % of overdose-related acute liver failure. [f:yoon2016]

The clinical presentation of paracetamol hepatotoxicity might vary, and N-acetylcysteine therapy is considered the mainstay of treatment. [f:yoon2016]

## counselling
