## molecule

FUBIMINA (also BIM-2201), [1-(5-fluoropentyl)benzimidazol-2-yl]-naphthalen-1-ylmethanone. Base: C23H21FN2O, 360.4 g/mol, CAS 1984789-90-3. Identity checked against PubChem; achiral. THJ-2201, the indazole isomer, has the same formula and is a different substance. [f:pubchem] [f:diao2016-fubimina]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Since 2013, marketing isomers of scheduled synthetic cannabinoids, such as FUBIMINA and THJ-2201, has been a way to bypass drug legislation; FUBIMINA was easily available on the Internet at the time of the study. [f:diao2016-fubimina]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist (2-naphthoylbenzimidazole with a 5-fluoropentyl chain). [f:wiley2015] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In human hepatocytes (10 μM, 3 h), 35 metabolites of FUBIMINA were identified, formed by oxidative defluorination, further carboxylation, hydroxylation, dihydrodiol formation, glucuronidation and combinations of these. [f:diao2016-fubimina]

The authors recommend 5'-OH-BIM-018, BIM-018 pentanoic acid and BIM-018 pentanoic acid dihydrodiol as FUBIMINA-specific metabolites, to tell its intake from that of THJ-2201. [f:diao2016-fubimina]

## effects

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists are potent full agonists. [f:jones2025]

For ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA, full CB1 agonism explains altered perception, agitation, anxiety, paranoia, hallucinations, loss of consciousness, chest pain, hypertension, tachycardia and seizures; no data on the effects of this compound in humans were found in the sources used. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

In mice, FUBIMINA bound to and activated CB1 and CB2 receptors, produced locomotor suppression, antinociception, hypothermia and catalepsy, substituted for Δ9-THC in discrimination, and was a full agonist in [35S]GTPγS binding, whereas Δ9-THC is a partial agonist. [f:wiley2015]

## doses

Potency varies widely among synthetic cannabinoids: ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA are up to 140 and 85 times more potent than Δ9-THC, and no potency data for this compound were found in the sources used, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## risks

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis (43.45 vs 22.91 days). [f:ricci2026]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
