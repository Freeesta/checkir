## molecule

Levamisole ((6S)-6-phenyl-2,3,5,6-tetrahydroimidazo[2,1-b][1,3]thiazole), formula C11H12N2S, molar mass 204.29 g/mol, CAS 14769-73-4. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI, which agree on formula and InChIKey. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

## category

Chemical class: anthelmintic (grouped under non-psychoactive substances). Expected effect: no psychoactive effect expected. Typical role in samples: pharmacological adulterant, reported mainly in cocaine. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Levamisole was a drug approved by the US FDA as an immunomodulator, a chemotherapy adjuvant and an anthelmintic; agranulocytosis linked to its therapeutic use may have led to its withdrawal from the US market. [f:chang2010]

It has been identified as a cocaine adulterant in the United States since 2003; in 2009 the US DEA estimated that 69 % of the cocaine it seized contained levamisole. [f:larocque2012]

## metabolism

After an oral dose in volunteers, only 0.5 % was found unchanged in urine. Metabolites include aminorex, an amphetamine-like drug, and hydroxylated levamisole, free or conjugated with sulfate or glucuronide. [f:hess2013]

In one pharmacokinetic study the half-life in plasma was 2.0 hours and levamisole was detectable up to 36 hours; aminorex stayed below the limit of quantification. [f:hess2014]

## cuts

Levamisole is on the list of the frequent cuts of cocaine. [f:unodc-cocaine2012]

In a French university hospital, levamisole was found in 76 % of the urine samples that tested positive for cocaine. [f:eiden2015]

Why it is added is not known; it has been speculated that it is meant to potentiate the effects of cocaine. [f:chang2010]

## risks

Levamisole in street cocaine has been linked to agranulocytosis (a severe fall in a type of white blood cells) in cocaine users; agranulocytosis is also associated with its therapeutic use, and this may have led to the withdrawal of the drug from the US market. [f:chang2010]

Reviews of medical use report leukopenia and agranulocytosis, and vasculitis (inflammation of blood vessels, often involving the ear lobes), which can recur on re-exposure. Complications in cocaine users were first published in 2009. [f:larocque2012]

## counselling

Explain that levamisole in cocaine has been linked to a fall in white blood cells (neutropenia, agranulocytosis) and to skin lesions, often involving the ear lobes: of 203 cases reviewed, 140 had neutropenia and 84 skin lesions. If the person reports such signs, mention the possible exposure to a medical professional. [f:larocque2012]

Point out that symptoms recurred in 27.1 % of cocaine users after further use of cocaine that was presumably adulterated, and that stopping the exposure is fundamental. [f:larocque2012]
