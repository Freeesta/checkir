## molecule

Levamisole ((6S)-6-phenyl-2,3,5,6-tetrahydroimidazo[2,1-b][1,3]thiazole), formula C11H12N2S, molar mass 204.29 g/mol, CAS 14769-73-4. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI, which agree on formula and InChIKey. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

## category

Chemical class: anthelmintic (grouped under non-psychoactive substances). Expected effect: no psychoactive effect expected. Typical role in samples: pharmacological adulterant, reported mainly in cocaine. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Levamisole was a drug approved by the US FDA as an immunomodulator, a chemotherapy adjuvant and an anthelmintic; agranulocytosis linked to its therapeutic use may have led to its withdrawal from the US market. [f:chang2010]

It has been identified as a cocaine adulterant in the United States since 2003; in 2009 the US DEA estimated that 69 % of the cocaine it seized contained levamisole. [f:larocque2012]

## metabolism

After oral doses in healthy volunteers (humans) only a small part is excreted unchanged in urine: 0.5 % after 100 mg [f:hess2013], 3.2 ± 2.9 % after 150 mg [f:kouassi1986]. The main documented metabolite is p-hydroxylevamisole, excreted mostly as a glucuronide (12.4 ± 5.5 % of the dose after hydrolysis) [f:kouassi1986].

Plasma half-life was 2.0 h after 100 mg (peak 214 ng/mL at about 2 h; detectable up to 36 h) [f:hess2014] and 5.6 ± 2.5 h after 150 mg [f:kouassi1986]; the two studies differ and the reason is not stated.

Aminorex, an amphetamine-like drug, was reported in urine after 100 mg (up to 45 ng/mL) [f:hess2013], but it never exceeded the quantification limit in plasma [f:hess2014] and was never detected in a controlled study, where the compound identified as aminorex in earlier forensic work was probably 4-phenyl-2-imidazolidinone, an isomer [f:hundertmark2023].

## cuts

Levamisole is on the list of the frequent cuts of cocaine. [f:unodc-cocaine2012]

In a French university hospital, levamisole was found in 76 % of the urine samples that tested positive for cocaine. [f:eiden2015]

Why it is added is not known; it has been speculated that it is meant to potentiate the effects of cocaine. [f:chang2010]

## effects

In humans, no study of the effects of levamisole alone at the doses found in cocaine was located and its dose-response in this setting is not known. In vitro and in rats: levamisole had no measurable activity at monoamine receptors and transporters (an earlier study found 100-300 times lower potency than cocaine at the noradrenaline and dopamine transporters), and did not change cocaine's effects on dopamine in rats [f:zolkowska2026] [f:hofmaier2014].

Its metabolite aminorex is a potent substrate of the dopamine, noradrenaline and serotonin transporters in vitro, so it could contribute only under repeated high exposure; this has not been shown in humans [f:zolkowska2026] [f:hofmaier2014].

## doses

No dose table: no data were found on the amount of levamisole taken with cocaine or on a dose-response for it as an adulterant. Doses given to volunteers in pharmacokinetic studies were 100 mg [f:hess2014] and 150 mg [f:kouassi1986] by mouth. In cocaine-positive plasma samples levamisole was quantified at 2.2-224 ng/mL in 42.5 % of cases [f:hess2014]. A risk assessment estimated exposure per gram of cocaine and stressed chronic exposure in heavy users [f:brunt2017].

## risks

Levamisole in street cocaine has been linked to neutropenia and agranulocytosis (a severe fall in a type of white blood cells), vasculopathy with purpuric skin lesions and necrosis, and leukoencephalopathy; females appear more susceptible [f:brunt2017]. Agranulocytosis is also associated with its therapeutic use, which may have led to withdrawal from the US market [f:chang2010].

In 32 published cases (mean age 44 years, 75 % female) lesions affected legs, face and ears; 63 % had leukopenia or neutropenia and 94 % ANCA autoantibodies; 10 of 22 patients had recurrences after cocaine use (median follow-up 21 days) [f:arora2013]. Reviews of medical use report vasculitis, which can recur on re-exposure [f:larocque2012].

## interactions

No interaction identified: in rats levamisole did not change the acute effects of cocaine on dopamine and locomotion [f:zolkowska2026], and in vitro it did not enhance cocaine's blockade of transporters [f:hofmaier2014]. Interactions with other drugs, including other myelotoxic agents, were not found in the sources consulted; absence of data is not absence of risk. The adverse effects below arise in people who use cocaine adulterated with it, mostly after repeated exposure [f:brunt2017].

## counselling

Explain that levamisole in cocaine has been linked to a fall in white blood cells (neutropenia, agranulocytosis) and to skin lesions, often involving the ear lobes: of 203 cases reviewed, 140 had neutropenia and 84 skin lesions. If the person reports such signs, mention the possible exposure to a medical professional. [f:larocque2012]

Point out that symptoms recurred in 27.1 % of cocaine users after further use of cocaine that was presumably adulterated, and that stopping the exposure is fundamental. [f:larocque2012]
