## molecule

DOI, 1-(4-iodo-2,5-dimethoxyphenyl)propan-2-amine (4-iodo-2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine). Base: C11H16INO2, 321.15 g/mol, CAS 64584-34-5. Hydrochloride: C11H16INO2·HCl, about 357 g/mol, melting point 200.9 °C, white powder. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and the SWGDRUG monograph. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:swgdrug-doi]

Forms: the SWGDRUG reference material is the hydrochloride, with a diamond ATR spectrum (3 bounces, 4 cm-1 resolution). [f:swgdrug-doi]

Analogues not told apart by a single band: DOI belongs to the 2,5-dimethoxy-4-substituted amphetamines together with DOB, DOC, DOM and DOET, which differ in the substituent at position 4; the full reference spectrum is needed. [f:unodc-ats2006]

## category

Chemical class: amphetamines (ring substituted at 2,4,5), DOx series. Expected effect: psychedelic. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:palamar2025] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

The DOx are among the first subclasses of psychedelic phenethylamines; the first published synthesis of DOC and DOI dates back to 1973. [f:palamar2025]

In 2023 the US DEA proposed placing DOC and DOI in Schedule I; in most of Europe DOI is not controlled. DOI is used in serotonin receptor research. [f:palamar2025]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a ring-substituted amphetamine of the DOx series. [f:palamar2025]

## metabolism

DOI and the other 2,5-dimethoxyamphetamines studied (DOM, DOC, DOB, MDOB, TMA-2) are metabolised mainly by O-demethylation (DOM by hydroxylation of the methyl group); among the human CYPs tested only CYP2D6 is involved in these steps, with very small amounts of metabolites. [f:ewald2008]

## effects

DOI is a potent agonist at serotonin 5-HT2 receptors. [f:ewald2008]

The DOx series has a reputation for a long high and adverse events, which may have kept it from becoming a widespread recreational substance. [f:palamar2025]

## doses

Figures by Shulgin reported by an epidemiological review (not clinical studies): 1.5–3.0 mg of DOI can give an intoxication of about 16–30 hours; they are not recommended doses. The UBC guidelines report the same range. [f:palamar2025] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

## risks

In the United States, from 2008 to 2024, the review found no deaths and no described poisonings for DOI (three for DOC); the authors note that detection needs advanced testing, so the absence of cases does not measure the risk. [f:palamar2025]

In a Brazilian survey DOI appeared only in 2017, in 3.7 % of the blotters analysed. DOM, of the same series, sold in the 1960s in 20 mg doses, caused clusters of people with confusion and panic, from large doses and from redosing because of effects delayed by up to an hour. [f:palamar2025]

## interactions

DOI and five related 2,5-dimethoxyamphetamines are competitive CYP2D6 inhibitors in vitro (Ki 7.1-296 µM with the recombinant enzyme and 2.7-19.9 µM in human liver microsomes, for the group); the authors judge interactions with other CYP2D6 substrates possible but rather unlikely, with the exception of DOI, whose Ki values are not higher than that of fluoxetine. [f:ewald2008]

## counselling

Explain that the effect rises slowly: for the DOx the UBC guidelines give an onset after about an hour and a peak after about three hours and advise against taking more doses on the same occasion; for DOM delayed effects led to redosing. [f:palamar2025] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

If the result points to DOI, remind that the DOx are sometimes sold as LSD: ask how the sample was presented. [f:palamar2025] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

With a reported duration of up to 16-30 hours, ask whether the person is rested and will be in a setting with trusted people; the UBC guidelines advise allowing at least one quiet day afterwards. [f:palamar2025] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
