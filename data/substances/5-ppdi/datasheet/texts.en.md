## molecule

5-PPDI (indanyl-α-PBP), 1-(2,3-dihydro-1H-inden-5-yl)-2-pyrrolidin-1-ylbutan-1-one. Base: C17H23NO, 257.4 g/mol, CAS 2304915-05-5. Identity checked against PubChem, where 5-PPDI, indanyl-α-PBP and 3',4'-trimethylene-α-pyrrolidinobutiophenone are listed for the same entry; one stereocentre, not specified there. It is not a cannabinoid: it is a pyrrolidinophenone cathinone with an indan ring. [f:pubchem] [f:ishii2020]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cathinones (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

5-PPDI was described as a new designer drug with an indan skeleton; before 2020 there were no reports on the metabolism of indan-type cathinones. [f:ishii2020] [f:fabregat2019-5ppdi]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cathinone of the pyrrolidinophenone type with an indan ring. [f:ishii2020] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In pooled human hepatocytes twelve phase I metabolites of 5-PPDI were elucidated; it is the first metabolic study of an indanyl cathinone. [f:fabregat2019-5ppdi]

In the urine of a user 5-PPDI and its metabolites were detected: hydroxylation at the indan skeleton, which seems to be the preferred pathway, and stereoselective reduction of the carbonyl group, reported for the first time for pyrrolidinophenones in humans; several 2'-OH isomers were also found. [f:ishii2020]

## risks

Synthetic cathinones are associated with acute symptomatic seizures, often with lethal outcomes: in 42 reported cases death occurred in nearly half, with higher fatality among cathinone users. [f:machacek2026]

## interactions

In most cases with seizures, serotonin and sympathomimetic toxidromes were present. [f:machacek2026]
