## molecule

5F-UR-144 (XLR-11), [1-(5-fluoropentyl)indol-3-yl]-(2,2,3,3-tetramethylcyclopropyl)methanone. Base: C21H28FNO, 329.5 g/mol, CAS 1364933-54-9; achiral. Identity checked against PubChem, where 5F-UR-144 and XLR-11 are listed for the same entry. It is the fluorinated analogue of UR-144. [f:pubchem] [f:wohlfarth2013]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

5F-UR-144 (listed as 5FUR-144, synonym XLR11) was detected in Japan in 2012 among cannabimimetic compounds newly distributed in illegal products. [f:uchiyama2012]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist (3-acylindole with a tetramethylcyclopropyl ketone and a fluoropentyl chain). [f:jones2025] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In pooled human hepatocytes XLR-11 gave more than 25 metabolites by hydroxylation, carboxylation, hemiketal and hemiacetal formation, internal dehydration and glucuronidation; no sulfate or glutathione conjugates were seen, and defluorination led to UR-144 metabolites. [f:wohlfarth2013]

The major metabolites were 2'-carboxy-XLR-11, UR-144 pentanoic acid, 5-hydroxy-UR-144, hydroxy-XLR-11 glucuronides and 2'-carboxy-UR-144 pentanoic acid. [f:wohlfarth2013]

In HepaRG cells the N-(5-hydroxypentyl) and N-pentanoic acid metabolites were found, but the urine of an XLR-11 user showed the metabolites of the thermal degradation product, which suggests that most of the drug was degraded by heating when the herbal product was smoked. [f:kanamori2015]

## effects

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists are potent full agonists. [f:jones2025]

For ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA, full CB1 agonism explains altered perception, agitation, anxiety, paranoia, hallucinations, loss of consciousness, chest pain, hypertension, tachycardia and seizures; no data on the effects of this compound in humans were found in the sources used. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## doses

Potency varies widely among synthetic cannabinoids: ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA are up to 140 and 85 times more potent than Δ9-THC and no potency data for this compound were found in the sources used, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## risks

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis (43.45 vs 22.91 days). [f:ricci2026]

XLR-11 use has been linked with acute kidney injury. [f:wohlfarth2013]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
