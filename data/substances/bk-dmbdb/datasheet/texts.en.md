## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cathinone: a methylenedioxy cathinone with an N,N-dimethylamino group and an ethyl group in the α position (the N,N-dimethyl analogue of butylone). [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In an in vitro study of nine methylenedioxy cathinones, including dibutylone, the main pathways are N-dealkylation, β-ketone reduction, aliphatic hydroxylation, demethylenation and glucuronide conjugation; analytes with an N,N-dimethylamino group show higher N-dealkylation and β-ketone reduction ratios. In 29 urine samples the metabolites from β-ketone reduction and from demethylenation followed by O-methylation were reliable targets. [f:yeh2025]

## effects

Synthetic cathinones have stimulant and, in part, hallucinogenic properties; no data on the effects of this compound in humans were found in the sources used. [f:machacek2026]

In rats dibutylone functioned as a reinforcer in self-administration, with inverted U-shaped dose-response curves. [f:lai2022]

## risks

Synthetic cathinones are associated with acute symptomatic seizures, often with lethal outcomes: in 42 reported cases death occurred in nearly half, with higher fatality among cathinone users. [f:machacek2026]

## interactions

In most cases with seizures, serotonin and sympathomimetic toxidromes were present. [f:machacek2026]
