## molecule

AB-FUBINACA, N-[(2S)-1-amino-3-methyl-1-oxobutan-2-yl]-1-[(4-fluorophenyl)methyl]indazole-3-carboxamide. Base: C20H21FN4O2, 368.4 g/mol, CAS 1185282-01-2. Identity checked against PubChem. The two fluorine positional isomers (2- and 3-fluorobenzyl) have their own sheets. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

In the USA AB-FUBINACA was placed in Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act by a DEA final rule of 6 September 2016. [f:dea-fr2016-ab-fubinaca]

Its 3-fluorobenzyl isomer was not separated from AB-FUBINACA on an ODS column in isocratic mode in an LC-MS study, while triple quadrupole mass spectrometry differentiated all six isomers studied. [f:murakami2018]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist (indazole-3-carboxamide of a valinamide with a 4-fluorobenzyl group). [f:jones2025] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In 28 authentic urine samples 15 urinary metabolites of AB-FUBINACA were identified: hydroxylations on the indazole ring and the amino-oxobutane moiety, dealkylations and hydrolysis of the primary amide; the fluorobenzyl side chain was not modified. The parent compound was found in 54 % of the samples and three metabolites were proposed as markers of intake. [f:vikingsson2016]

In human liver microsomes the oxidation of AB-FUBINACA mainly occurred on the amino-oxobutane moiety. [f:takayama2014]

## effects

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists are potent full agonists. [f:jones2025]

For ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA, full CB1 agonism explains altered perception, agitation, anxiety, paranoia, hallucinations, loss of consciousness, chest pain, hypertension, tachycardia and seizures; no data on the effects of this compound in humans were found in the sources used. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## doses

Potency varies widely among synthetic cannabinoids: ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA are up to 140 and 85 times more potent than Δ9-THC, and no potency data for this compound were found in the sources used, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## risks

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis (43.45 vs 22.91 days). [f:ricci2026]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
