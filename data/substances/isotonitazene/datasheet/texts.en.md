## molecule

Isotonitazene, N,N-diethyl-2-[5-nitro-2-[(4-propan-2-yloxyphenyl)methyl]benzimidazol-1-yl]ethanamine. Base: C23H30N4O3, 410.5 g/mol, CAS 14188-81-9. Identity checked against PubChem and ChEBI. [f:pubchem] [f:chebi]

## category

Chemical class: nitazenes (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

The nitazenes were developed in the 1950s and abandoned because of their toxicity and unfavourable therapeutic index; they have since appeared in illicit drug markets. [f:stangeland2025]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a 2-benzylbenzimidazole 'nitazene' opioid. [f:vandeputte2024]

## metabolism

In authentic postmortem cases positive for isotonitazene the metabolites N-desethyl isotonitazene (the most frequent), 5-aminoisotonitazene and 4'-hydroxy nitazene were looked for in blood and urine; N-desethyl isotonitazene was found in six blood and four urine samples out of 11 cases. [f:tishion2025]

In vitro N-desethyl isotonitazene was consistently more potent than isotonitazene itself, unlike the other N-desethyl nitazenes, which were slightly less potent than their parents. [f:vandeputte2024]

## effects

Nitazenes are agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; in vitro their affinity and potency often exceed those of morphine and of fentanyl, and the real-world potency reported is often lower than the experimental one. [f:stangeland2025]

## doses

For the nitazenes, potency in vitro is very high and varies widely between compounds, while data on potency in humans are scarce, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:stangeland2025]

## risks

A sample bought online as isotonitazene and handed in to a Swiss drug checking service contained N-desethyl etonitazene, with no isotonitazene or etonitazene; its potency at the μ-opioid receptor was about 6-9 times that of fentanyl in two in vitro assays. [f:monti2024]

In 11 postmortem cases positive for isotonitazene, fentanyl was the most frequent co-finding. [f:tishion2025]

Nitazene poisonings often involve other opioids and sedating drugs; naloxone reversed the poisoning (median parenteral dose 1.20 mg), but some patients needed prolonged naloxone infusions, so longer observation may be needed. [f:stangeland2025]

## interactions

Isotonitazene cases are mixed drug intoxications: fentanyl was the most frequent co-finding in the postmortem series. [f:tishion2025]
