## category

## history

MDPV was described in 2009 as a classic α-pyrrolidinophenone carrying a methylenedioxy group; besides Germany it appeared in many countries in Europe and Asia. [f:meyer2010]

It emerged in the United States in 2010 within 'bath salts' products: it was found in 13 of 17 living patients analysed in the first eight months, and in that time 16 states listed synthetic cathinones as Schedule I. [f:spiller2011]

## origin

A synthetic substance, an α-pyrrolidinophenone with a methylenedioxy group. The form found in bath salts is a racemic mixture, and the S isomer is much more potent than the R isomer at the dopamine transporter. [f:baumann2017]

## metabolism

Main phase I steps in rat and human: demethylenation followed by methylation, aromatic and side-chain hydroxylation, oxidation of the pyrrolidine ring to the lactam and ring opening to the carboxylic acid. In vitro CYP2C19, CYP2D6 and CYP1A2 catalyse the first step, demethylenation. [f:meyer2010]

In rats the metabolites are 3,4-dihydroxypyrovalerone and 4-hydroxy-3-methoxypyrovalerone, the main one, which is weak at the dopamine and norepinephrine transporters; motor activation follows plasma levels of the parent drug, not of its metabolites. Plasma peaks 10-20 minutes after injection. [f:baumann2017]

## effects

MDPV is a potent uptake inhibitor at the dopamine and norepinephrine transporters; the rise in extracellular dopamine is likely to underlie its locomotor stimulant and addictive properties. [f:baumann2017]

The clinical effects of MDPV and α-PVP are individual and depend on the dose and the route of administration. [f:karila2018]

## risks

In 236 poison-centre cases from 'bath salts' exposures (MDPV found in 13 of 17 living patients analysed) the main effects were agitation (194), combative behaviour (134), tachycardia (132), hallucinations (94) and paranoia (86); outcomes were one death, 8 major and 130 moderate. [f:spiller2011]

MDPV concentrations were 24-241 ng/mL (mean 58) in the 13 living patients in whom it was detected, and 170 ng/mL in the post-mortem blood of the death. [f:spiller2011]

MDPV and α-PVP have been involved in a growing number of acute intoxications and of deaths. [f:karila2018]

## counselling

Ask which substance the person thinks it is: MDPV and α-PVP are close structural analogues, and α-PVP appeared after MDPV was banned. [f:karila2018]

Ask about route and pattern of use: in a survey of 96 cathinone users the nasal and intravenous routes were the most used and about 80% reported binge use; the UBC guidelines say to follow the rules for safer snorting and never share the straw. [f:schmoll2018] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about repeated intakes and other stimulants: the UBC guidelines warn that cathinone effects seem minimal at first and advise against mixing them with speed, ecstasy, cocaine and alcohol; in poison-centre cases the leading effects were agitation and tachycardia. [f:spiller2011] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
