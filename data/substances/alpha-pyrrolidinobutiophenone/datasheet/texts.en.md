## molecule

α-PBP, 1-phenyl-2-pyrrolidin-1-ylbutan-1-one. Base: C14H19NO, 217.31 g/mol, CAS 13415-82-2. Identity checked against PubChem (one stereocentre, not specified in the entry). [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cathinones (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

In the Swedish STRIDA project, from April 2013 to November 2015, 43 intoxicated patients tested positive for α-PBP; the substance was never declared and was confirmed only by analysis, and in all cases but one other substances were present. [f:franzen2018]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cathinone: a pyrrolidinophenone with an ethyl group in the α position, one carbon atom fewer than α-PVP. [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In urine from patients admitted after intoxication, the metabolites identified arise from hydroxylation, reduction of the carbonyl group to an alcohol and oxidation to a lactam with ring opening; most hydroxylated metabolites are glucuronidated, and carbonyl reduction and lactam formation with ring opening are the most useful targets to confirm intake. [f:kavanagh2020]

In vitro CYP2C19 and CYP2D6 are the main enzymes in the first steps. [f:manier2018]

## risks

Synthetic cathinones are associated with acute symptomatic seizures, often with lethal outcomes: in 42 reported cases death occurred in nearly half, with higher fatality among cathinone users. [f:machacek2026]

In the 43 patients: agitation or anxiety in 59%, tachycardia in 54%, hypertension in 37%; 14 (33%) needed intensive-care monitoring, 8 with severe intoxication; no fatalities reported. [f:franzen2018]

## interactions

In most cases with seizures, serotonin and sympathomimetic toxidromes were present. [f:machacek2026]
