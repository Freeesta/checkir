## molecule

MDMB-5Br-INACA (MDMB-5'Br-INACA), methyl (2S)-2-[(5-bromo-1H-indazole-3-carbonyl)amino]-3,3-dimethylbutanoate. Base: C15H18BrN3O3, 368.2 g/mol; PubChem lists no CAS number. Identity checked against PubChem (the (S) entry). It has no alkyl chain on the indazole nitrogen ("tail-less"); ADB-5Br-INACA is a different compound. [f:pubchem] [f:deventer2024]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a tail-less brominated indazole-3-carboxamide of a valine-derived ester. [f:deventer2024] [f:pubchem]

Tail-less compounds of this type are described as precursors of controlled synthetic cannabinoids in a new production route [f:monti2025]; MDMB-5'Br-INACA and ADB-5'Br-INACA were frequently detected together in Scottish prisons, and two powders seized by Belgian customs and confirmed to contain them showed varying purity. [f:deventer2024]

## effects

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists are potent full agonists. [f:jones2025]

In vitro the (S)-enantiomer retained cannabinoid receptor activity, with lower potency than the tailed analogues, which are potent and efficacious CB1 agonists; no data on its effects in humans were found in the sources used. [f:deventer2024]

## doses

Potency varies widely among synthetic cannabinoids: ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA are up to 140 and 85 times more potent than Δ9-THC, and no potency data for this compound were found in the sources used, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## risks

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis (43.45 vs 22.91 days). [f:ricci2026]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
