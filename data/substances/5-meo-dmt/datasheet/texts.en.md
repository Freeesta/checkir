## molecule

5-MeO-DMT, 5-methoxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, 2-(5-methoxy-1H-indol-3-yl)-N,N-dimethylethanamine. Base: C13H18N2O, 218.29 g/mol, CAS 1019-45-0. Identity checked on PubChem. [f:pubchem]

Structure: the DMT skeleton with a methoxy group on the indole ring. Expected bands: indole N-H at 3380-3490 cm-1 and aryl ether C-O at 1190-1300 cm-1. There is no reference spectrum of 5-MeO-DMT in the public data, so these ranges only indicate the groups. [f:coates2000] [f:nist-webbook] [f:smith-ethers]

## category

Chemical class: tryptamines. Expected effect: psychedelic. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

5-MeO-DMT is among the new synthetically produced tryptamine hallucinogens that emerged on the recreational drug market, claimed to be 'legal' alternatives to LSD. [f:araujo2015]

## origin

Described among the synthetically produced tryptamine hallucinogens. The UBC guidelines also state that tryptamines of this kind are found in plants, animals and humans, but that street material is often of synthetic origin. [f:araujo2015] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

## metabolism

5-MeO-DMT is O-demethylated by CYP2D6 to the active metabolite bufotenine and is mainly inactivated by deamination through MAO-A. [f:shen2010b] [f:shen2010]

In vitro, the variants CYP2D6.2 and CYP2D6.10 formed bufotenine with 2.6 and 40 times lower catalytic efficiency than the wild-type enzyme CYP2D6.1. [f:shen2010]

Sublingual 5-MeO-DMT has an elimination half-life of about 28 minutes. [f:collyer2026]

## effects

5-MeO-DMT acts as a non-selective serotonin agonist and causes many physiological and behavioural changes. [f:shen2010b]

Tryptamine hallucinogens cause profound changes in sensory perception, mood and thought and act primarily as agonists of the 5-HT2A receptor. [f:araujo2015]

## risks

In mice the exposure (AUC) to 5-MeO-DMT rose more than proportionally with the dose, which suggests that the risk of intoxication may increase disproportionately at higher doses. [f:shen2011]

In a systematic review of 51 publications on DMT, 5-MeO-DMT and zalsupindole the common adverse events were nausea, headache and transient cardiovascular increases. [f:collyer2026]

Intoxications and deaths with the newer tryptamines have been reported, and the lack of toxicological data hampers the assessment of their harm. [f:araujo2015]

## interactions

5-MeO-DMT is often used with MAO-A inhibitors such as harmaline. Harmaline reduces its deamination and prolongs and increases the exposure to 5-MeO-DMT and to bufotenine; the review warns of hyperserotonergic effects or serotonin toxicity. [f:shen2010b]

In mice harmaline raised the exposure to 5-MeO-DMT 3.6- to 4.4-fold and to bufotenine 6.1- to 9.9-fold. [f:shen2010]

## counselling

Ask whether it is taken with an MAO inhibitor such as harmaline, found in ayahuasca-type preparations: the exposure to 5-MeO-DMT rose several-fold in mice and a review warns of serotonin toxicity. [f:shen2010] [f:shen2010b]

Explain that raising the dose can raise the exposure more than proportionally (shown in mice), so small steps matter; the UBC guidelines add that with regular use tolerance is high and doses may rise a lot. [f:shen2011] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about mood and company: the most likely risk of hallucinogens is overwhelming distress; the UBC guidelines treat DMT and 5-MeO-DMT together and advise against using alone and against hasty mixes. [f:johnson2008] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
