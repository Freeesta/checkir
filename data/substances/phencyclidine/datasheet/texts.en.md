## molecule

Phencyclidine (PCP), 1-(1-phenylcyclohexyl)piperidine. Base: formula C17H25N, 243.40 g/mol, CAS 77-10-1. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

Forms met on the market: white crystalline powder, tablets, crystals and liquid. [f:bey2007]

The structure has no carbonyl and a tertiary amine; a salt (the hydrochloride) is recognised by the ammonium envelope, as for other amine salts. No band value is given because no verified reference spectrum is cited. [f:pubchem] [f:smith-amine-salts]

## category

Chemical class: arylcyclohexylamines (phencyclidine type). Expected effect: dissociative. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

PCP was used as a pre-induction anaesthetic and animal tranquilliser; it was recalled when patients had post-operative psychosis, agitation and dysphoria. [f:bey2007]

## origin

Synthetic compound. More than 60 analogues, some more toxic than PCP, circulated before the sale of piperidine and its derivatives became illegal in the United States in 1978. [f:bey2007]

## metabolism

About 90 % of PCP is metabolised on first pass by oxidative hydroxylation in the liver and the metabolites are glucuronidated; about 9 % is excreted unchanged in the urine. [f:bey2007]

PCP is stored in fat and released slowly, so its half-life is believed to be three days; its volume of distribution is 6.2 L/kg. [f:bey2007]

## cuts

PCP has been found in cannabis cigarettes, up to 24 % of the street cannabis samples in the data reviewed. [f:bey2007]

## effects

PCP causes dissociative anaesthesia and analgesia without the cardiorespiratory depression of other anaesthetics, mainly by blocking NMDA receptors; it also inhibits dopamine, noradrenaline and serotonin reuptake. [f:bey2007]

In over 50 % of adults the picture is violent behaviour, nystagmus, tachycardia, hypertension, anaesthesia and analgesia. [f:bey2007]

## doses

Doses as reported in a clinical review: 1-5 mg orally gives sedation and loss of inhibition, 5-10 mg may cause a schizophrenia-like psychosis and more than 10 mg usually coma; 0.25 mg intravenously sedates, against 10 mg by mouth or inhalation. [f:bey2007]

A PCP-laced cannabis cigarette contains 1-10 mg and a tablet 1-6 mg. Onset is 2-5 minutes by inhalation and 15-60 minutes by mouth; effects last from 7 hours to 7 days in chronic users. [f:bey2007]

## risks

PCP can cause hyperthermia, seizures, rhabdomyolysis, hypertension, arrhythmias and vasospasm; respiratory depression is uncommon. [f:bey2007]

Most deaths result from violent behaviour and self-inflicted trauma rather than from the direct effect of the drug. [f:bey2007]

After use an emergence reaction with psychosis, bizarre behaviour or depression can last days to weeks; prolonged psychosis is more common in chronic users. [f:bey2007]

## counselling

Explain that most deaths come from violent behaviour and self-injury, as pain is abolished: ask whether someone sober will be with the person. [f:bey2007]

Explain that the amount cannot be judged from the effect: the volume of distribution is very large, release from fat is slow, and in a series of 216 patients the blood level did not match the clinical picture. [f:bey2007]

Ask whether the sample comes from cannabis: PCP has been found in PCP-laced cannabis cigarettes. [f:bey2007]
