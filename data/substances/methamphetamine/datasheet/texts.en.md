## molecule

Methamphetamine, N-methyl-1-phenylpropan-2-amine, C10H15N, 149.23 g/mol; hydrochloride C10H15N·HCl, 185.69 g/mol. [f:pubchem] CAS of the (S)-enantiomer: base 537-46-2, hydrochloride 51-57-0. [f:swgdrug-methamphetamine] CAS of the racemate: 7632-10-2. [f:wikidata]

Forms: the most frequent salt is the hydrochloride, as powder, crystals or tablets; the (S)-enantiomer is often found, and the racemate too. [f:unodc-ats2006] The base is a liquid at room temperature. [f:swgdrug-methamphetamine] The d- and l-methamphetamine hydrochlorides melt at 170-175 °C, the racemate at 130-135 °C. [f:unodc-ats2006]

Diagnostic bands of the hydrochloride (KBr): a broad N-H2+ envelope between about 3000 and 2400 cm-1, the ring at 1488 cm-1, a band at 1060 cm-1 and the two bands of the monosubstituted ring at 749 and 700 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-methamphetamine] [f:smith-amine-salts] [f:smith-benzene]

Comparing spectra: the SWGDRUG monograph notes that different isomers and ion exchange with the matrix make comparison difficult, so sample and reference must be prepared the same way. [f:swgdrug-methamphetamine] For hydrochlorides KCl is preferred to KBr, to avoid halide exchange. [f:unodc-ats2006]

Isomers and analogues: amphetamine has the same monosubstituted ring; ephedrine and pseudoephedrine, which are precursors, can be found mixed with methamphetamine. [f:smith-benzene] [f:unodc-ats2006]

## category

Chemical class: phenethylamines, amphetamines subgroup. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Methamphetamine was first made in Japan in 1919. It has limited therapeutic use (narcolepsy, ADHD); the (S) enantiomer and the racemate are in Schedule II of the 1971 UN Convention. [f:euda-meth]

## origin

A synthetic substance. The precursor classes are the ephedrines (ephedrine, pseudoephedrine) and phenylacetone (P2P), all in Table I of the 1988 UN Convention. [f:euda-meth]

The (S) enantiomer usually comes from l-ephedrine or d-pseudoephedrine; phenylacetone gives the racemate. The impurity profile identifies the preparation route. [f:euda-meth]

Ephedrine and pseudoephedrine, the precursors, may remain in the product. [f:unodc-ats2006]

## metabolism

The main routes are aromatic hydroxylation to 4-hydroxymethamphetamine and N-demethylation to amphetamine. [f:schep2010] [f:euda-meth]

Metabolism and intoxication correlate with CYP2D6 activity. [f:chaichana2022]

Terminal half-life about 5-30 h; the mean elimination half-life is about 10 h with large variability; in plasma about 9 h. [f:schep2010] [f:cruickshank2009] [f:euda-meth]

Urine can test positive for methamphetamine because of medicines such as selegiline. [f:euda-meth]

## cuts

Caffeine is frequent in samples of amphetamine-type stimulants and, in some regions, so is ketamine. [f:unodc-ats2006]

## effects

Taken orally the effects start within about 30 minutes and last many hours; smoked, the drug reaches the brain much faster and is more addictive. [f:euda-meth]

Doses of 5-30 mg increase arousal, mood and attention. [f:cruickshank2009]

Hypertension, tachycardia and insomnia are common. [f:euda-meth]

## doses

Therapeutic oral dose of the (S) enantiomer: up to 25 mg. Illicit ingested doses range from several tens to several hundreds of mg. [f:euda-meth]

From 50 mg upwards, psychotic symptoms can appear. [f:cruickshank2009]

In fatal poisonings the blood concentration is usually above 0.5 mg/L. [f:euda-meth]

## risks

Intoxication gives a sympathomimetic picture: tachycardia, hypertension, chest pain, arrhythmias, headache, cerebral haemorrhage, hyperthermia and aggression. [f:schep2010]

Injection carries the risk of HIV and hepatitis. [f:euda-meth]

Chronic use causes neurochemical changes, with symptoms resembling paranoid schizophrenia. [f:euda-meth]

Withdrawal: low mood, anxiety and sleep disturbance, acute for 7-10 days with residual symptoms for months. [f:cruickshank2009]

## interactions

With other sympathomimetic stimulants (cocaine, amphetamine, MDMA) and with alcohol, which the UBC guidelines list as mixes to avoid, an added effect on heart rate and blood pressure is expected: this is an inference from the sympathomimetic picture, not a clinical finding. [f:schep2010] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

## counselling

Ask the route: smoked and injected reach the brain faster and are more addictive; with injection ask whether equipment is shared (HIV, hepatitis). [f:euda-meth] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Effects last many hours and with high doses or redosing can exceed 24 hours: suggest not redosing and allowing time for the comedown. [f:euda-meth] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Hyperthermia and insomnia are common: ask about sleep, food and water around the use and suggest breaks between uses. [f:schep2010] [f:euda-meth] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about anxiety, paranoia and mood after use: withdrawal lasts days and can leave symptoms for months. [f:cruickshank2009] [f:euda-meth] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Signs to recognise: chest pain, irregular heartbeat, severe headache, high temperature, agitation; with chest pain or tachycardia the UBC guidelines say to call an ambulance. [f:schep2010] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about alcohol and other stimulants (cocaine, amphetamine, MDMA): these are the combinations the service flags; the sympathomimetic picture includes tachycardia, hypertension and hyperthermia. [f:schep2010] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
