## molecule

Methamphetamine, N-methyl-1-phenylpropan-2-amine, C10H15N, 149.23 g/mol; hydrochloride C10H15N·HCl, 185.69 g/mol. [f:pubchem] CAS of the (S)-enantiomer: base 537-46-2, hydrochloride 51-57-0. [f:swgdrug-methamphetamine] CAS of the racemate: 7632-10-2. [f:wikidata]

Forms: the most frequent salt is the hydrochloride, as powder, crystals or tablets; the (S)-enantiomer is often found, and the racemate too. [f:unodc-ats2006] The base is a liquid at room temperature. [f:swgdrug-methamphetamine] The d- and l-methamphetamine hydrochlorides melt at 170-175 °C, the racemate at 130-135 °C. [f:unodc-ats2006]

Diagnostic bands of the hydrochloride (KBr): a broad N-H2+ envelope between about 3000 and 2400 cm-1, the ring at 1488 cm-1, a band at 1060 cm-1 and the two bands of the monosubstituted ring at 749 and 700 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-methamphetamine] [f:smith-amine-salts] [f:smith-benzene]

Comparing spectra: the SWGDRUG monograph notes that different isomers and ion exchange with the matrix make comparison difficult, so sample and reference must be prepared the same way. [f:swgdrug-methamphetamine] For hydrochlorides KCl is preferred to KBr, to avoid halide exchange. [f:unodc-ats2006]

Isomers and analogues: amphetamine has the same monosubstituted ring; ephedrine and pseudoephedrine, which are precursors, can be found mixed with methamphetamine. [f:smith-benzene] [f:unodc-ats2006]

Frequent cuts: caffeine, and in some regions ketamine, are often found in amphetamine-type samples. [f:unodc-ats2006]
