## molecule

4-CMC (clephedrone, 4-chloromethcathinone), 1-(4-chlorophenyl)-2-(methylamino)propan-1-one. Base: C10H12ClNO, 197.66 g/mol, CAS 1225843-86-6. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem] Hydrochloride: C10H12ClNO·HCl, 234 g/mol; melting point not determined in the SWGDRUG monograph. [f:swgdrug-4cmc]

Bands of the hydrochloride (ATR) labelled in the SWGDRUG spectrum: aryl ketone C=O at 1687 cm-1, ring at 1588 and 1572 cm-1, N-H2+ envelope with bands at 2914 and 2698 cm-1, then 1471, 1402, 1297, 1244, 1205, 1092 and 1031 cm-1, and below 900 cm-1 the bands at 840, 745 and 682 cm-1; the band at 840 cm-1 is in the region of the para-disubstituted ring. [f:swgdrug-4cmc] [f:smith-benzene] [f:smith-amine-salts]

Isomers: 2-CMC and 3-CMC have the same formula and the C=O in the same region; the ring bands below 900 cm-1 separate them, only against a reference. [f:smith-benzene] [f:swgdrug-3cmc]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cathinones. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

4-CMC has been among the most frequently seized cathinones in recent years. [f:poyatos2025]

In 199 Polish forensic cases between 2015 and 2025 it was found, often with other drugs; it is one of the chlorinated cathinones that replaced traditional stimulants such as amphetamine. [f:adamowicz2026]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a ring-substituted cathinone. Ring-substituted N-methylcathinones of this kind are obtained as racemates. [f:euda-cathinones]

## metabolism

For the methcathinone derivatives studied in rats and humans the EUDA reports N-demethylation, reduction of the keto group to hydroxyl and oxidation of ring alkyl groups; no study of the metabolites of 4-CMC was read for this sheet. [f:euda-cathinones]

## effects

In eight healthy volunteers who took a single oral dose of 4-CMC, stimulant-like subjective effects were observed over 5 hours. Compared with methylone, both the onset and the peak of the effects were delayed, and oral fluid concentrations peaked later, a pharmacokinetic difference that the authors call clinically relevant. [f:poyatos2025]

## doses

In the observational study of eight volunteers (six women) the oral doses were 100 or 150 mg of 4-CMC, self-administered and followed for 5 hours; they are the doses of that study, not recommended doses. [f:poyatos2025]

## risks

Blood concentrations in 199 Polish forensic cases ranged from under 1 to 2,084 ng/mL (mean 51 ng/mL); the mean was 198 ng/mL in fatal cases, 42 ng/mL in single-substance driving cases and 165 ng/mL in traffic accidents with 4-CMC alone. [f:adamowicz2026]

Polydrug use was common; mono-intoxication occurred in 56 cases. [f:adamowicz2026]

## interactions

Co-exposure to other substances was common in the forensic cases; the source does not establish a causal link. [f:adamowicz2026]

## counselling

Ask what the person expects: 4-CMC, 3-CMC and 2-CMC are isomers, and the ring band near 840 cm-1 points to the para isomer only against a reference. [f:swgdrug-4cmc] [f:smith-benzene]

Explain the delay: in the volunteer study onset and peak came later than with methylone, so redosing because nothing is felt yet can add up; the UBC guidelines warn that cathinone effects seem minimal at first. [f:poyatos2025] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about driving: the Polish cases were mostly driving under the influence, traffic accidents and deaths, with 4-CMC alone in traffic accidents. [f:adamowicz2026]

Ask about other stimulants and alcohol: the UBC guidelines advise against mixing cathinones with them, and polydrug use was common in the forensic cases. [f:adamowicz2026] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
