## molecule

Heroin (diacetylmorphine). Base: C21H23NO5, 369.4 g/mol, CAS 561-27-3. Hydrochloride: C21H23NO5·HCl, 405.9 g/mol, CAS 1502-95-0 (anhydrous). Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata, ChEBI and the SWGDRUG monograph. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi] [f:swgdrug-heroin]

Forms: mostly found as the base or as the hydrochloride, and a hydrochloride sample with a few percent of base is not uncommon; tartrate and citrate are less frequent. [f:swgdrug-heroin]

Diagnostic bands (ATR): the hydrochloride has the acetate C=O at 1735 cm-1, the acetyl CH3 at 1369 cm-1, the ester C-O at 1236 cm-1 (the strongest) and then 1445, 1177, 1036 and 909 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-heroin] [f:smith-esters]

Salt or base: the base has bands at 1738, 1447, 1367, 1230, 1192, 1037, 885 and 775 cm-1 and lacks the broad N-H+ envelope that the hydrochloride shows below 3000 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-heroin] [f:smith-amine-salts] The citrate has its C=O at 1723 cm-1 and bands at 1419, 1371, 1238, 1035 and 833 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-heroin]

Analogues and impurities: seized samples also contain 6-monoacetylmorphine, acetylcodeine and morphine, which share the morphinan skeleton. [f:tittarelli2020]

## category

Chemical class: morphinans (a semi-synthetic derivative of morphine), among the plant-based substances of the UNODC grouping. Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Diacetylmorphine was first synthesised in 1874 and then commercialised as a cough suppressant and a remedy for respiratory diseases; it has a powerful analgesic effect. [f:milella2023]

## origin

Semisynthetic opioid derived from morphine, an alkaloid of opium. Seized samples also contain 6-monoacetylmorphine, acetylcodeine and morphine. [f:milella2023] [f:tittarelli2020]

## metabolism

Heroin is a prodrug: its arterial half-life is 2.4 ± 0.8 min after intravenous use. It is hydrolysed to 6-monoacetylmorphine and then to morphine (also in peripheral tissues), which is glucuronidated; morphine-6-glucuronide is active. Arterial morphine input lasts up to 90 minutes. [f:rentsch2001] [f:milella2023] Morphine has a half-life of about 3-4 hours. [f:milella2023]

Bioavailability: inhaled («chasing the dragon») 52% (95% CI 44-61%), with peak levels 2-6 times lower than intravenous [f:rook2006]; smoked in laced tobacco about 14%, intranasal not known [f:milella2023]. Orally heroin is converted to morphine before reaching the circulation, and morphine bioavailability was 22.9% in opioid-naive and 45.6% in dependent people. [f:halbsguth2008]

In urine 6-monoacetylmorphine (half-life 0.6 h, detectable for 2-8 h) is the specific marker of heroin; morphine stays detectable for about 24 h. [f:cone1991]

## cuts

Caffeine and paracetamol are the most reported adulterants; Italian seizures also show methorphan, phenacetin and dimenhydrinate. [f:tittarelli2020]

## effects

Heroin acts on the mu opioid receptor through 6-monoacetylmorphine and morphine; 6-monoacetylmorphine is more efficacious than morphine in activating the G-protein (rat thalamus, cloned human receptor). [f:selley2001] It is lipophilic and crosses the blood-brain barrier easily, whereas morphine enters the brain slowly. [f:milella2023]

Intravenous use gives a «rush» of about a minute, then several hours of calm well-being; inhalation gives a comparable rush (peak within 5 min), while snorting does not reach the same euphoria. [f:milella2023] Intranasal heroin acts within 2 min and peaks at 30-35 min. [f:guessoum2025]

Intravenous heroin lowers respiratory rate, heart rate and body temperature in volunteers. [f:tress1980] In patients on high doses it lengthened reaction time by 28 ± 5.3 ms and reduced craving. [f:rook2006]

## doses

No street dose is given in the sources checked (EUDA and WHO profiles not reachable). Prescribed intravenous doses in heroin-assisted treatment are 120-450 mg; increments of 50% of the regular dose caused no serious effect in tolerant patients. [f:milella2023] [f:rook2006]

In non-dependent volunteers 2.5 and 5 mg intravenous already lowered respiratory rate. [f:tress1980] Intranasal doses in treatment averaged 346 mg (190-700 mg). [f:guessoum2025]

## risks

Respiratory depression, with the ensuing brain hypoxia, is the most serious adverse effect of mu agonists. Tolerance is lost after abstinence (prison, treatment): a high-risk period for overdose. Heroin is sometimes mixed with fentanyl and derivatives. [f:milella2023] [f:kerensky2017]

Deaths attributed to heroin overdose involve heroin alone only in a minority of cases: alcohol and benzodiazepines are commonly found, and morphine levels are no higher than in survivors. [f:darke1996]

Tolerance develops to analgesia, euphoria, sedation, nausea and respiratory depression, but not to constipation. [f:milella2023]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines add to central nervous system depression and are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017] [f:darke1996]

Alcohol and other depressants are frequently found in heroin deaths. [f:darke1996] With cocaine, opioids are the substance most often found in cocaine-related deaths in Europe, and cocaine does not reverse the brain hypoxia of a high heroin dose. [f:roque-bravo2022]

## counselling

Ask how long since last use: after breaks (prison, treatment) tolerance is lost and the usual amount may be too much. [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about benzodiazepines, alcohol and other sedatives: they are commonly found in heroin deaths. [f:darke1996] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Talk about naloxone: it reverses respiratory depression; heroin is sometimes mixed with fentanyl and derivatives. [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about the route: bioavailability is 38-53% inhaled and about 14% smoked in laced tobacco, so changing route changes the effect. [f:milella2023] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
