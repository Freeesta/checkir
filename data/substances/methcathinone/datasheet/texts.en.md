## molecule

Methcathinone (ephedrone, N-methylcathinone), 2-(methylamino)-1-phenylpropan-1-one. Base: C10H13NO, 163.22 g/mol, CAS 5650-44-2, a liquid at room temperature. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem] Hydrochloride: C10H13NO·HCl, 199.6 g/mol, melting range 176-177 °C. [f:swgdrug-methcathinone]

The SWGDRUG monograph holds KBr-pellet spectra of the hydrochloride and of the base; no band values were read from it for this sheet, so the sheet gives none. [f:swgdrug-methcathinone]

Isomers and analogues: methcathinone has the same C=O region as the ring-substituted N-methylcathinones (1684-1696 cm-1 in KBr for the methyl isomers); without a ring substituent the out-of-plane bands of the monosubstituted ring distinguish it from them, only against a reference. [f:unodc-cathinones2015] [f:smith-benzene]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cathinones. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Methcathinone was the first synthetic cathinone to appear on the recreational drug market. It is listed, with cathinone, in Schedule I of the 1971 UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances. [f:euda-cathinones]

In the fifteen years up to 2016 recreational injection of home-made ephedrone was recognised as a new cause of chronic manganese toxicity. [f:selikhova2016]

## origin

A synthetic substance. The simplest cathinones derive from ephedrine or pseudoephedrine, which are precursors of this class; if the home-made product is not purified, manganese from the oxidant used can remain in it, with a risk of manganese poisoning. [f:euda-cathinones] [f:sanotsky2020]

## metabolism

For the methcathinone derivatives studied in rats and humans the EUDA reports N-demethylation, reduction of the keto group to hydroxyl and oxidation of ring alkyl groups. [f:euda-cathinones]

## effects

Without ring substituents cathinones behave as central nervous system stimulants, with lower potency than the corresponding phenethylamine because the β-keto group makes the molecule more polar. [f:euda-cathinones]

In mice low doses of methcathinone (10 and 25 mg/kg) stimulated locomotor activity in a dose-dependent way; high doses produced epileptic seizures in 74 % of the animals. [f:asser2019]

## risks

Intravenous use of the home-made product causes a manganese-methcathinone encephalopathy: parkinsonism with dystonia and gait, balance and speech disorders, not responsive to L-dopa. [f:sanotsky2020] [f:ennok2019]

Dysarthria developed after 8.5 ± 3.2 months of daily intravenous use and in some patients got worse after stopping; MRI showed signal changes in the striatum and pallidum. [f:selikhova2016]

In mice the lethal dose 50 was 201.7 mg/kg for methcathinone alone and 110.2 mg of methcathinone/kg for the manganese-containing mixture. [f:asser2019]

## counselling

Ask the route and where the product comes from: the encephalopathy is described in people who inject a home-made product, and in the animal study both manganese and methcathinone contributed to the toxicity. [f:sanotsky2020] [f:asser2019]

Ask about changes in speech, walking or balance, even after stopping: in the patients speech disorders were often the first symptom and some worsened after stopping; these are reasons for a neurological check. [f:selikhova2016] [f:sanotsky2020]

Ask about other stimulants and alcohol: the UBC guidelines advise against mixing cathinones with them and warn that effects seem minimal at first and repeated intakes lead to overdose. [f:euda-cathinones] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

For the test: cathinones give no colour with the Marquis reagent and immunoassay tests for methamphetamine can give false positives with some of them. [f:euda-cathinones]
