## molecule

AB-CHMINACA, N-[(2S)-1-amino-3-methyl-1-oxobutan-2-yl]-1-(cyclohexylmethyl)indazole-3-carboxamide. Base: C20H28N4O2, 356.5 g/mol, CAS 1185887-21-1. Identity checked against PubChem (the (S) entry). [f:pubchem] [f:erratico2015]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cannabinoids (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: cannabinoid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

A 2026 study characterised the metabolism of AB-CHMINACA because 'older', highly potent indole- and indazole-3-carboxamide synthetic cannabinoids have re-emerged since the class-wide ban imposed by China in 2021, through the sale of unregulated precursors. [f:eccarius2026]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cannabinoid (indazole-3-carboxamide with a valine amide residue and a cyclohexylmethyl group). [f:erratico2015] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In human liver microsomes 26 metabolites of AB-CHMINACA were detected: seven mono-hydroxylated, six di-hydroxylated and one from N-dealkylation (all produced by CYP enzymes), two carboxylated (likely by amidases) and five glucuronidated. CYP3A4 was the most active of seven recombinant CYPs tested. [f:erratico2015]

Most in vitro metabolites were also present in urine from an AB-CHMINACA user; five mono-hydroxylated metabolites and one carboxylated metabolite were likely the major ones. [f:erratico2015]

A 2026 microsome study identified nine AB-CHMINACA metabolites, two verified in authentic samples; biotransformations across its three compounds included ester and amide hydrolysis, oxidation, carboxylation and dealkylation. [f:eccarius2026]

## effects

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists are potent full agonists. [f:jones2025]

For ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA, full CB1 agonism explains altered perception, agitation, anxiety, paranoia, hallucinations, loss of consciousness, chest pain, hypertension, tachycardia and seizures; no data on the effects of this compound in humans were found in the sources used. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## doses

Potency varies widely among synthetic cannabinoids: ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA are up to 140 and 85 times more potent than Δ9-THC, and no potency data for this compound were found in the sources used, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## risks

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis (43.45 vs 22.91 days). [f:ricci2026]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
