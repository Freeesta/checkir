## molecule

Esketamine, (2S)-2-(2-chlorophenyl)-2-(methylamino)cyclohexan-1-one. Base: C13H16ClNO, 237.72 g/mol, CAS 33643-46-8. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

It is the S enantiomer of ketamine, which is a racemate of two optical isomers: same formula and connectivity as the racemic ketamine sheet, with a different configuration at the quaternary carbon. This sheet is not an alias of ketamine. [f:peltoniemi2016] [f:pubchem]

Like ketamine, it has a cyclohexanone: the C=O gives a band in the region of saturated ketones, and the position of the ring substituent changes the bands below 900 cm-1. No band value is given here because no verified reference spectrum is cited. [f:smith-carbonyl] [f:smith-benzene]

## category

Chemical class: arylcyclohexylamines. Expected effect: dissociative. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Esketamine was approved by the FDA and the EMA in 2019 and is used together with an SSRI or SNRI for treatment-resistant depression. [f:kawczak2024]

## origin

A synthetic pharmaceutical substance: the S enantiomer of ketamine, authorised as a nasal spray. [f:spravato-label] [f:peltoniemi2016]

## metabolism

Esketamine is mainly metabolized to noresketamine by CYP2B6 and CYP3A4, and to a lesser extent by CYP2C9 and CYP2C19; noresketamine is metabolized further by CYP-dependent pathways and some of its metabolites are glucuronidated. [f:spravato-label]

After nasal use the terminal half-life of esketamine is 7-12 hours and that of noresketamine about 8 hours; less than 1 % of a nasal dose is excreted unchanged in urine. [f:spravato-label]
