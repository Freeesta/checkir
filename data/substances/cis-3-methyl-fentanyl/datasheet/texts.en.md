## molecule

cis-3-Methylfentanyl, N-[(3R,4S)-3-methyl-1-(2-phenylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]-N-phenylpropanamide. Base: C23H30N2O, 350.5 g/mol. Identity checked against PubChem. The PubChem entry used here is a single enantiomer of the cis form. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a designer drug of the fentanyl type with a methyl group on the piperidine ring. [f:meyer2012]

## metabolism

For 3-methylfentanyl (isomer not stated), N-dealkylation, hydroxylation of the alkyl and aryl groups, hydroxylation of the propanamide chain followed by oxidation to the carboxylic acid, and hydroxylation of the benzyl moiety followed by methylation were postulated in rats; hydroxy metabolites were partly excreted as glucuronides. [f:meyer2012]

CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP3A4 and CYP3A5 were involved in the initial steps; in rat urine after suspected recreational doses the parent drug was not detected but its nor metabolite was, so it should be the target for urine screening. [f:meyer2012]

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

## doses

The potency of fentanyl analogues varies widely between compounds, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lovrecic2019]

## risks

In poisonings with fentanyl analogues larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
