## molecule

Adinazolam, 1-(8-chloro-6-phenyl-4H-[1,2,4]triazolo[4,3-a][1,4]benzodiazepin-1-yl)-N,N-dimethylmethanamine. Base: C19H18ClN5, 351.8 g/mol, CAS 37115-32-5. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem]

Like alprazolam it is a triazolobenzodiazepine, but it carries a dimethylaminomethyl group at position 1 in place of the methyl. [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In human liver microsomes adinazolam is demethylated to N-desmethyladinazolam (NDMAD) and this to didesmethyladinazolam; the first reaction is mediated by CYP3A4 and CYP2C19 (isoenzymes 1A2, 2C9, 2D6 and 2E1 showed no activity). [f:venkatakrishnan1998]

After intravenous infusion in nine healthy volunteers the elimination half-life was 2.9 h for adinazolam and 2.8 h for NDMAD, and more than 80% of the dose appeared in the circulation as NDMAD; NDMAD produced sedation and increased beta EEG activity. [f:venkatakrishnan2005]

## effects

Designer benzodiazepines act on the GABA-A receptor and produce effects similar to therapeutic benzodiazepines: sedation, hypnosis, anxiolysis, muscle relaxation, euphoria, amnesia and addiction. [f:el-balkhi2023]

They are central nervous system depressants and sedatives that can cause psychomotor impairment. [f:wu2023]

In healthy volunteers intravenous adinazolam produced observer-rated sedation. [f:venkatakrishnan2005]

## risks

Relative potency is hard to establish, because it depends on the structure and on the many GABA-A receptor subtypes. [f:el-balkhi2023]

For many of these molecules human metabolism data are scarce, and the specific pathways and the influence of genetic polymorphisms are not clarified. [f:gameli2024] [f:wu2023]

## interactions

Pharmacodynamic interactions with other central nervous system depressants, such as opioids, are extensively reported; pharmacokinetic interactions with opioids are considered less important. [f:el-balkhi2023]

Combined with other sedatives, the risk of overdose increases. [f:wu2023]
