## molecule

4-MMC (mephedrone, 4-methylmethcathinone), 2-(methylamino)-1-(4-methylphenyl)propan-1-one. Base: C11H15NO, 177.24 g/mol, CAS 1189805-46-6. Hydrochloride: C11H15NO·HCl, 213.70 g/mol, CAS 1189726-22-4. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

Diagnostic bands of the hydrochloride (ATR): aryl ketone C=O at ~1686 cm-1, ring at ~1606 cm-1, a broad N-H2+ envelope near 2715 cm-1 and the out-of-plane band of the para-disubstituted ring at ~833 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-4mmc] [f:vujovic2020] [f:smith-benzene] [f:smith-amine-salts] In KBr the C=O is at 1684.2 cm-1 and the ring at 1605.0 cm-1. [f:unodc-cathinones2015]

Isomers: 2-MMC and 3-MMC have the same C=O; the para ring band near 833 cm-1 is what separates 4-MMC, and only by comparison with a candidate. [f:swgdrug-4mmc] [f:smith-benzene] [f:unodc-cathinones2015] Other cathinones such as methylone have a nearby C=O (1679.6 cm-1 in KBr) but also the methylenedioxy bands. [f:unodc-cathinones2015] [f:goncalves2021]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cathinones. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Until 2010 mephedrone and methylone were the most common cathinones. The first toxicologically confirmed fatal case linked to mephedrone was recorded in Sweden in 2008. [f:euda-cathinones]

## origin

A synthetic substance: cathinones are the β-keto analogues of the corresponding phenethylamine. Products may be mixtures. [f:euda-cathinones]

## metabolism

Cathinones are N-demethylated, keto-reduced and oxidised on the ring methyl group. [f:euda-cathinones]

After 150 mg orally in 6 volunteers, the most abundant plasma metabolite is 4'-carboxy-mephedrone, then nor-mephedrone; dihydro-mephedrone is about 10 % of the parent. N-succinyl-nor-mephedrone has a half-life of 8.2 h. [f:olesti2017]

In urine 4'-carboxy-mephedrone is about 10 times the parent. A novel succinyl conjugate was described after 200 mg orally in 2 volunteers. [f:olesti2017] [f:pozo2015]

## cuts

Among the common adulterants of mephedrone the UNODC manual lists paracetamol, caffeine, lidocaine and benzocaine. [f:unodc-cathinones2015]

## effects

According to user reports the effects resemble those of cocaine, amphetamine or MDMA, with sympathomimetic effects similar to amphetamine derivatives. [f:euda-cathinones]

As with cocaine, the effect is short-lived. [f:euda-cathinones]

## doses

User-reported typical doses on internet sites: 100-250 mg. Users may take several doses in succession, up to 1 g in a session; the most common wrap size in UK seizures is about 750 mg. [f:euda-cathinones]

A study gave 150 mg orally to 6 volunteers. [f:olesti2017]

## risks

In 7 patients with acute toxicity, agitation (4), palpitations (2), chest pain (2) and one seizure were seen; mean heart rate 109, mean systolic pressure 153 mmHg. [f:wood2010]

## interactions

The sympathomimetic effects are similar to those of amphetamines: with other stimulants an added effect on heart and blood pressure is expected; this is a mechanistic inference, not a clinical finding. [f:euda-cathinones]

## counselling

Effects are short and some users take several doses in succession (up to 1 g in a session); the UBC guidelines warn that effects seem minimal at first and that repeated intakes lead to overdose. [f:euda-cathinones] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about other stimulants and alcohol: the UBC guidelines advise against mixing with speed, ecstasy and cocaine and with alcohol; the sympathomimetic effects are similar to amphetamines. [f:euda-cathinones] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Signs to recognise: agitation, palpitations, chest pain, seizures; the UBC guidelines mention hyperthermia and say to take the person outside and cool them. [f:wood2010] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

For analysis: cathinones give no colour with the Marquis test, immunoassay tests for methamphetamine can give false positives, and IR is straightforward. [f:euda-cathinones]
