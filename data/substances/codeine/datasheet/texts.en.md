## metabolism

In people with normal CYP2D6 activity the O-demethylation to morphine is a minor route (5–10% of codeine clearance); about 80% of a dose goes to inactive products, mainly codeine-6-glucuronide (UGT2B7) and norcodeine (N-demethylation by CYP3A4). [f:crews2014]

## effects

Codeine is bioactivated to morphine, a strong opioid agonist, by CYP2D6, so its efficacy and safety depend on CYP2D6 activity; poor metabolisers have lower morphine levels and less analgesia. [f:crews2014]

## risks

In ultrarapid CYP2D6 metabolisers conversion to morphine is higher and standard doses of codeine have caused toxic morphine concentrations and severe or life-threatening effects; codeine is not recommended in children under 2 years. [f:crews2014]

A breastfeeding mother who is an ultrarapid metaboliser may reach high morphine concentrations and expose the infant to dangerous amounts; a fatal poisoning of a breastfed neonate has been described. [f:crews2014]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]

## counselling

Explain that the effect of codeine differs between people because it depends on CYP2D6 activity: some people form much more morphine from the same dose, others very little. [f:crews2014]

Ask whether the person is breastfeeding or gives codeine to a child: morphine formed from codeine can reach the infant, and codeine is not recommended under 2 years. [f:crews2014]

Ask about benzodiazepines, alcohol and other sedatives: mixed with opioids they are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask how long it has been since any opioid was last used, and talk about naloxone: tolerance is lost after breaks, and naloxone has no effect in someone who has taken no opioid. [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
