## molecule

Amphetamine, 1-phenylpropan-2-amine. Base: C9H13N, 135.21 g/mol, CAS 300-62-9 (racemate). Sulfate: (C9H13N)2·H2SO4, 368.49 g/mol, CAS 60-13-9. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

Forms: illicit amphetamine is mostly found as the sulfate powder, rarely as tablets; the base, in clandestine laboratories, is a brown oily liquid smelling of 1-phenyl-2-propanone; it is usually the racemate. [f:unodc-ats2006]

Diagnostic bands of the sulfate: the strongest band is the broad sulfate band near 1049 cm-1 (S-O stretch), which separates the sulfate from the hydrochloride; then the weak NH3+ bend near 1539 cm-1 and the two bands of the monosubstituted ring at ~737 and ~698 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-amphetamine] [f:smith-inorg2] [f:smith-amine-salts] [f:smith-benzene]

Salt or base: the SWGDRUG monograph gives the spectra of the hydrochloride, sulfate and phosphate; the liquid base has neither the anion bands nor the NH3+ bands. [f:swgdrug-amphetamine] [f:unodc-ats2006] [f:smith-amine-salts]

Isomers and analogues: methamphetamine has the same monosubstituted ring, so the bands below 800 cm-1 are not enough on their own. [f:smith-benzene] The optically active forms and the racemate are told apart by dedicated methods such as the melting point or microcrystal tests. [f:unodc-ats2006]

## category

Chemical class: phenethylamines, amphetamines subgroup. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Amphetamine was first synthesised in 1887 and marketed from 1933 as a nasal inhaler, then as benzedrine sulfate tablets for narcolepsy and depression. [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

## origin

Synthetic compound. Precursor: phenyl-2-propanone (P-2-P/BMK). By-products of the Leuckart route: N-formylamphetamine and 4-methyl-5-phenylpyrimidine. [f:unodc-ats2006]

## metabolism

Oral amphetamine is absorbed rapidly with high absolute bioavailability, peaking after 2-3 h (immediate release) with dose-proportional exposure; metabolism is extensive, with some routes favouring the l-isomer. [f:markowitz2017] Plasma half-life is about 10-12 h in adults (7.9 h, range 4.3-15 h, after 40 mg of oral d-amphetamine). [f:markowitz2017] [f:dolder2017]

The routes include 4-hydroxylation by CYP2D6, whose phenotype shows in the urinary ratio (121 samples), and norephedrine. [f:miranda2007] [f:losacker2021] (+)-Amphetamine is eliminated faster than (-), most at basic urinary pH. [f:wan1978]

Renal excretion depends on pH: 35-44% unchanged in 24 h after oral d-amphetamine 5-20 mg, more with high urine flow and low pH. [f:poklis1998]

After 10 mg of dexamphetamine the last detection was at about 78 h in urine and 69 h in oral fluid (cut-offs 50 and 1.5 ng/mL). [f:helland2025]

## cuts

Amphetamine-type samples often contain caffeine and, in some regions, ketamine. [f:unodc-ats2006]

## effects

It releases catecholamines by a non-exocytotic mechanism: redistribution from synaptic vesicles to the cytosol and reverse transport through the membrane carriers. [f:sulzer2005] It is a substrate of the dopamine transporter and, less so, of the noradrenaline and serotonin transporters. [f:spiller2013]

Release of noradrenaline is its most potent action in vitro (rat synaptosomes), and the oral dose giving amphetamine-type effects in humans tracks it. [f:rothman2001] Dopamine mediates the stimulant and euphoriant properties; adrenaline release is linked to the cardiovascular effects. [f:heal2013]

Intranasal d-amphetamine (12 and 50 mg/70 kg, 13 users) raised cardiovascular activity and positive mood, and its self-administration rose with dose. [f:kirkpatrick2012]

## doses

Doses of the authorised medicine, from the US prescribing information of amphetamine sulfate tablets: in narcolepsy the usual dose is 5-60 mg per day in divided doses; in attention deficit disorder with hyperactivity, children aged 6 or over start with 5 mg once or twice daily and it is only rarely necessary to exceed 40 mg per day; in exogenous obesity the usual dose is up to 30 mg daily. [f:evekeo-label]

The service guidelines advise against exceeding 50 mg of amphetamine itself, since the content of powders ranges from about 8% to nearly 100%. [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines] [f:evekeo-label]

## risks

Anorexia, weight loss and insomnia are predictable and frequent adverse effects. The move from the oral route to snorting, smoking or injection, which reach the brain faster, makes use more hazardous. [f:heal2013]

Stimulants raise blood pressure by 2-4 mm Hg and heart rate by 3-6 bpm on average; sudden death is reported with structural heart disease, and psychosis or mania can appear or worsen. [f:evekeo-label]

Overdose gives mydriasis, tremor, agitation, hallucinations, delirium and seizures, and can end in hyperthermia above 40 °C, rhabdomyolysis, myocardial infarction and coma. [f:spiller2013] [f:evekeo-label] Target organs of amphetamines include the heart, brain, liver and kidney. [f:carvalho2012]

## interactions

Use during or within 14 days after monoamine oxidase inhibitors is contraindicated: they slow amphetamine metabolism and potentiate its release of norepinephrine and other monoamines, which can cause headache and other signs of hypertensive crisis; neurological toxic effects and malignant hyperpyrexia can occur, sometimes with fatal results. [f:evekeo-label]

The concomitant use of serotonergic drugs increases the risk of serotonin syndrome. [f:evekeo-label]

CYP2D6 inhibitors (for example paroxetine, fluoxetine, quinidine, ritonavir) may increase the exposure to amphetamine and the risk of serotonin syndrome. [f:evekeo-label]

Gastrointestinal acidifying agents (guanethidine, reserpine, glutamic acid HCl, ascorbic acid, fruit juices) lower the absorption of amphetamines and urinary acidifying agents increase their urinary excretion; both lower blood levels. [f:evekeo-label]

## counselling

Ask how long the person plans to stay awake and about sleep, food and water: insomnia and anorexia are frequent, so plan at least 4 hours of rest in 24 and a sip every 15-20 minutes. [f:heal2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about the route: swallowing is the least risky; snorting, smoking or injecting reach the brain faster and are more hazardous, and injection adds the risk of HIV and hepatitis. [f:heal2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about antidepressants and MAO inhibitors (contraindicated), alcohol, which masks intoxication, and cannabis, which can raise anxiety. [f:evekeo-label] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain the comedown (low mood, jaw tremor, loss of pleasure, oversleeping, hallucinations after heavy use; leave weeks between uses) and that stimulants can cause psychotic or manic symptoms. [f:evekeo-label] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Remember that the content varies a lot and that amphetamine-type samples often contain caffeine and, in some regions, ketamine. [f:unodc-ats2006] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
