## molecule

Amphetamine, 1-phenylpropan-2-amine. Base: C9H13N, 135.21 g/mol, CAS 300-62-9 (racemate). Sulfate: (C9H13N)2·H2SO4, 368.49 g/mol, CAS 60-13-9. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

Forms: illicit amphetamine is mostly found as the sulfate powder, rarely as tablets; the base, in clandestine laboratories, is a brown oily liquid smelling of 1-phenyl-2-propanone; it is usually the racemate. [f:unodc-ats2006]

Diagnostic bands of the sulfate: the strongest band is the broad sulfate band near 1049 cm-1 (S-O stretch), which separates the sulfate from the hydrochloride; then the weak NH3+ bend near 1539 cm-1 and the two bands of the monosubstituted ring at ~737 and ~698 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-amphetamine] [f:smith-inorg2] [f:smith-amine-salts] [f:smith-benzene]

Salt or base: the SWGDRUG monograph gives the spectra of the hydrochloride, sulfate and phosphate; the liquid base has neither the anion bands nor the NH3+ bands. [f:swgdrug-amphetamine] [f:unodc-ats2006] [f:smith-amine-salts]

Isomers and analogues: methamphetamine has the same monosubstituted ring, so the bands below 800 cm-1 are not enough on their own. [f:smith-benzene] The optically active forms and the racemate are told apart by dedicated methods such as the melting point or microcrystal tests. [f:unodc-ats2006]

Frequent cuts: caffeine, and in some regions ketamine, are often found in amphetamine-type samples. [f:unodc-ats2006]

## category

Chemical class: phenethylamines, amphetamines subgroup. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]
