## molecule

Amphetamine, 1-phenylpropan-2-amine. Base: C9H13N, 135.21 g/mol, CAS 300-62-9 (racemate). Sulfate: (C9H13N)2·H2SO4, 368.49 g/mol, CAS 60-13-9. Identity checked against PubChem, Wikidata and ChEBI. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata] [f:chebi]

Forms: illicit amphetamine is mostly found as the sulfate powder, rarely as tablets; the base, in clandestine laboratories, is a brown oily liquid smelling of 1-phenyl-2-propanone; it is usually the racemate. [f:unodc-ats2006]

Diagnostic bands of the sulfate: the strongest band is the broad sulfate band near 1049 cm-1 (S-O stretch), which separates the sulfate from the hydrochloride; then the weak NH3+ bend near 1539 cm-1 and the two bands of the monosubstituted ring at ~737 and ~698 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-amphetamine] [f:smith-inorg2] [f:smith-amine-salts] [f:smith-benzene]

Salt or base: the SWGDRUG monograph gives the spectra of the hydrochloride, sulfate and phosphate; the liquid base has neither the anion bands nor the NH3+ bands. [f:swgdrug-amphetamine] [f:unodc-ats2006] [f:smith-amine-salts]

Isomers and analogues: methamphetamine has the same monosubstituted ring, so the bands below 800 cm-1 are not enough on their own. [f:smith-benzene] The optically active forms and the racemate are told apart by dedicated methods such as the melting point or microcrystal tests. [f:unodc-ats2006]

## category

Chemical class: phenethylamines, amphetamines subgroup. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## origin

Synthetic compound. Precursor: phenyl-2-propanone (P-2-P/BMK). By-products of the Leuckart route: N-formylamphetamine and 4-methyl-5-phenylpyrimidine. [f:unodc-ats2006]

## metabolism

Amphetamine is metabolised to norephedrine and 4-hydroxyamphetamine; CYP2D6, which is polymorphic and stereoselective, is involved. (S)-amphetamine has a shorter elimination half-life than (R). [f:losacker2021]

After 40 mg of oral d-amphetamine the half-life is 7.9 hours (95% CI 6.9-9.1; range 4.3-15 hours). [f:dolder2017]

## cuts

Amphetamine-type samples often contain caffeine and, in some regions, ketamine. [f:unodc-ats2006]

## effects

It releases dopamine and noradrenaline; dopamine is the primary mediator of the stimulant and euphoriant properties. It also releases adrenaline from the peripheral sympathetic nervous system, an action linked to the cardiovascular effects. [f:heal2013]

## doses

Doses of the authorised medicine, from the US prescribing information of amphetamine sulfate tablets: in narcolepsy the usual dose is 5-60 mg per day in divided doses; in attention deficit disorder with hyperactivity, children aged 6 or over start with 5 mg once or twice daily and it is only rarely necessary to exceed 40 mg per day; in exogenous obesity the usual dose is up to 30 mg daily. [f:evekeo-label]

## risks

Anorexia, weight loss and insomnia are predictable and frequent adverse effects. The move from the oral route to snorting, smoking or injection, which reach the brain faster, makes use more hazardous. [f:heal2013]

## interactions

Use during or within 14 days after monoamine oxidase inhibitors is contraindicated: they slow amphetamine metabolism and potentiate its release of norepinephrine and other monoamines, which can cause headache and other signs of hypertensive crisis; neurological toxic effects and malignant hyperpyrexia can occur, sometimes with fatal results. [f:evekeo-label]

The concomitant use of serotonergic drugs increases the risk of serotonin syndrome. [f:evekeo-label]

CYP2D6 inhibitors (for example paroxetine, fluoxetine, quinidine, ritonavir) may increase the exposure to amphetamine and the risk of serotonin syndrome. [f:evekeo-label]

Gastrointestinal acidifying agents (guanethidine, reserpine, glutamic acid HCl, ascorbic acid, fruit juices) lower the absorption of amphetamines and urinary acidifying agents increase their urinary excretion; both lower blood levels. [f:evekeo-label]

## counselling

Ask about sleep and food: insomnia and anorexia are frequent effects, so plan for rest and meals. [f:heal2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about the route: snorting, smoking or injection reach the brain faster than the oral route and are more hazardous. [f:heal2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Remember that amphetamine-type samples often contain caffeine and, in some regions, ketamine. [f:unodc-ats2006] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
