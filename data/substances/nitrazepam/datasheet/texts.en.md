## origin

A synthetic substance, a 7-nitro-1,4-benzodiazepin-2-one used as a hypnotic. [f:pubchem] [f:konishi2017]

## metabolism

In man nitrazepam is mainly reduced to 7-aminonitrazepam and then acetylated to 7-acetylaminonitrazepam. In human liver the reduction is carried out by aldehyde oxidase 1 (AOX1) and the acetylation by N-acetyltransferase 2 (NAT2). [f:konishi2017]

Arylacetamide deacetylase (AADAC) hydrolyses 7-acetylaminonitrazepam in human liver; CYP3A4 is the enzyme that most readily forms, from 7-aminonitrazepam, the precursor of the N-acetylcysteine conjugate of a suspected reactive metabolite, detected in the study. [f:konishi2017]

In 16 healthy volunteers given 5 mg by mouth the half-life was 22.1 hours in the Japanese group and 21.5 hours in the white group, with no pharmacokinetic difference between the groups. [f:vangerven1998]

## effects

Benzodiazepines act on the GABA-A receptor and produce sedation, hypnosis, anxiolysis, muscle relaxation, euphoria, amnesia and addiction. [f:el-balkhi2023]

They are central nervous system depressants and sedatives that can cause psychomotor impairment. [f:wu2023]

## risks

Relative potency is hard to establish, because it depends on the structure and on the many GABA-A receptor subtypes. [f:el-balkhi2023]

## interactions

Pharmacodynamic interactions with other central nervous system depressants, such as opioids, are extensively reported; pharmacokinetic interactions with opioids are considered less important. [f:el-balkhi2023]

Combined with other sedatives, the risk of overdose increases. [f:wu2023]
