## molecule

Diazepam, 7-chloro-1-methyl-5-phenyl-3H-1,4-benzodiazepin-2-one. Formula C16H13ClN2O, 284.74 g/mol. Identity checked on PubChem. [f:pubchem]

CAS 439-14-5, melting point 131-135 °C. [f:swgdrug-diazepam]

Diazepam exists in different crystalline forms (polymorphs) that give different IR spectra: sample and standard should be prepared in the same way. [f:swgdrug-diazepam]

## category

Chemical class: benzodiazepines. Expected effect: central nervous system depressant (sedative). Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

In 1991 alprazolam and diazepam were the two most prescribed benzodiazepine anxiolytics in the United States. [f:juergens1991]

## metabolism

Diazepam is converted by N-demethylation to the active metabolite desmethyldiazepam (nordiazepam), which accumulates during long-term treatment. [f:mandelli1978]

In six healthy volunteers after 10 mg intravenously the mean elimination half-life of diazepam was 43.3 and 44.9 h in two sessions; the presence of desmethyldiazepam did not change it. [f:abernethy1981]

The dose/blood level ratio varies up to 30-fold between people, and the half-life is prolonged in the elderly, in newborns and in some liver diseases. [f:mandelli1978]

## effects

From its kinetic properties a single evening dose is enough for hypnotic and anxiolytic effects in most patients; there is no clear relationship between plasma concentration and clinical effect. [f:mandelli1978]

## risks

Diazepam is rapidly absorbed and enters the brain quickly, which favours reinforcement; it has addictive potential and causes a withdrawal syndrome. [f:juergens1991]

Physiological dependence on benzodiazepines comes with a withdrawal syndrome of sleep disturbance, irritability, anxiety, tremor, sweating and palpitations; seizures and psychotic reactions are also reported at high doses. Withdrawal appears more severe after high doses or after short-acting benzodiazepines. [f:petursson1994]

## interactions

Concurrent use of an opioid and a benzodiazepine is associated with a higher risk of an emergency room visit or hospital admission for opioid overdose (adjusted odds ratio 2.14, 95% confidence interval 2.05-2.24; privately insured US patients 2001-2013, retrospective study: an association, not proof of causation). [f:sun2017]

## counselling

Ask whether the person also takes opioids or heroin: the UBC guidelines say benzodiazepines can depress breathing and that the risk of respiratory arrest rises with opiates; the combination with opioids is linked to more overdose visits. [f:sun2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain that after repeated doses the active metabolite accumulates and the half-life is long and varies widely between people: sedation may last longer than expected. [f:mandelli1978]
