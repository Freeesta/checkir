## molecule

GBL (gamma-butyrolactone), oxolan-2-one. C4H6O2, 86.09 g/mol, CAS 96-48-0, melting point -42 °C. Identity checked on PubChem. [f:pubchem] [f:swgdrug-gbl]

In aqueous solution GBL is hydrolysed and exists in equilibrium with GHB, depending on pH. [f:swgdrug-gbl]

Bands (ATR, diamond): the lactone C=O at 1762 cm-1 (1770 cm-1 neat between KBr disks) is the strongest band, then 1160, 1034 and 989 cm-1, weaker bands at 1376, 1240, 930 and 869 cm-1, and C-H stretches at 2991 and 2916 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-gbl]

## category

Chemical class: other substances. Expected effect: depressant/sedative (as a pro-drug of GHB). Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

GBL and 1,4-butanediol have cosmetic and industrial uses and, at the time of the review, were not regulated like GHB, so they were used as alternative sources of GHB. [f:busardo2015]

## origin

An industrial solvent and the lactone of GHB; it is a precursor in the manufacture of GHB. [f:tay2022] [f:swgdrug-gbl]

## metabolism

After ingestion GBL is converted into GHB with a half-life of about 1 minute. [f:busardo2015]

The conversion is catalysed by the enzyme lactonase. [f:tay2022]

GHB then follows its own pathway: oxidation to succinic semialdehyde and succinic acid, with a plasma half-life of 30-50 min. [f:busardo2015]

## effects

GBL acts as a pro-drug: it is rapidly converted into GHB in the body, so its effects are those of GHB. [f:busardo2015] [f:schep2012]

## risks

The risks are those of GHB: central nervous and respiratory depression, with GBL and 1,4-butanediol metabolised endogenously to GHB. [f:schep2012]

Long-term use of GHB and GBL leads to addiction, tolerance and physical dependence, so stopping abruptly triggers a withdrawal syndrome. [f:busardo2015]

## interactions

Because GBL acts through GHB, co-ingestion with alcohol is associated with more severe toxicity, more intensive-care admissions and longer hospital stays. [f:tay2022]

## counselling

Explain that GBL becomes GHB within about a minute of ingestion and acts as GHB: the UBC guidelines for GHB say that if the concentration of a liquid is uncertain a reduced dose is the rule, and that overdoses often follow further doses. [f:busardo2015] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about alcohol and other depressants: co-ingestion with alcohol is linked to more severe toxicity, and the UBC guidelines for GHB say never to combine them. [f:tay2022] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about frequency: long-term use leads to tolerance and physical dependence, and stopping abruptly causes withdrawal; the UBC guidelines for GHB mention sleep problems and anxiety with regular use. [f:busardo2015] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Signs to recognise: central nervous and respiratory depression, with deep sleep that is hard to tell from coma; vomiting while drowsy risks choking. [f:schep2012] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
