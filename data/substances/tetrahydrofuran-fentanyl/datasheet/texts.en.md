## molecule

Tetrahydrofuranylfentanyl, N-phenyl-N-[1-(2-phenylethyl)piperidin-4-yl]oxolane-2-carboxamide. Base: C24H30N2O2, 378.5 g/mol, CAS 2142571-01-3. Identity checked against PubChem: the entry has one stereocentre (position 2 of the oxolane ring) without stereochemistry, so it covers the racemate or an unspecified form. The oxolane-3-carboxamide is a separate substance. [f:pubchem]

## category

Chemical class: fentanyl analogues (UNODC grouping). Expected effect: opioid. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Tetrahydrofuranylfentanyl was identified in Europe and the United States as an emerging novel opioid, causing death in at least 15 drug-using individuals as of the 2018 report; it is structurally similar to furanylfentanyl. [f:krotulski2018-thff]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a fentanyl analogue with a tetrahydrofuran (oxolane) ring in the N-acyl group in place of the propanoyl group. [f:pubchem] [f:armenian2018]

## metabolism

In pooled human liver microsomes seven metabolites were identified, most notably THF-norfentanyl, a metabolite unique to this compound, and hydroxyl-THFF; 4-ANPP and its hydroxylated form were also found but are of limited use as markers because other fentanyl analogues share them. [f:krotulski2018-thff]

In a separate study on tetrahydrofuranoyl-fentanyl (THF-F), 13 phase I metabolites were identified, formed mainly by N-dealkylation, hydroxylation and dihydroxylation, with CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 as the main isozymes. [f:gampfer2020]

## effects

Fentanyl analogues are full agonists at the μ-opioid receptor; the typical clinical effects are miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

## doses

Potency varies widely between fentanyl analogues and no potency data for this compound were found in the sources used, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lovrecic2019]

## risks

In poisonings with fentanyl analogues larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

As soon as a compound is regulated new ones appear, which makes detection difficult; some illicit pills with these compounds look like known opioids or benzodiazepines. [f:armenian2018]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]
