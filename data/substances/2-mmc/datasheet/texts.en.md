## molecule

2-MMC (2-methylmethcathinone), 2-(methylamino)-1-(2-methylphenyl)propan-1-one. Base: C11H15NO, 177.24 g/mol, CAS 1246911-71-6. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem] Hydrochloride: C11H15NO·HCl, 213 g/mol, melting point 144.6 °C; the SWGDRUG monograph gives no CAS. [f:swgdrug-2mmc]

Bands of the hydrochloride (ATR) labelled in the SWGDRUG spectrum: aryl ketone C=O at 1693 cm-1, ring at 1601 and 1583 cm-1, N-H2+ envelope with bands at 2729, 2534 and 2470 cm-1, then 1489, 1458, 1302, 1246 and 1030 cm-1, and below 800 cm-1 the bands at 787, 752 and 729 cm-1, in the region of the ring out-of-plane bands. [f:swgdrug-2mmc] [f:smith-benzene] [f:smith-amine-salts]

Isomers: 3-MMC and 4-MMC have the same formula and a C=O in the same region (1684-1696 cm-1 in KBr); the out-of-plane ring bands below 900 cm-1 separate them, and only by comparison with a reference. [f:unodc-cathinones2015] [f:smith-benzene]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cathinones. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

Since 2023 a notable emergence of 2-MMC has been noted in Europe; a series of 13 cases from the French Alps suggests a resurgence in which it may replace cathinones previously prevalent, such as 3-MMC and 3-CMC. [f:boudin2026]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a ring-substituted cathinone. Ring-substituted N-methylcathinones of this kind are obtained as racemates. [f:euda-cathinones]

## metabolism

In 13 clinical and forensic cases a hydroxylated, an N-desmethyl and a keto-reduced derivative were confirmed; the same phase I metabolites had been described for 3-MMC. [f:boudin2026]

For the methcathinone derivatives studied in rats and humans the EUDA reports N-demethylation, reduction of the keto group to hydroxyl and oxidation of ring alkyl groups. [f:euda-cathinones]

## effects

The sources read give no controlled data on the effects of 2-MMC. For ring-substituted cathinones the EUDA states that few formal pharmacological studies exist and that, in patients, they show sympathomimetic effects similar to those of amphetamine derivatives. [f:euda-cathinones]

## risks

In France 13 men aged 19-63 were described (five deaths, eight living), in contexts of drug-facilitated crime, driving under the influence, chemsex and suicide. [f:boudin2026]

Post-mortem blood concentrations ranged from 4.7 to 3,030 ng/mL and plasma concentrations in clinical and other cases from 106 to 218 ng/mL. [f:boudin2026]

## counselling

Ask what the person expects: 2-MMC, 3-MMC and 4-MMC share a formula and the ring band below 900 cm-1 separates them only against a reference; in France 2-MMC has appeared where 3-MMC and 3-CMC used to circulate. [f:unodc-cathinones2015] [f:boudin2026]

Ask about repeated intakes: no dose or duration data for 2-MMC were found; for cathinones the EUDA notes that the high is short and users may take several doses in a session, and the UBC guidelines warn that at first the effects seem minimal. [f:euda-cathinones] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about other stimulants and alcohol: the UBC guidelines advise against mixing cathinones with speed, ecstasy, cocaine and alcohol, and the EUDA describes sympathomimetic effects like amphetamine. [f:euda-cathinones] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about route and context: cathinones are water-soluble and can be injected, and chemsex was among the contexts of the French cases; for overheating the UBC guidelines say to move the person to open air and cool them. [f:euda-cathinones] [f:boudin2026] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
