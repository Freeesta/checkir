## molecule

ADB-FUBINACA, (2S)-N-(1-amino-3,3-dimethyl-1-oxobutan-2-yl)-1-[(4-fluorophenyl)methyl]indazole-3-carboxamide. Base: C21H23FN4O2, 382.4 g/mol, CAS 1185282-00-1. Identity checked against PubChem (the (S) entry). [f:pubchem] [f:carlier2017-adb-fubinaca]

## category

## origin

A synthetic substance, a synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist (indazole-3-carboxamide of a tert-leucinamide with a fluorobenzyl group). It was among the indazole-3-carboxamides identified in Kuwait in 2018. [f:al-matrouk2019-adb] [f:pubchem]

## metabolism

In human liver microsomes the half-life of ADB-FUBINACA was 39.7 min, with a predicted hepatic clearance of 9.0 mL/min/kg (intermediate clearance); 23 metabolites were identified in hepatocytes, mainly from hydroxylation of the alkyl chain and the indazole, hydrolysis of the terminal amide, glucuronidation and dehydrogenation. [f:carlier2017-adb-fubinaca]

The hydroxyalkyl, hydroxydehydroalkyl and hydroxyindazole metabolites are recommended as intake markers; N-dealkylated metabolites are not specific. [f:carlier2017-adb-fubinaca]

## effects

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists are potent full agonists. [f:jones2025]

All the indole and indazole synthetic cannabinoids synthesised in a pharmacology study, ADB-FUBINACA among them, were high-potency agonists of CB1 (EC50 0.24-21 nM) and CB2 (EC50 0.88-15 nM). [f:banister2015]

For ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA, full CB1 agonism explains altered perception, agitation, anxiety, paranoia, hallucinations, loss of consciousness, chest pain, hypertension, tachycardia and seizures; no data on the effects of this compound in humans were found in the sources used. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## doses

Potency varies widely among synthetic cannabinoids: ADB-FUBINACA and AMB-FUBINACA are up to 140 and 85 times more potent than Δ9-THC, so no dose is safe to suggest. [f:lobato-freitas2021]

## risks

Synthetic cannabinoids have been found as adulterants of several recreational substances, including cannabis and opioids, with unpredictable clinical outcomes. [f:jones2025]

In a cohort comparison, hospitalisations for synthetic cannabinoid psychosis lasted longer than those for cannabis psychosis (43.45 vs 22.91 days). [f:ricci2026]

## interactions

Adulteration of cannabis and opioids with synthetic cannabinoids raises the potential for drug interactions. [f:jones2025]
