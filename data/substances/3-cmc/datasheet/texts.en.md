## molecule

3-CMC (3-chloromethcathinone), 1-(3-chlorophenyl)-2-(methylamino)propan-1-one. Base: C10H12ClNO, 197.66 g/mol, CAS 1049677-59-9. Identity checked against PubChem. [f:pubchem] Hydrochloride: C10H12ClNO·HCl, 234.12 g/mol, CAS 1607439-32-6, melting point 198.3 °C. [f:swgdrug-3cmc]

Bands of the hydrochloride (ATR) labelled in the SWGDRUG spectrum: aryl ketone C=O at 1692 cm-1, ring at 1593 and 1569 cm-1, N-H2+ envelope with bands at 2793, 2705 and 2441 cm-1, then 1464, 1360, 1245, 1207, 1093 and 1036 cm-1, and below 900 cm-1 the bands at 806, 732 and 672 cm-1. [f:swgdrug-3cmc] [f:smith-benzene] [f:smith-amine-salts]

Isomers: 2-CMC and 4-CMC have the same formula and the C=O in the same region; only the ring bands below 900 cm-1 help to tell them apart, and only against a reference. [f:smith-benzene] [f:swgdrug-4cmc]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cathinones. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

3-CMC became involved in many seizures and poisoning reports. [f:bassi2025]

In 81 Polish forensic cases between 2015 and 2025 the chlorinated cathinones 3-CMC and 4-CMC were found to be replacing traditional stimulants such as amphetamine. [f:adamowicz2026]

## origin

A synthetic substance, a ring-substituted cathinone. Ring-substituted N-methylcathinones of this kind are obtained as racemates. [f:euda-cathinones]

## metabolism

No human metabolism study was found. A computational prediction for 3-CMC points to N-dealkylation, N-hydroxylation and phenyl hydroxylation as the main reactions; these are predictions, not measurements. [f:bassi2025]

For the methcathinone derivatives studied in rats and humans the EUDA reports N-demethylation, reduction of the keto group to hydroxyl and oxidation of ring alkyl groups. [f:euda-cathinones]

## effects

In male and female mice 3-CMC (0.1-30 mg/kg, intraperitoneal) caused locomotor stimulation, with marked tachypnoea and hypothermia; sensorimotor and prepulse-inhibition changes appeared only at high doses, with minor sex differences. [f:bassi2025]

For human effects the source gives only intoxication cases; the literature on its pharmaco-toxicological effects is lacking. [f:bassi2025]

## risks

In Italy (2014-2025) the poison centre recorded non-fatal intoxications in male patients with psychomotor agitation, psychosis, aggressiveness, CNS depression, cardiac arrhythmias, chest pain and tachypnoea. [f:bassi2025]

In Polish forensic cases blood concentrations ranged from under 1 to 2,680 ng/mL (mean 68 ng/mL), with a mean of 668 ng/mL in fatal cases and 15 ng/mL in single-substance driving cases; polydrug use was common (mono-intoxication in 26 of 81 cases). [f:adamowicz2026]

## interactions

Computational predictions suggest a potential for drug-drug interactions and for cardiotoxicity for 3-CMC and related compounds; they are not clinical findings. [f:bassi2025]

Co-exposure to other substances was common in the forensic cases; the source does not establish a causal link. [f:adamowicz2026]

## counselling

Ask what the person expects: 3-CMC, 4-CMC and 2-CMC are isomers, and the ring band below 900 cm-1 separates them only against a reference; the Polish cases show both 3-CMC and 4-CMC circulating, in 15 cases together. [f:smith-benzene] [f:adamowicz2026]

Ask about repeated intakes: no dose data for 3-CMC were found; for cathinones the EUDA notes a short high and several doses in a session, and the UBC guidelines warn that at first the effects seem minimal. [f:euda-cathinones] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about other substances, alcohol and medicines: most Polish cases involved several substances, and the UBC guidelines advise against mixing cathinones with other stimulants and alcohol. [f:adamowicz2026] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Signs to recognise: agitation, psychosis, palpitations or chest pain, rapid breathing; for overheating the UBC guidelines say to move the person to open air and cool them. [f:bassi2025] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
