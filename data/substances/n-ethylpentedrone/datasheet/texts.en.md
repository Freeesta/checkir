## molecule

N-Ethylpentedrone (NEP), 2-(ethylamino)-1-phenylpentan-1-one. Base: C13H19NO, 205.30 g/mol, CAS 779974-89-9. Identity checked against PubChem and Wikidata. [f:pubchem] [f:wikidata]

The abbreviation NEP is also used for N-ethylpentylone (ephylone), a different substance; some authors call N-ethylpentedrone N-ethyl-nor-pentedrone. [f:espinosa2024] [f:nunezmontero2025]

## category

Chemical class: synthetic cathinones. Expected effect: stimulant. Typical role in samples: active principle. This classification is a checkir proposal awaiting review. [f:unodc-ewa] [f:checkir-classification]

## history

N-Ethylpentedrone emerged in the mid-2000s and stayed legally available in the Netherlands until 1 July 2025; it has been scheduled by UNODC and by the European Union Drugs Agency since 2024. [f:roosendaal2026] [f:pasquet2026]

## origin

A synthetic substance, an N-ethyl cathinone with a three-carbon α chain. [f:nunezmontero2025]

## metabolism

In volunteers keto reduction, hydroxylation and dealkylation were the common pathways of NEP, with a glucuronide of the parent drug; in a fatal case six phase I metabolites were proposed (keto reduction, N-dealkylation, hydroxylation, oxidation) and no phase II metabolites were found. [f:massano2024] [f:pasquet2026]

In 12 forensic cases the target compound was NEP itself, with dihydro-NEP and N-desethyl-NEP as complementary analytes; one case suggested a half-life of about 2-3 h. [f:roosendaal2026]

## effects

After intranasal self-administration in 8 experienced users, NEP produced psychostimulant-like effects within 20 min, with a return to baseline 3-4 h later; compared with NEH, onset was faster and the effects disappeared sooner. [f:nunezmontero2025]

## doses

The amount self-administered by the participants of the observational study was 20-40 mg intranasally; it is a study dose, not an indication of an amount to take. [f:nunezmontero2025]

## risks

In 12 forensic cases the median blood concentration was 0.062 mg/L before death (range below 0.005 to 0.45) and 0.63 mg/L after death (0.26-0.93); in four cases a multi-drug intoxication was judged a possible cause of death. NEP may rise up to 5 times after death and degrades in blood kept at room temperature. [f:roosendaal2026]

A 29-year-old man died in a chemsex context with 18 mg/L of NEP in peripheral blood; hair analysis showed repeated exposure over several months. [f:pasquet2026]

## counselling

Check which substance is meant: NEP also abbreviates N-ethylpentylone (ephylone), and the name on the label or in a report may not tell them apart. [f:espinosa2024] [f:nunezmontero2025]

Ask about the context of use, including chemsex: the published fatal case involved it, with very high concentrations and repeated use over months. [f:pasquet2026]

Invite not to repeat the intake to chase effects that fade within hours: onset and offset are fast, and the UBC guidelines note that early effects can seem mild. [f:nunezmontero2025] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
