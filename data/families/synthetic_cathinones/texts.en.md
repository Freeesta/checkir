## effects

Synthetic cathinones have stimulant and, in part, hallucinogenic properties. [f:machacek2026]

They amplify catecholamine release, like cocaine and amphetamine-type stimulants. [f:gresnigt2026]

## risks

They can cause arrhythmia, ischaemia, myocardial injury and sudden cardiac death, with presentations sometimes more severe in young people without traditional risk factors. [f:gresnigt2026]

They are associated with acute symptomatic seizures, often with lethal outcomes: in 42 reported cases death occurred in nearly half, with higher fatality among cathinone users. [f:machacek2026]

Intracranial haemorrhage and cerebral oedema (often after a hypertensive crisis), hypoglycaemia and hyponatraemia can contribute to the seizures. [f:machacek2026]

In a case series from an outbreak, rhabdomyolysis related to cathinones reached 63 %. [f:ricci2026]

## interactions

In most cases with seizures, serotonin and sympathomimetic toxidromes were present. [f:machacek2026]

Cocaine, amphetamine-type stimulants and synthetic cathinones have sympathomimetic effects through catecholamine excess. [f:gresnigt2026]

## counselling

Ask whether they take them with other stimulants: the UBC guidelines advise against mixing a cathinone with speed, ecstasy and cocaine, and all of them act through catecholamine excess. [f:gresnigt2026] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Signs to recognise: seizures, chest pain and palpitations; for hyperthermia the UBC guidelines say to take the person into the open air and cool them. [f:machacek2026] [f:gresnigt2026] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
