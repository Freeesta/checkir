## effects

Tryptamine hallucinogens cause profound changes in sensory perception, mood and thought and act primarily as agonists of the 5-HT2A receptor. [f:araujo2015]

In healthy volunteers the pharmacokinetics of LSD and psilocybin were dose-proportional, while oral and intravenous DMT differed in ways that may be clinically significant. [f:hampsey2026]

## risks

Classical hallucinogens are relatively well tolerated physiologically and are not considered drugs of dependence, but they carry unique psychological risks: the most likely is overwhelming distress (a 'bad trip') that can lead to dangerous behaviour, and prolonged psychoses are less common. [f:johnson2008]

Hallucinogen persisting perception disorder (HPPD) is rarely encountered clinically; it takes two forms, brief flashbacks and a chronic form lasting months to years. [f:halpern2018]

Intoxications and deaths with the newer tryptamines have been reported, and the lack of toxicological data hampers the assessment of their harm. [f:araujo2015]

## interactions

Among 62 online reports of lithium taken with classic psychedelics such as LSD or psilocybin, 47% involved seizures; none of 34 reports with lamotrigine did. These are self-reports, not controlled data. [f:nayak2021]

DMT by mouth is active only with an MAO inhibitor, as in ayahuasca; 5-MeO-DMT is often used with MAO-A inhibitors such as harmaline, which raise its exposure and may cause serotonin toxicity. [f:riba2003] [f:shen2010b]

## counselling

Ask about state of mind, health and company: the most likely risk of classical hallucinogens is overwhelming distress, and the UBC guidelines tie it to set and setting and advise against using alone. [f:johnson2008] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about medicines: lithium, and MAO inhibitors for DMT and 5-MeO-DMT, are the combinations with documented signals of serious effects. [f:nayak2021] [f:shen2010b]

Ask about personal or family history of psychotic disorders: research with these substances excludes people with such a history, and the UBC guidelines warn that latent disorders can surface even after a single use. [f:johnson2008] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
