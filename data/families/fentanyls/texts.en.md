## effects

Fentanyl, its analogues and other novel synthetic opioids are full agonists of varying potencies at the μ-opioid receptor, with the typical clinical effects of miosis and respiratory and central nervous system depression. [f:armenian2018]

Effects reported for the novel synthetic opioids also include sedation, hypothermia and inhibition of gastrointestinal propulsion. [f:lovrecic2019]

## risks

Potency varies widely between analogues: acetylfentanyl is 5–15 times more potent than heroin and carfentanil about 10,000 times more potent than morphine, 100 times more than fentanyl. [f:lovrecic2019]

In poisonings with these compounds larger naloxone doses are needed than in a classic heroin overdose. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:armenian2018]

As soon as a compound is regulated new ones appear, which makes detection difficult; some illicit pills with these compounds look like known opioids or benzodiazepines. [f:armenian2018]

## interactions

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives mixed with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]

## counselling

If the result points to a fentanyl analogue, explain that its potency differs from that of fentanyl (acetylfentanyl 5–15 times heroin, carfentanil about 100 times fentanyl) and that no usual amount of another opioid applies. [f:lovrecic2019] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Talk about naloxone: larger doses than for heroin may be needed, and emergency services should be called. [f:armenian2018] [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about benzodiazepines and other sedatives: mixed with opioids they are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
