## effects

The 2C drugs are 2,5-dimethoxy-substituted phenethylamines that act on 5-HT2 and alpha-adrenergic receptors; reported effects combine hallucinogenic and stimulant ones and depend on the dose. [f:dean2013]

In cell assays the 2C compounds 2C-B, 2C-C, 2C-D, 2C-E, 2C-H and 2C-I interacted with the 5-HT2A, 5-HT2B and 5-HT2C receptors and with rat TAAR1, mostly with Ki or EC50 below 1 μM. [f:rickli2015]

## risks

Intoxication with 2C drugs presents with hallucinations, agitation, aggression, dysphoria, hypertension, tachycardia, seizures and hyperthermia; the deaths attributed to 2C drugs showed an excited delirium picture. [f:dean2013]

Patients may show a sympathomimetic picture, serotonin toxicity or both; at least five deaths attributed to 2C drugs have been reported, and there is no antidote: treatment is supportive, with urgent action for excited delirium, hyperthermia and seizures. [f:dean2013]

## interactions

MAO-A and MAO-B are the major enzymes of deamination of the 2C compounds studied, so monoamine oxidase inhibitors can raise their concentrations and the risk of toxicity. [f:theobald2007] [f:dean2013]

## counselling

Hallucinogenic effects depend on set and setting: ask where and with whom it will be taken, whether the person is well and has slept, and suggest not taking it alone; at moderate doses hallucinations appear and at higher ones they can be unpleasant. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask which 2C compound the person expects and where it was bought: a product sold as 2C-I was 2C-E in ten patients (one died), and the UBC guidelines note that the compounds of the 2C-x group differ in effects and doses. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Explain which signs need urgent medical attention: severe agitation or aggression, hyperthermia, seizures and delirium, the picture seen in the deaths attributed to 2C drugs; there is no antidote and treatment is supportive. [f:dean2013] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]

Ask about MAO inhibitors and other medicines: deamination goes through the MAOs, and the UBC guidelines, written for 2C-B, advise against mixing it with alcohol, medicines and MDMA. [f:theobald2007] [f:ubc-counselling-guidelines]
