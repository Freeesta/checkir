## effects

Nitazenes are potent synthetic opioids, developed in the 1950s and abandoned because of their high toxicity and unfavourable therapeutic index. [f:stangeland2025]

Their potency varies widely; in experimental studies receptor affinity and potency often exceed those of morphine and fentanyl. [f:stangeland2025]

## risks

In real-world data potency is often lower than the experimental one, and post-mortem concentrations of many nitazenes are similar to those of fentanyl; human data are scarce and the pharmacokinetics are unknown. [f:stangeland2025]

Post-mortem concentrations are highly variable and overlap with those of patients, so lethal thresholds cannot be defined. [f:stangeland2025]

In the reviewed cases other opioids and sedatives were always present; in some cases only metabolites were found, suggesting their own activity or other routes of use. [f:stangeland2025]

## interactions

In poisoning cases several opioids and other sedatives were present, and polysubstance use complicates diagnosis and management. [f:stangeland2025]

Benzodiazepines and other sedatives taken with opioids are a risk factor for overdose. [f:kerensky2017]

## counselling

Explain that naloxone is effective in nitazene poisoning too (median parenteral dose 1.20 mg), but some cases needed repeated doses or a prolonged infusion and longer observation. [f:stangeland2025]

Explain that experimental potency exceeds that of morphine and fentanyl while human data are scarce: there is no reference amount. [f:stangeland2025]

Ask about benzodiazepines and other sedatives: in nitazene poisonings other sedatives were always present and, with opioids, they are a risk factor for overdose. [f:stangeland2025] [f:kerensky2017]
